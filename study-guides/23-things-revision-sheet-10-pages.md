# *23 Things They Don't Tell You About Capitalism*  
## 10-Page High-Density Revision Sheet

## How to use this sheet
Use this version for fast revision after reading the longer digest. Each Thing is reduced to five exam-focused items:
- memory hook
- mainstream/free-market claim
- Chang's counterargument
- strongest example
- chapter links / essay use

## The book in one paragraph
Chang is not against capitalism in general. He is against the free-market claim that capitalism works best when markets are treated as natural, self-correcting, and morally authoritative. His answer is that markets are rule-made, firms are political institutions, wages are not pure merit signals, development needs strategic state action, welfare can support dynamism, finance has become too powerful, and mainstream economics is too narrow to explain or govern capitalism well.

## Thing 1. There is no such thing as a free market
- **Memory hook:** Every market sits inside rules.
- **Mainstream claim:** Regulation distorts naturally efficient exchange.
- **Chang's reply:** All markets are already regulated; the real issue is which rules are visible and whose interests they serve.
- **Best example:** Child-labour regulation once looked like an attack on freedom; now it looks like a moral minimum.
- **Use in essays:** Start here whenever discussing trade, labour, finance, or welfare. This chapter turns "market versus government" into "which institutional design?"
- **Links:** 3, 7, 8, 16, 22.

## Thing 2. Companies should not be run in the interest of their owners
- **Memory hook:** Share price is not corporate health.
- **Mainstream claim:** Shareholders are owners and bear residual risk, so managers should maximize shareholder value.
- **Chang's reply:** Modern shareholders are often the most short-term and least committed stakeholders. Shareholder value can weaken investment and long-run firm capability.
- **Best example:** Buybacks and high payouts in the shareholder-value era alongside weaker long-run growth.
- **Use in essays:** Good for questions on corporate governance, short-termism, and inequality.
- **Links:** 13, 14, 18, 22.

## Thing 3. Most people in rich countries are paid more than they should be
- **Memory hook:** High wages often reflect borders, not just merit.
- **Mainstream claim:** Wages reflect marginal productivity.
- **Chang's reply:** Wages depend on collective productivity, institutions, and immigration controls as much as on individual effort.
- **Best example:** Rich-country and poor-country bus drivers do not differ enough in personal skill to explain massive wage gaps.
- **Use in essays:** Strong for merit, migration, inequality, and global justice questions.
- **Links:** 1, 10, 15, 20.

## Thing 4. The washing machine has changed the world more than the internet has
- **Memory hook:** Old technology can matter more than flashy technology.
- **Mainstream claim:** The internet revolution proves the superiority of modern flexible capitalism.
- **Chang's reply:** Household technology transformed daily life, women's labour, and social organization more deeply than the internet had by 2010.
- **Best example:** Washing machines reduced unpaid domestic labour and helped shift women into paid work.
- **Use in essays:** Use this chapter to critique technological hype and post-industrial rhetoric.
- **Links:** 9, 17, 22.

## Thing 5. Assume the worst about people and you get the worst
- **Memory hook:** Distrust can create the selfishness it expects.
- **Mainstream claim:** Institutions should assume selfish actors and align incentives accordingly.
- **Chang's reply:** Firms and states depend on trust, duty, and discretionary cooperation; pure opportunism would make real organization impossible.
- **Best example:** Work-to-rule shows how much firms rely on effort beyond the written contract.
- **Use in essays:** Important for corporate governance, public service, and institutional design.
- **Links:** 2, 12, 16, 19, 23.

## Thing 6. Greater macroeconomic stability has not made the world economy more stable
- **Memory hook:** Low inflation is not real stability.
- **Mainstream claim:** Price stability creates healthy growth.
- **Chang's reply:** Low inflation can coexist with financial crisis, labour insecurity, and weak growth. Stability must be judged more broadly.
- **Best example:** The so-called Great Moderation ended in the 2008 crash.
- **Use in essays:** Useful for macro policy, central banking, and crisis analysis.
- **Links:** 13, 21, 22, 23.

## Thing 7. Free-market policies rarely make poor countries rich
- **Memory hook:** Rich countries did not follow the rules they now preach.
- **Mainstream claim:** Liberalization, free trade, and privatization create development.
- **Chang's reply:** Rich countries usually developed through protection, industrial policy, and strategic state action; many poor countries grew less after neoliberal reform.
- **Best example:** The US and Britain used intervention while catching up.
- **Use in essays:** Core chapter for development strategy, infant industry, and historical method.
- **Links:** 9, 11, 12, 23.

## Thing 8. Capital has a nationality
- **Memory hook:** Global firms still have home bases.
- **Mainstream claim:** Ownership nationality matters little in a globalized world.
- **Chang's reply:** Core functions such as strategy, R&D, and commitment remain nationally embedded; ownership shapes where value and responsibility stay.
- **Best example:** Multinationals keep high-value functions near home and behave differently in crises.
- **Use in essays:** Excellent for FDI, national development, and globalization debates.
- **Links:** 7, 18, 22.

## Thing 9. We do not live in a post-industrial age
- **Memory hook:** Services rise, but industry still carries the system.
- **Mainstream claim:** Rich countries naturally move beyond manufacturing and poor countries can leap straight to services.
- **Chang's reply:** Manufacturing still matters for productivity, exports, innovation, and tradability. Service growth does not make industry strategically irrelevant.
- **Best example:** Manufacturing can shrink in share because productivity rises faster and prices fall relative to services.
- **Use in essays:** Key for deindustrialization, productivity, and development structure.
- **Links:** 4, 17, 18, 22.

## Thing 10. The US does not have the highest living standard in the world
- **Memory hook:** Average income is not the same as a good life.
- **Mainstream claim:** US GDP and PPP rankings prove the superiority of the American model.
- **Chang's reply:** Living standards depend on distribution, hours, health, public services, and median outcomes - not just GDP per capita.
- **Best example:** Cheap services may reflect low wages and insecurity rather than superior social efficiency.
- **Use in essays:** Good for comparing capitalism models and questioning GDP triumphalism.
- **Links:** 3, 13, 20, 21.

## Thing 11. Africa is not destined for underdevelopment
- **Memory hook:** Structure is constraint, not destiny.
- **Mainstream claim:** Africa remains poor because of geography, culture, or permanent institutional weakness.
- **Chang's reply:** Africa's stagnation is historically produced and partly policy-driven; structural burdens matter but do not amount to fate.
- **Best example:** Earlier growth before structural adjustment undermines permanent-failure stories.
- **Use in essays:** Strong for development, structural adjustment, and anti-determinist arguments.
- **Links:** 7, 12, 23.

## Thing 12. Governments can pick winners
- **Memory hook:** States do not need perfect foresight to guide development.
- **Mainstream claim:** Officials lack information and pick only losers.
- **Chang's reply:** Governments can coordinate learning, direct resources, and support strategic sectors; private actors also make major errors.
- **Best example:** POSCO in South Korea as state-backed industrial success.
- **Use in essays:** Essential for industrial policy and developmental-state essays.
- **Links:** 7, 11, 19, 23.

## Thing 13. Making rich people richer doesn't make the rest of us richer
- **Memory hook:** Trickle-down is a promise, not a law.
- **Mainstream claim:** Rewarding the rich increases saving, investment, and growth.
- **Chang's reply:** Since the 1980s, top enrichment often raised inequality without delivering stronger broad growth or investment.
- **Best example:** Postwar high-growth decades coexisted with stronger taxation and lower top concentration than the neoliberal era.
- **Use in essays:** Ideal for inequality, demand, political economy, and tax debates.
- **Links:** 2, 14, 20, 21, 22.

## Thing 14. US managers are over-priced
- **Memory hook:** Top pay often reflects power, not productivity.
- **Mainstream claim:** Huge CEO packages reflect scarce talent in very large firms.
- **Chang's reply:** Historical, international, and performance evidence all suggest US executive pay is institutionally inflated.
- **Best example:** CEO-to-worker pay ratios exploded without comparable proof of managerial genius.
- **Use in essays:** Useful for corporate governance, meritocracy, and rent extraction.
- **Links:** 2, 13, 18, 22.

## Thing 15. People in poor countries are more entrepreneurial than people in rich countries
- **Memory hook:** Poverty is not caused by lack of enterprise.
- **Mainstream claim:** Poor countries need more entrepreneurial culture and microcredit.
- **Chang's reply:** Poor societies already have high self-employment; what they lack is productive structure, technology, and large-scale organization.
- **Best example:** High self-employment in poor countries versus rich countries with more salaried work.
- **Use in essays:** Strong for microfinance, development, and structure-versus-culture debates.
- **Links:** 3, 7, 12, 17.

## Thing 16. We are not smart enough to leave things to the market
- **Memory hook:** Complexity can exceed human judgment.
- **Mainstream claim:** Decentralized actors know best, so less regulation is epistemically humble.
- **Chang's reply:** Bounded rationality means markets can become too complex to navigate safely; regulation often simplifies choices and limits systemic harm.
- **Best example:** LTCM and financial experts badly misjudging systemic risk.
- **Use in essays:** Central for regulation, behavioural critique, and finance.
- **Links:** 5, 19, 22, 23.

## Thing 17. More education in itself is not going to make a country richer
- **Memory hook:** More schooling is not the same as development.
- **Mainstream claim:** In the knowledge economy, education is the main engine of growth.
- **Chang's reply:** Education matters, but prosperity also requires firms, sectors, and institutions able to use skills productively.
- **Best example:** High educational expansion without corresponding growth in some countries.
- **Use in essays:** Good for human capital debates and development policy.
- **Links:** 9, 15, 20, 23.

## Thing 18. What is good for General Motors is not necessarily good for the United States
- **Memory hook:** Corporate gain is not automatically public gain.
- **Mainstream claim:** Helping business helps the country.
- **Chang's reply:** Firms may underinvest, externalize costs, financialize, or lobby for private advantage at public expense.
- **Best example:** GM's reliance on financial profits while industrial strength eroded.
- **Use in essays:** Strong for corporate power, regulation, and national interest.
- **Links:** 2, 8, 14, 22.

## Thing 19. Despite the fall of communism, we are still living in planned economies
- **Memory hook:** Planning never disappeared.
- **Mainstream claim:** Soviet collapse proved planning failed and markets won.
- **Chang's reply:** Capitalist economies still depend on state planning, public coordination, and large-firm internal planning.
- **Best example:** Herbert Simon's image of a Martian seeing organizations everywhere rather than pure markets.
- **Use in essays:** Important for planning, state capacity, and institutional realism.
- **Links:** 12, 16, 21, 23.

## Thing 20. Equality of opportunity may not be fair
- **Memory hook:** Formal equality without capability is hollow.
- **Mainstream claim:** Non-discrimination plus open competition is enough; equality of outcome is harmful.
- **Chang's reply:** Material conditions shape whether people can use opportunities, so some equalization of outcome is needed to make opportunity real.
- **Best example:** Post-apartheid South Africa with formal rights but persistent structural disadvantage.
- **Use in essays:** Excellent for meritocracy, capability, social mobility, and justice.
- **Links:** 3, 13, 17, 21.

## Thing 21. Big government makes people more open to change
- **Memory hook:** Security can make people more flexible.
- **Mainstream claim:** Welfare makes people lazy and slows restructuring.
- **Chang's reply:** Social insurance reduces fear of ruin and can therefore increase occupational mobility, retraining, and acceptance of change.
- **Best example:** Korean students crowding into medicine because insecure labour markets make riskier careers unattractive.
- **Use in essays:** Core for welfare-state debates, flexibility, and labour-market reform.
- **Links:** 6, 13, 20, 22.

## Thing 22. Financial markets need to become less, not more, efficient
- **Memory hook:** Faster finance can mean worse capitalism.
- **Mainstream claim:** Deep, liquid finance allocates capital efficiently and supports growth.
- **Chang's reply:** High-speed, short-term finance increases fragility, encourages short-termism, and detaches capital from production.
- **Best example:** Iceland's finance-led boom and crash.
- **Use in essays:** Essential for crisis, regulation, and financialization.
- **Links:** 2, 6, 18, 23.

## Thing 23. Good economic policy does not require good economists
- **Memory hook:** One school of economics does not own truth.
- **Mainstream claim:** Good policy requires economists trained in market orthodoxy and simple free-market rules.
- **Chang's reply:** Successful developmental states often relied on broader intellectual traditions; mainstream economists also helped justify the policies that led to crisis.
- **Best example:** East Asian policy elites with legal or engineering rather than orthodox economic backgrounds; the Queen's question after 2008.
- **Use in essays:** Best final chapter for discussing expertise, pluralism, and the politics of economic knowledge.
- **Links:** almost all chapters, especially 7, 12, 16, 22.

## Major recurring themes
- Markets are political institutions.
- Productive capability matters more than abstract exchange.
- Rich countries used intervention while catching up.
- Manufacturing remains central.
- Inequality is economically damaging as well as morally troubling.
- Welfare can support flexibility.
- Finance should serve production.
- Economics should be plural, historical, and institutionally grounded.

## Chang's overall view of capitalism
- Capitalism is still the best broad system available.
- But free-market capitalism is a poor version of it.
- Good capitalism is regulated, developmental, socially protected, and financially disciplined.

## Final revision formula
