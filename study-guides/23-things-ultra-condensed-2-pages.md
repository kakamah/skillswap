# *23 Things They Don't Tell You About Capitalism*  
## 2-Page Ultra-Condensed Exam Sheet

## The whole book in one sentence
Chang argues that capitalism can work, but only when markets are treated as politically designed tools inside social institutions rather than as natural and morally authoritative judges of value.

## The 23 Things in ultra-condensed form
1. **No free market** - all markets are rule-made; "free" usually means support for existing rules.  
2. **Not just for owners** - shareholder value encourages short-termism and underinvestment.  
3. **Rich-country wages are political** - pay reflects systems and borders, not just merit.  
4. **Washing machine > internet** - old domestic technology transformed labour and society more deeply than recent hype suggests.  
5. **Assume selfishness, get selfishness** - firms and states depend on trust and mixed motives.  
6. **Low inflation ≠ stability** - the neoliberal era had financial crises and insecurity despite price discipline.  
7. **Free-market development story is false** - rich countries used protection and industrial policy while catching up.  
8. **Capital has a nationality** - ownership still shapes where strategy, R&D, and loyalty sit.  
9. **Not post-industrial** - manufacturing still drives productivity, exports, and innovation.  
10. **US not obviously #1 in living standards** - GDP averages hide inequality, long hours, and weaker social outcomes.  
11. **Africa is not doomed** - underdevelopment is historically and politically produced, not fated.  
12. **Governments can pick winners** - industrial policy can work if institutions support learning and discipline.  
13. **Trickle-down is weak** - enriching the rich does not reliably enrich everyone else.  
14. **US managers are over-priced** - executive pay reflects power and governance, not pure productivity.  
15. **Poor countries are highly entrepreneurial** - the problem is low-productivity structure, not lack of hustle.  
16. **We are not smart enough for pure markets** - bounded rationality makes regulation necessary in complex systems.  
17. **More education alone won't make a country rich** - skills need firms, sectors, and productive demand.  
18. **What helps GM may not help America** - private profitability and national development can diverge.  
19. **We still live in planned economies** - firms and states plan constantly; capitalism is not pure market coordination.  
20. **Equality of opportunity may still be unfair** - opportunity requires minimum capability and some outcome support.  
21. **Big government can support change** - welfare makes risk-taking and restructuring more bearable.  
22. **Finance should be less efficient** - high-speed liquidity and innovation can destabilize capitalism.  
23. **Good policy does not require orthodox economists** - plural economics is better than one free-market school.  

## 10 strongest examples to cite fast
- Child-labour regulation -> shows markets are rule-made.  
- Bus drivers in rich vs poor countries -> wages are system-based.  
- Washing machine -> social transformation beats technological hype.  
- Historical US tariffs -> rich countries used intervention.  
- POSCO -> industrial policy can succeed.  
- Cappuccino society (South Africa) -> formal opportunity without capability is hollow.  
- Korean medicine rush -> insecurity misallocates talent.  
- Iceland crash -> finance-led growth is fragile.

- Herbert Simon's Martian -> capitalist economies are full of planning.

## Chang versus free-market orthodoxy: final contrast
- **Orthodoxy:** markets are natural, prices reflect merit, finance allocates efficiently, welfare weakens incentives, education solves development, and globalization makes national strategy obsolete.  
- **Chang:** markets are institutional, prices reflect power and structure, finance often destabilizes, welfare can raise adaptability, education needs productive structure, and national development strategy still matters.  

## 5 exam-ready thesis lines
1. **Chang's core move is to denaturalize the market: economic outcomes are politically organized before they are priced.**  
2. **For Chang, capitalism fails when short-term finance and shareholder value overpower long-term production and capability building.**  
3. **Development is not created by passive openness but by strategic structural transformation.**  
4. **A fair and dynamic economy requires social protection, because insecurity can reduce rather than increase productive flexibility.**  
5. **The book is not anti-capitalist; it is anti-free-market ideology and pro-institutional, plural, developmental capitalism.**
