# 06 Turning Points

Source basis: uploaded markdown extraction of Cambridge International AS Level History: International History, 1870-1945. Page references are only included where the extraction made them reasonably clear. Any OCR damage, internal inconsistency, or weakly supported detail is flagged rather than normalised.


## 1. German unification (1871)
- **What changed:** Europe gained a powerful new German state.
- **Why the change mattered:** France became a security-seeking power with a grievance; Bismarck's diplomacy became central.
- **What came before:** Fragmented German states and a less concentrated balance of power.
- **What came after:** Intensified nationalism and a new continental power structure.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** It is the structural backdrop for later rivalry, not just a single military result.

## 2. British occupation of Egypt (1882)
- **What changed:** Britain turned strategic interest into direct control over a key route.
- **Why the change mattered:** It linked empire, route security, and African partition more tightly.
- **What came before:** Commercial / strategic interest without the same level of direct control.
- **What came after:** Stronger scramble logic in Africa.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** The chapter explicitly treats Egypt as one of the key triggers of wider imperial acceleration.

## 3. Berlin Conference / Treaty of Berlin (1884-85)
- **What changed:** Imperial competition gained agreed rules.
- **Why the change mattered:** It made expansion faster and more systematic.
- **What came before:** Rival claims without an accepted framework.
- **What came after:** Rapid territorial acquisition by European powers.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** It institutionalised the scramble rather than merely participating in it.

## 4. Triple Intervention (1895)
- **What changed:** Japan discovered that battlefield victory did not guarantee diplomatic security.
- **Why the change mattered:** Humiliation sharpened Japanese expansionism and anti-Russian hostility.
- **What came before:** Japanese victory over China.
- **What came after:** Japanese military and diplomatic drive toward 1904-05.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** It explains why the Russo-Japanese War had such strong emotional and strategic roots.

## 5. US Spanish-American War victory (1898)
- **What changed:** The USA became an overseas imperial power.
- **Why the change mattered:** US power now shaped Pacific and Caribbean politics directly.
- **What came before:** Mainly continental expansion.
- **What came after:** Treaty of Paris, Cuba, Philippines, Panama, stronger global role.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** It marks the decisive outward turn in the USA story.

## 6. Russo-Japanese War (1904-05)
- **What changed:** A non-European power defeated a European great power.
- **Why the change mattered:** Japan gained status and the hierarchy of power looked less purely western.
- **What came before:** Japanese rise was visible but still doubted.
- **What came after:** Portsmouth, stronger Japanese diplomacy, wider respect and fear.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** It is the clearest proof-point of Japan's emergence.

## 7. USA enters the First World War (1917)
- **What changed:** US economic power became direct military and diplomatic weight.
- **Why the change mattered:** The outcome of the war and the language of peace were shaped by the USA.
- **What came before:** Profitable but formally neutral involvement.
- **What came after:** Fourteen Points and greater US leverage.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** It altered both victory and peace-making.

## 8. Paris Peace Conference / Versailles (1919)
- **What changed:** War ended with a new order of treaties, borders, and institutions.
- **Why the change mattered:** The whole interwar system rested on these decisions.
- **What came before:** Allied wartime diplomacy and Wilsonian expectations.
- **What came after:** League system, reparations disputes, revisionism, minority crises.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** No other interwar development can be understood without it.

## 9. Ruhr crisis and Dawes shift (1923-24)
- **What changed:** Coercive reparations enforcement gave way to financial stabilisation.
- **Why the change mattered:** It created the basis of the apparent mid-1920s recovery.
- **What came before:** Harsh enforcement and hyperinflation.
- **What came after:** Dawes, Locarno, League entry for Germany.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** It shows exactly where the 1920s order changed from confrontation to managed compromise.

## 10. Wall Street Crash (1929)
- **What changed:** The financial base of interwar stability collapsed.
- **Why the change mattered:** Extremism gained strength while cooperative diplomacy weakened.
- **What came before:** Dawes / Young-era confidence.
- **What came after:** Depression, Hitler, stronger militarism.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** It turned weakness into systemic crisis.

## 11. Manchurian Crisis (1931-33)
- **What changed:** A major aggressor openly defied collective security and kept its gains.
- **Why the change mattered:** It damaged faith in the League and encouraged future risk-taking.
- **What came before:** Washington-system restraint and 1920s diplomacy.
- **What came after:** League failure narrative, Japanese military dominance, harsher 1930s climate.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** It is the first decisive international breakdown of the 1930s.

## 12. Hitler takes power (1933)
- **What changed:** German revisionism became tied to a radical dictatorship.
- **Why the change mattered:** Treaty revision, rearmament, and expansion gained ideological drive and speed.
- **What came before:** Weimar revisionism under Stresemann.
- **What came after:** League exit, rearmament, Rhineland, Munich, war.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** It transformed the character of the German question.

## 13. Abyssinia (1935-36)
- **What changed:** The League failed publicly against Italy.
- **Why the change mattered:** Collective security lost practical credibility.
- **What came before:** Residual hope that sanctions and procedure could still work.
- **What came after:** Italy drifted toward Germany; League deterrence collapsed.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** It destroyed the moral and strategic authority of the League at the exact moment it was most needed.

## 14. Rhineland (1936)
- **What changed:** Germany altered the western strategic balance at low risk.
- **Why the change mattered:** Hitler learned that the western powers would not fight over treaty enforcement.
- **What came before:** Open rearmament and diplomatic testing.
- **What came after:** Anschluss, Sudeten crisis, tougher German diplomacy.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** It was both reversible in theory and decisive in practice.

## 15. Xi'an Incident (1936)
- **What changed:** Chiang was pushed toward anti-Japanese cooperation.
- **Why the change mattered:** Chinese politics shifted away from exclusive anti-communist priority on the eve of wider war.
- **What came before:** KMT campaigns against the CCP and limited anti-Japanese response.
- **What came after:** Second United Front, stronger anti-Japanese framing.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** It changes how the 1937 war should be understood inside China.

## 16. Munich (1938)
- **What changed:** Britain and France sacrificed Czech security for temporary peace.
- **Why the change mattered:** Germany gained strategically while deterrence weakened further.
- **What came before:** Anschluss and Sudeten agitation.
- **What came after:** March 1939 occupation and the collapse of confidence in appeasement.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** It was the high point of appeasement and the clearest case of short-term peace bought at long-term cost.

## 17. Occupation of Czechoslovakia (March 1939)
- **What changed:** Hitler openly exceeded the self-determination argument.
- **Why the change mattered:** British and French views shifted from limited revision to broader aggression.
- **What came before:** Munich and claims to protect Germans.
- **What came after:** Poland guarantee, harder diplomacy, still failed deterrence.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** It changed the interpretation of Hitler's aims.

## 18. Nazi-Soviet Pact (August 1939)
- **What changed:** Germany secured freedom from immediate eastern war.
- **Why the change mattered:** Poland became strategically exposed.
- **What came before:** Failed anti-German coalition building.
- **What came after:** Invasion of Poland and general war.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** It is the decisive diplomatic precondition for the September crisis.

## 19. Pearl Harbor (1941)
- **What changed:** The USA entered the war directly against Japan; Germany declared war on the USA.
- **Why the change mattered:** European and Asian conflicts fused into full global war.
- **What came before:** Japanese expansion and US sanctions pressure.
- **What came after:** Totalised global conflict and the endgame of Japanese empire.
- **Why this matters more than surrounding events:** It is the clearest link-point between the Asia chapter and the wider international story.
