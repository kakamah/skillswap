# Treaty / Settlement / Agreement Matrix — Cambridge AS Level International History (1870–1945)

Dense revision notes: each entry uses the same field headings. Dates are given as in standard textbooks; some instruments are **declarations** or **plans** rather than bilateral “treaties.”

---

## 1. Treaty (General Act) of Berlin — 1885

**Name:** General Act of the Berlin Conference (often taught as the **Treaty / settlement of Berlin, 1885**).

**Date:** Signed **26 February–1 March 1885** (conference ran **15 Nov 1884 – 26 Feb 1885**).

**Parties involved:** **14 European states + USA** (including Britain, France, Germany, Belgium, Portugal, etc.); **no African rulers** as equal negotiators.

**Main terms:** “Effective occupation” and **free trade** on the **Congo** and **Niger** basins (in principle); rules for **future annexation** in Africa; recognition of **Leopold II’s** personal rule in the **Congo Free State** (as a neutral “international” territory on paper).

**Territorial terms:** Did **not** partition Africa in one stroke; it set **procedures** and **recognition** principles. **Congo basin** opened to commercial access; **spheres of influence** followed from subsequent bilateral deals, not this act alone.

**Military terms:** None as enforcement machinery; **“effective occupation”** encouraged **conquest/administration** on the ground.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** **Free navigation** on key rivers; suppression of **slave trading** (stated aim); **most-favoured-nation** style commercial clauses in defined zones; **international commission** for the Congo.

**Who benefited:** **Belgium’s king** (Leopold) gained diplomatic cover; **European imperial powers** gained a “legal” framework for **scramble** diplomacy; **trading nations** rhetorically favoured open access.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **African societies** (excluded); later critics of **atrocities** in the Congo; smaller traders often found “free trade” **illusory** where one power dominated locally.

**Why it mattered later:** Codified the **diplomatic logic** of the **Scramble for Africa**; linked to **rail/river/telegraph** imperial competition and **World War I** African campaigns.

**What students often confuse about it:** Thinking it **drew the map of Africa** in 1885; confusing it with **Congress of Berlin (1878)** on the Balkans; assuming **African consent**.

## 2. Treaty of Shimonoseki — 17 April 1895

**Name:** Treaty of Shimonoseki (First Sino-Japanese War settlement).

**Date:** **17 April 1895**.

**Parties involved:** **Qing China** and **Japan**.

**Main terms:** Ended the **First Sino-Japanese War**; marked Japan as a **major imperial power** in East Asia.

**Territorial terms:** **Taiwan (Formosa)**, the **Pescadores (Penghu)**, and the **Liaodong Peninsula** ceded to Japan — **Liaodong** later reversed by the **Triple Intervention** (Russia, France, Germany pressure).

**Military terms:** Ceasefire and withdrawal arrangements; reflected **Japanese military victory** at sea and on land (e.g. **Port Arthur** context).

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** Heavy **indemnity** (200 million **taels**); opening of **treaty ports**; **Japanese factories** permitted in Chinese treaty ports; **most-favoured-nation** style gains for Japan’s commercial position.

**Who benefited:** **Japan** (territory, money, prestige); **Western powers** could piggyback extraterritorial/commercial rights.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Chinese nationalists/reformers**; **Russia** (wanted Manchurian leverage — hence intervention).

**Why it mattered later:** Fuelled **Chinese reform** and **revolutionary** pressures; **Russo-Japanese rivalry** intensified; **Korea’s** fate remained contested → contributes to **1904–05** war.

**What students often confuse about it:** Which bits stayed Japanese after **Triple Intervention**; mixing it up with **Treaty of Portsmouth (1905)** or **Twenty-One Demands (1915)**.

## 3. Treaty of Vereeniging — 31 May 1902

**Name:** Treaty of Vereeniging (Peace of Vereeniging).

**Date:** **31 May 1902**.

**Parties involved:** **British Empire** and **Boer republics** (**Transvaal** and **Orange Free State**).

**Main terms:** Ended the **South African (Boer) War**; Boer republics **surrendered** independence.

**Territorial terms:** **Transvaal** and **Orange Free State** became **British colonies** (later **Union of South Africa**).

**Military terms:** Boers **laid down arms**; amnesty subject to conditions; **British military supremacy** acknowledged.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** Promise of **£3,000,000** “reconstruction” aid; **Uitlander** franchise issues folded into future **self-government** trajectory; **Dutch** language rights in schools/courts recognised (wording varies by source, but “language/culture” protections are standard textbook points).

**Who benefited:** **British imperial** strategists seeking **Cape–Cairo** logic and **Rand** gold security; **moderate Boer leaders** who could negotiate **future dominance** within empire.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Bittereinders**; many **Afrikaners** long-term; **Black African** majorities **excluded** from the deal’s “citizenship” framework.

**Why it mattered later:** Set path to **Union (1910)** and **Afrikaner–British** power-sharing that **entrenched white rule**; shaped **Commonwealth** and **WWI** South African participation.

**What students often confuse about it:** Thinking it granted **immediate** self-rule; confusing **concentration camps** timeline with **treaty clauses**; treating it as anti-colonial “equality.”

## 4. Anglo-Japanese Alliance — 30 January 1902 (renewed)

**Name:** Anglo-Japanese Alliance.

**Date:** **30 January 1902** (renewed/expanded **1905**, **1911**).

**Parties involved:** **United Kingdom** and **Japan**.

**Main terms:** Recognised each other’s **special interests** — UK in **China** (not fully uniform across all clauses by revision), Japan in **Korea** and **China**; **neutrality** if either fought a single third power; **military support** if either fought **two powers** (wording evolved by revision).

**Territorial terms:** Not a land-transfer treaty; it **sanctioned Japanese ascendancy** in **Korea** diplomatically (especially after **1905** revision).

**Military terms:** **Strategic naval** implications in **East Asia**; deterrence against **Russian** expansion (pre-1905).

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** Strengthened Japan’s **great-power** status; eased UK **overstretch** (“splendid isolation” ended in practice in the Far East).

**Who benefited:** **Japan** and **Britain** strategically.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Russia**; **USA** grew wary later; **China/Korea** as objects of great-power deals.

**Why it mattered later:** Context for **Russo-Japanese War** diplomacy; **WWI**: Japan entered on **Allied** side (1914); alliance **lapsed** (1923) → affects **Washington Conference** dynamics.

**What students often confuse about it:** Exact **casus foederis** (one enemy vs two); thinking it was **anti-German** in 1902 (primary driver was **Russia/Far East**).

## 5. Entente Cordiale — 8 April 1904

**Name:** Entente Cordiale.

**Date:** **8 April 1904**.

**Parties involved:** **United Kingdom** and **France**.

**Main terms:** Settled **imperial disputes** (not a formal military alliance at first); improved diplomatic trust → step toward **Triple Entente** with **Russia (1907)**.

**Territorial terms (colonial “swaps”):** **France** recognised British primacy in **Egypt**; **Britain** recognised French primacy in **Morocco** (with caveats); adjustments over **Newfoundland** fisheries, **Siam**, **West Africa** border issues, etc.

**Military terms:** None mandatory in 1904 itself; became strategically significant as **general staff talks** and **wartime planning** evolved.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** Reduced **Anglo-French** colonial friction; enabled focus on **German** rise.

**Who benefited:** **France** and **Britain** diplomatically; **Moroccan** crisis diplomacy becomes a test case.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Germany** (felt “encircled” — **First Moroccan Crisis 1905**); some domestic critics in both countries.

**Why it mattered later:** Core diplomatic glue of the **Allied** side in **WWI**; shaped **Middle East** and **North Africa** imperial assumptions.

**What students often confuse about it:** Calling it a **military alliance** in 1904; confusing with **Triple Entente** or **NATO-like** guarantees.

---

## 6. Treaty of Portsmouth — 5 September 1905

**Name:** Treaty of Portsmouth.

**Date:** **5 September 1905**.

**Parties involved:** **Russia** and **Japan** (mediated by **USA**, **Roosevelt**).

**Main terms:** Ended **Russo-Japanese War**; Japan confirmed as **regional hegemon**.

**Territorial terms:** Japan gained **lease on Liaodong** (Port Arthur/Dalian area) and **south Sakhalin**; **Korea** fell into Japan’s **sphere** (annexed formally **1910**).

**Military terms:** Ceasefire; Russian **Baltic** fleet defeat and land war exhaustion made terms inevitable.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** **No Russian indemnity** (Japanese public anger); limited transfers; strategic gains substituted for cash.

**Who benefited:** **Japan**; **USA** prestige as mediator.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Japanese public** (wanted more indemnity); **Russian revolutionaries** (1905 Revolution context).

**Why it mattered later:** Encouraged **Japanese expansionism**; showed **US** willingness to broker **balance of power** in Asia; fed **Russian internal crisis**.

**What students often confuse about it:** Thinking **USA** was a party; confusing indemnity outcome with **Shimonoseki**; mixing **south Sakhalin** with later **north** shifts (post-WWII is outside 1945 scope but students blur eras).

## 7. Treaty of Paris — 10 December 1898

**Name:** Treaty of Paris (Spanish-American War).

**Date:** **10 December 1898**.

**Parties involved:** **United States** and **Spain**.

**Main terms:** Spain **lost empire remnants**; USA became a **colonial power** overseas.

**Territorial terms:** **Puerto Rico** and **Guam** ceded to USA; **Philippines** ceded for **$20 million**; **Cuba** “released” from Spanish rule but under US influence (**Platt Amendment** followed).

**Military terms:** Ended hostilities; reflected US naval victory (**Santiago**, **Manila Bay**).

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** Purchase payment for Philippines; opened era of **US protectorates** and **intervention**.

**Who benefited:** **USA** strategically and economically; **Cuban** independence advocates **partially**.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Spanish** nationalists; **Filipino** nationalists (**Aguinaldo**) fighting **Philippine-American War**; **Cubans** wanting full sovereignty.

**Why it mattered later:** Pivot to **US imperialism**; **Open Door** / **Pacific** strategy; long shadows in **Caribbean** interventions.

**What students often confuse about it:** Thinking **Cuba** became a US state; confusing with **Paris 1856** or **Versailles**; forgetting **Filipino** resistance after 1898.

---

## 8. Treaty of Brest-Litovsk — 3 March 1918

**Name:** Treaty of Brest-Litovsk.

**Date:** **3 March 1918** (supplementary agreements followed).

**Parties involved:** **Bolshevik Russia (RSFSR)** and **Central Powers** (chiefly **Germany** and **Austria-Hungary**; **Bulgaria** and **Ottoman Empire** also linked).

**Main terms:** Russia **left WWI**; massive **territorial losses**; exposed **Allied** fears of **German** eastern domination.

**Territorial terms:** **Poland**, **Baltic lands**, **Ukraine** separated/independent under German orientation (as treaty framework); **Finland**; **Transcaucasus** issues complicated — overall: huge **German** sphere.

**Military terms:** **Demobilisation**; Russian army stripped from the **Eastern Front**; freed **German divisions** for **1918 Western offensives**.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** Heavy **economic** leverage for Germany in the east; **Lenin** accepted “breathing space” for **revolution survival**.

**Who benefited:** **Germany** short-term; **Bolsheviks** politically (exit war).

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Russian patriots**; **Allies**; many **Bolsheviks** (Left Communists); **national minorities** caught in chaos.

**Why it mattered later:** Shaped **post-war borders** mentally; **Allied** intervention in **Russian Civil War**; invalidated by **German defeat** but influenced **national self-determination** debates.

**What students often confuse about it:** Thinking it remained valid after **Nov 1918**; confusing **Ukrainian** independence clauses with **Wilsonian** ideals; mixing dates with **Rapallo 1922**.

---

## 9. Treaty of Versailles — 28 June 1919

**Name:** Treaty of Versailles.

**Date:** **28 June 1919**.

**Parties involved:** **Allied and Associated Powers** vs **Germany** (German delegation signed under protest).

**Main terms:** **Peace settlement** for **WWI**; created **League of Nations Covenant**; **War Guilt** framework.

**Territorial terms (textbook detail set):** Germany lost **~70,000 km²** and **~7 million people**. **Alsace-Lorraine → France**. **Eupen-Malmedy → Belgium**. **North Schleswig → Denmark** (plebiscite logic). **Saar** under **League** administration **15 years** (**French** use of **coal mines**). **West Prussia / Posen** area → **Poland** (**Polish Corridor** to **Danzig** as **Free City**). **Memel** → **Lithuania** (later seizure/administration patterns taught as “to Lithuania”). **Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania** independent. **German colonies → League mandates** (UK/France/Belgium/Japan etc.). **Rhineland demilitarised**.

**Military terms (textbook detail set):** Army **100,000**; **no conscription**; **no tanks**, **no military aircraft**, **no submarines**; navy capped (**6 battleships**, etc.); restrictions on **General Staff**.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** **Article 231** (**War Guilt** clause) as basis for reparations; reparations bill famously **£6.6 billion** (fixed **1921**, **London Schedule**); **Danzig**, **plebiscites**, **minority treaties**; **Anschluss forbidden**.

**Who benefited:** **France** (security + coal + Alsace-Lorraine); **Poland**; **Belgium**; **UK** imperially (mandates); **Japan** (Shandong controversy — separate Chinese clauses).

**Who was dissatisfied:** **German** “November criminals” narrative; **USA** Senate opposition; **Italy** (“mutilated victory”); **China** (Shandong).

**Why it mattered later:** Direct road to **Weimar instability**, **Dawes/Young**, **Locarno**, **Hitler’s** diplomatic reversals, **WWII** origins debates.

**What students often confuse about it:** **Article 231** = legal basis, not a single “guilt paragraph” only; confusing **Saar** with **Rhineland**; thinking reparations were paid in full on time; mixing **mandates** with **colonies** returned.

---

## 10. Treaty of Saint-Germain — 10 September 1919

**Name:** Treaty of Saint-Germain-en-Laye.

**Date:** **10 September 1919**.

**Parties involved:** **Allies** vs **Austria** (rump **Republic of Austria** after **Habsburg** collapse).

**Main terms:** Dissolved **Austria-Hungary’s** Austrian core as an imperial state; **Anschluss forbidden**.

**Territorial terms (textbook detail set):** **Bohemia + Moravia → Czechoslovakia**. **Dalmatia + Bosnia-Herzegovina → Yugoslavia**. **Bukovina → Romania**. **Galicia → Poland**. **Trentino, Istria, Trieste, South Tyrol → Italy**.

**Military terms:** Small army limits (often taught ~**30,000** — verify your specification booklet); no heavy weapons/air force in practice similar to other successor treaties.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** **War guilt**-style clauses; reparations burdens; **minorities** issues; economic dislocation in **Vienna**.

**Who benefited:** **Italy** (territory); **Czechoslovakia**; **Yugoslavia**; **Romania**; **Poland**.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Austria** (economically unviable rump); **German Austrians** wanting **Anschluss**; some **South Tyrol** Germans.

**Why it mattered later:** **Anschluss** becomes a live **1930s** issue; **Central European** flashpoints; contributes to **fascism** narratives.

**What students often confuse about it:** Mixing with **Trianon** (Hungary) or **Sèvres** (Ottomans); thinking **Vienna** kept **industrial hinterland**.

## 11. Treaty of Neuilly — 27 November 1919

**Name:** Treaty of Neuilly-sur-Seine.

**Date:** **27 November 1919**.

**Parties involved:** **Allies** vs **Bulgaria**.

**Main terms:** **Balkan** frontier revision punishing **Bulgaria** as a former **Central Power** ally.

**Territorial terms:** Losses to **Greece** (**Western Thrace**), **Yugoslavia**, **Romania** (**Dobruja** adjustments — specify per map in your textbook).

**Military terms (textbook detail set):** Army limited to **20,000**.

**Economic / reparations / political terms (textbook detail set):** Reparations **over $400 million** (gold francs/dollar equivalents vary in sources — use your examiner’s figure).

**Who benefited:** **Greece**, **Yugoslavia**, **Romania**.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Bulgaria** (national humiliation → **revisionism**).

**Why it mattered later:** Bulgarian alignment questions in **interwar** diplomacy; **Balkan** instability; relevance to **WWII** Balkan campaigns.

**What students often confuse about it:** Mixing with **Trianon** or **Sèvres**; overstating Bulgarian gains from **Balkan Wars** vs losses now.

---

## 12. Treaty of Sèvres — 10 August 1920

**Name:** Treaty of Sèvres.

**Date:** **10 August 1920**.

**Parties involved:** **Allies** vs **Ottoman Empire** (sultanate government).

**Main terms:** Harsh **dismemberment** of the Ottoman state; **Kemalist** resistance + **Turkish War of Independence** prevented full implementation.

**Territorial terms (textbook detail set):** Anatolia partitioned on paper: **Greek** zone around **Smyrna (İzmir)**; **Italian** spheres; **Armenia**; **Kurdistan** (often noted as proposed); Arab lands split into **mandates**: **Syria** (**France**); **Palestine** + **Transjordan** + **Mesopotamia (Iraq)** (**Britain**) — *some textbook lines misprint **Iraq** as **Iran**; Iran was **not** a British mandate.*

**Military terms:** Army/navy limits; **Straits** international control proposals.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** **Capitulations**/financial control elements; reparations; **international** commissions.

**Special textbook point:** **Dardanelles permanently open to shipping** (freedom of passage regime in the treaty’s logic).

**Ratification:** **Never properly ratified** in full force; superseded by **Lausanne (1923)**.

**Who benefited (on paper):** **Greece**; **Italy**; **Britain/France** mandates; **Armenian** state project.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Turkish nationalists**; **USA** not taking mandates; **Arabs** wanting full independence.

**Why it mattered later:** **Lausanne** becomes the “real” Turkey settlement; Middle East **mandate** borders echo forward.

**What students often confuse about it:** Treating Sèvres as **final**; confusing **Iran** vs **Iraq** mandate; mixing **Dardanelles** clauses with **Montreux** (later).

---

## 13. Treaty of Trianon — 4 June 1920

**Name:** Treaty of Trianon.

**Date:** **4 June 1920**.

**Parties involved:** **Allies** vs **Hungary**.

**Main terms:** Largest territorial/punitive settlement for **Hungary** as successor to **Magyar**-led **Kingdom of Hungary**.

**Territorial terms (textbook detail set):** **Slovakia + Ruthenia → Czechoslovakia**. **Transylvania → Romania**. **Croatia + Slovenia → Yugoslavia**. Hungary lost **~two-thirds** of population and much **industrial** land.

**Military terms:** Restricted armed forces; no aggressive posture.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** Reparations; **minority** obligations; economic dislocation.

**Who benefited:** **Romania**, **Czechoslovakia**, **Yugoslavia**.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Hungary** (core **revisionist** state of the 1930s).

**Why it mattered later:** Hungarian diplomacy tilts toward **Italy/Germany** partially for **revision**; **Ethnic Hungarian** minorities abroad become flashpoints.

**What students often confuse about it:** Mixing with **Saint-Germain** (Austria) or **Neuilly** (Bulgaria); thinking Hungary kept **Croatia**.

---

## 14. Treaty of Rapallo — 16 April 1922

**Name:** Treaty of Rapallo.

**Date:** **16 April 1922**.

**Parties involved:** **Weimar Germany** and **Soviet Russia (RSFSR/USSR framework)**.

**Main terms:** Mutual **renunciation of financial/territorial claims** from the war era; **normalisation**; secret military-industrial cooperation.

**Territorial terms:** **Renunciation** of old claims (not a border-drawing treaty).

**Military terms (textbook “SECRET” bundle):** **German firms** (e.g. **Krupp**) helped build **tanks/artillery/aircraft** tested/trained in **Russia**; **German officers** trained on Soviet soil — designed to **evade Versailles** controls.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** **Trade**; diplomatic **outflanking** of the **Versailles** enforcement circle; shocked **Allies**.

**Who benefited:** **Germany** and **USSR** (survival + modernisation).

**Who was dissatisfied:** **France/Poland** (security fears); **Allied** opinion.

**Why it mattered later:** Prototype for later **Nazi-Soviet** realpolitik (different ideology, similar **cynical** geometry); shows **Weimar** was not purely “pacifist” in practice.

**What students often confuse about it:** Mixing **1922 Rapallo** with **1920 Rapallo** (Italy–Yugoslav) if your course mentions both; thinking it was **open alliance** rather than **selective cooperation**.

## 15. Treaty of Lausanne — 24 July 1923

**Name:** Treaty of Lausanne.

**Date:** **24 July 1923**.

**Parties involved:** **Turkey (Grand National Assembly government)** and **Allies** (UK, France, Italy, Greece, Japan, etc.).

**Main terms:** Replaced **Sèvres** for the **Turkish core state**; recognised **Kemalist** Turkey.

**Territorial terms:** **Borders** roughly modern Turkey; **Eastern Thrace** adjustments; **Aegean islands** split Greece/Turkey; **Mosul** left to later **Iraq** (British) diplomacy (exam courses often mention dispute).

**Military terms:** Turkish sovereignty restored over **Straits** subject to later conventions (students: **Montreux 1936** updates control).

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** Ended **capitulations**; **population exchange** Greek/Turkish context (often linked in teaching to **1923** humanitarian catastrophe).

**Who benefited:** **Turkey**; **Greece** gained clarity in some islands; **Britain** kept **Iraq** oil trajectory.

**Who was dissatisfied:** Some **Greek** refugees; **Kurdish** autonomy hopes; **Armenian** diaspora claims.

**Why it mattered later:** **Modern Middle Eastern** neighbour relations; **Turkish** secular state-building under **Atatürk**.

**What students often confuse about it:** Thinking it created **Israel/Palestine** mandates (that’s **Paris/San Remo** layer); confusing with **Sèvres**.

## 16. Dawes Plan — April 1924

**Name:** Dawes Plan.

**Date:** Agreed **April 1924** (implementation 1924–25).

**Parties involved:** **Germany**, **Allies** (committee led by **Charles Dawes**), **USA** as lender/informal guarantor of flows.

**Main terms:** Stabilised **reparations** after **Ruhr crisis** and **hyperinflation**.

**Economic / reparations / political terms (textbook detail set):** **French/Belgian troops withdraw from the Ruhr**; annual payments set to what Germany **“could reasonably afford”**; **Reichsbank** restructured under **foreign supervision**; Germany received **large US loans** → **capital imports**.

**Military terms:** None direct; stabilisation reduced **Franco-German** coercion cycles.

**Territorial terms:** None.

**Who benefited:** **Germany** (recovery + credit); **USA** (banking/bonds); **France** (payments resumed without occupation).

**Who was dissatisfied:** **German nationalists** (foreign control); critics of **American** financial leverage.

**Why it mattered later:** **Golden twenties** partial recovery; fragility exposed by **Wall Street crash** → **Young Plan** then collapse.

**What students often confuse about it:** Mixing with **Young Plan**; thinking it **cancelled** reparations; confusing **Ruhr withdrawal** with **Rhineland evacuation** schedules.

---

## 17. Locarno Treaties — October 1925

**Name:** Locarno Treaties.

**Date:** Signed **October 1925**, registered **1926**.

**Parties involved:** **Germany**, **France**, **Belgium**, **Britain**, **Italy** (multiple instruments).

**Main terms:** “**Locarno spirit**” — partial **normalisation** of **Weimar Germany**; **western** guarantees vs **eastern** ambiguity.

**Territorial / frontier terms (textbook detail set):** **Germany/France/Belgium** promise to respect **western frontiers**; **Germany** accepts **loss of Alsace-Lorraine** and **Eupen-Malmédy** as final in this framework.

**Military terms:** Arbitration/compulsory processes; demilitarised zone logic reinforced in places (link to **Versailles** Rhineland regime).

**Guarantee terms (textbook detail set):** **Treaty of Mutual Guarantee**: **Britain + Italy** assist any victim of **unprovoked aggression** violating the western pact.

**Eastern borders (textbook detail set):** **No** comparable mutual guarantees for Germany’s **eastern** borders with **Poland/Czechoslovakia** (only **arbitration** treaties) → huge strategic implication.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** Helps **Germany** enter **League** (**1926**); diplomatic thaw.

**Who benefited:** **Stresemann’s Germany**; **Britain** (appears as balancer).

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Poland/Czechoslovakia** (second-class security); **France** if it wanted firmer eastern ties.

**Why it mattered later:** **Hitler** could later claim **eastern revision** was always “allowed” diplomatically; **Rhineland remilitarisation 1936** breaks the spirit.

**What students often confuse about it:** Thinking **eastern** borders were guaranteed; confusing **Locarno** with **Kellogg-Briand**.

---

## 18. Kellogg-Briand Pact — 27 August 1928

**Name:** General Treaty for Renunciation of War (Kellogg-Briand Pact).

**Date:** Signed **27 August 1928**; effective **1929**.

**Parties involved:** Initially **USA + France**, then **many states** worldwide.

**Main terms:** **Renounced war** as an instrument of **national policy** (with major loopholes in practice).

**Territorial terms:** None.

**Military terms:** None enforcement mechanism.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** Moral-legal **anti-war** framing; later used (weakly) in **Nuremberg** discourse.

**Who benefited:** **Political leaders** seeking **public opinion** wins; **USA** “outlawry of war” movement.

**Who was dissatisfied:** Realists who noted **no enforcement**; states planning **self-defence** expansions.

**Why it mattered later:** Shows **1920s** legalism; contrast with **1930s** aggression; demonstrates **League** limitations.

**What students often confuse about it:** Thinking it **stopped wars**; confusing signing with **collective security**.

---

## 19. Young Plan — 1929 (implemented from 1930)

**Name:** Young Plan.

**Date:** Agreed **1929**; operational **1930** (linked to **Hague** agreements).

**Parties involved:** **Germany**, **Allies**, **USA** (Young Committee).

**Main terms:** Restructured **reparations** and international controls.

**Economic / reparations / political terms (textbook detail set):** Total reparations cut from **£6.6 billion** to **£2 billion**; **dismantles international controls** over the **German economy** compared to **Dawes**-era supervision (Bank for International Settlements created as part of package — often examined).

**Military terms:** None direct.

**Territorial terms:** None.

**Who benefited:** **Germany** (lighter burden, more autonomy).

**Who was dissatisfied:** **German nationalists** still opposed any payments (**Hugenberg** campaigns); **French** security anxieties.

**Why it mattered later:** **Great Depression** + politics → **Hoover Moratorium**, **Lausanne 1932** (often adjacent in courses), **Hitler** ends payments.

**What students often confuse about it:** Mixing with **Dawes**; thinking it **ended** reparations immediately; confusing **BIS** purpose.

---

## 20. Anglo-German Naval Agreement — 18 June 1935

**Name:** Anglo-German Naval Agreement (AGNA).

**Date:** **18 June 1935**.

**Parties involved:** **United Kingdom** and **Nazi Germany**.

**Main terms:** Allowed **German navy** to reach **35%** of **Royal Navy** surface tonnage and **45%** of **submarine** tonnage (with escape clauses); **bilateral** deal **undermining Versailles** naval limits.

**Territorial terms:** None.

**Military terms:** **Naval** ratios; **submarine** parity clauses in some readings — check your textbook’s exact percentages.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** **Diplomatic coup** for **Hitler**; **stab in the back** to **Stresa** solidarity.

**Who benefited:** **Hitler** (legitimacy + wiggle room); **Britain** (temporary avoidance of naval race rhetoric).

**Who was dissatisfied:** **France**; **Italy**; **Poland**; **League** supporters.

**Why it mattered later:** Encouraged **German** rearmament narrative; showed **appeasement** as **selective**.

**What students often confuse about it:** Thinking it was **multilateral**; mixing with **Washington Naval Treaty** ratios.

---

## 21. Stresa Front — April 1935

**Name:** Stresa Front (Stresa Conference).

**Date:** **11–14 April 1935**.

**Parties involved:** **Italy**, **France**, **United Kingdom**.

**Main terms:** **Diplomatic alignment** to resist **German** revisionism (especially post-**Saar** plebiscite, **Hitler** announcements) and **Austrian** independence concerns.

**Territorial terms:** None binding.

**Military terms:** No automatic military convention; political **front**.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** **League** rhetoric vs **aggression**; soon eroded by **Abyssinia** and **Anglo-German Naval Agreement**.

**Who benefited:** Short-term **French** security diplomacy; **Mussolini** prestige.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Germany**; later **Ethiopia** (invaded).

**Why it mattered later:** Demonstrates **fragility** of **anti-Hitler** unity; **Abyssinian War** breaks **Anglo-Italian** trust.

**What students often confuse about it:** Treating it as a **formal military alliance**; thinking it **stopped** Italian expansion.

## 22. Franco-Soviet Treaty of Mutual Assistance — 2 May 1935

**Name:** Franco-Soviet Treaty of Mutual Assistance.

**Date:** **2 May 1935**.

**Parties involved:** **France** and **USSR**.

**Main terms:** **Mutual assistance** against aggression, tied to **League** procedures (textbook emphasis: **Locarno**-compatible framing attempted).

**Territorial terms:** None.

**Military terms:** Potential **military coordination** if aggression verified — **never** effectively operationalised.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** **Diplomatic isolation** of **Germany** from east; feared by **French** right and **British** conservatives.

**Who benefited:** **USSR** (returns to great-power diplomacy); **France** (eastern counterweight dream).

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Germany**; **Poland** (refused transit); **British** appeasers.

**Why it mattered later:** Feeds **Hitler’s** anti-Comintern propaganda; shows **collective security** couldn’t overcome **Polish** refusal and **British** hesitation.

**What students often confuse about it:** Thinking it created an **eastern Locarno**; mixing dates with **Nazi-Soviet Pact**.

## 23. Rome-Berlin Axis — 25 October 1936 (declaration) / reinforced later

**Name:** Rome–Berlin Axis.

**Date:** **25 October 1936** (first public axis language); deepened by **1937–39** cooperation.

**Parties involved:** **Fascist Italy** and **Nazi Germany** (plus **Japan** sometimes in wider “Axis” talk).

**Main terms:** Coordination against **Communism** and **Spanish Republic** ( **Civil War** intervention); ideological **fascist–nazi** alignment.

**Territorial terms:** Not a partition treaty; **Spanish** intervention as testing ground.

**Military terms:** **Condor Legion**; Italian troops; shared **intervention** in Spain.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** Propaganda of a **new order**; undermines **League**.

**Who benefited:** **Hitler** and **Mussolini**.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Liberals**; **Spanish Republicans**; **USSR**.

**Why it mattered later:** Stepping stone to **Pact of Steel** and **WWII** coalition.

**What students often confuse about it:** Thinking it was a **written military pact** like **Pact of Steel**; confusing **Anti-Comintern** with **Axis**.

## 24. Anti-Comintern Pact — 25 November 1936

**Name:** Anti-Comintern Pact.

**Date:** **25 November 1936** (expanded later with **Japan**, **Italy**, satellites).

**Parties involved:** **Nazi Germany** and **Japan** initially; **Italy** joined **1937**.

**Main terms:** Public **anti-communist** intelligence/cooperation façade; **secret** clauses could imply **neutrality** in future wars (interpretations vary — courses often stress **anti-Soviet** alignment).

**Territorial terms:** None direct.

**Military terms:** Not a full alliance; more **diplomatic** alignment.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** Ideological **bloc-building**; scares **Stalin**.

**Who benefited:** **Germany** and **Japan** diplomatically.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **USSR**; Chinese **United Front** context.

**Why it mattered later:** Context for **Spanish Civil War** diplomacy and **China**; ironic prelude to **1939 Nazi-Soviet** deal.

**What students often confuse about it:** Treating it as **automatic war** against USSR; mixing with **Tripartite Pact 1940** (often beyond 1945 course edge).

## 25. Munich Agreement — 30 September 1938

**Name:** Munich Agreement.

**Date:** **30 September 1938**.

**Parties involved:** **Germany**, **Italy**, **UK**, **France** — **Czechoslovakia not invited**; **USSR not invited**.

**Main terms:** **Sudetenland** transferred to **Germany**; **appeasement** climax.

**Territorial terms (textbook detail set):** Germany takes **Sudetenland** (heavily fortified Czech defences lost).

**Military terms:** Czechoslovakia effectively **strategically crippled**; later **March 1939** occupation becomes easier.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** **Hitler** promises **no further territorial demands** in that moment (broken); **Chamberlain**: **“peace for our time”**; **Anschluss** logic reinforced.

**Who benefited:** **Hitler** (bloodless win); **Sudeten German** Nazi leaders.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Czechoslovakia**; **USSR** (ignored); **anti-appeasement** British voices; eventually **Chamberlain’s** credibility.

**Why it mattered later:** Proved **appeasement** failed; **Polish/German** crisis accelerates; **Stalin** learns **West** won’t fight for eastern clients.

**What students often confuse about it:** Thinking **Poland** was noble defender (it took **Teschen**); confusing **Sudetenland** with **all of Czechoslovakia** immediately.

## 26. Pact of Steel — 22 May 1939

**Name:** Pact of Steel.

**Date:** **22 May 1939**.

**Parties involved:** **Nazi Germany** and **Fascist Italy**.

**Main terms:** **Military-political alliance**; aggressive tone; **Italy** often the weaker partner.

**Territorial terms:** None explicit partition.

**Military terms:** Commitment to **common war** if conflict arises (wording still left **Mussolini** some wriggle — but textbook: formal alliance).

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** Solidified **Axis**; alarmed **Allies**.

**Who benefited:** **Hitler** (southern flank diplomacy); **Mussolini** (great-power pose).

**Who was dissatisfied:** **France/UK**; **Italian** generals (unprepared).

**Why it mattered later:** Italy enters **WWII** (1940) as co-belligerent; shapes **Mediterranean** war.

**What students often confuse about it:** Mixing with **Anti-Comintern** or **Nazi-Soviet Pact**; thinking Italy was **ready**.

## 27. Nazi-Soviet Pact (Molotov-Ribbentrop) — 23 August 1939

**Name:** Nazi-Soviet Non-Aggression Pact (+ **Secret Additional Protocol**).

**Date:** **23 August 1939**.

**Parties involved:** **Nazi Germany** and **USSR**.

**Main terms:** **Non-aggression**; **economic** deals; **spheres of influence** in Eastern Europe.

**Territorial / secret terms (textbook detail set):** **Secret**: Germany can **attack Poland** without **Soviet** interference; USSR gains **eastern Poland**, **Finland**, **Estonia**, **Latvia** (and **Bessarabia** sphere with Romania often in fuller accounts — use your map).

**Military terms:** **Joint** parades later in **occupied Poland**; **invasion of Poland 17 Sept** by USSR.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** **Extended trading links**; **raw materials** for Germany; shocks **world** diplomacy.

**Who benefited:** **Stalin** (time + buffer); **Hitler** (one-front war for Poland/early war).

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Allies**; **Comintern** true believers; **anti-Nazi** left worldwide.

**Why it mattered later:** Made **WWII** possible in the **Polish** form; **Katyn**, **Baltic** annexations, **Winter War** seeds.

**What students often confuse about it:** Thinking it was **ideological friendship**; ignoring **secret protocol**; dating **Barbarossa** wrong.

## 28. Anglo-Polish Agreement of Mutual Assistance — 25 August 1939

**Name:** Anglo-Polish Agreement of Mutual Assistance (often “**guarantee**” in teaching).

**Date:** **25 August 1939** (protocols continued into **1939**).

**Parties involved:** **United Kingdom** and **Poland** (France parallel treaties often paired in lessons).

**Main terms:** **Mutual assistance** if either is attacked by a **European power**; attempt to **deter** **Hitler** after **Munich** collapse.

**Territorial terms:** Implicit defence of **Polish** independence/frontiers as understood diplomatically (courses stress **credibility** debate).

**Military terms:** Limited **practical** ability to **rescue Poland** quickly; **Royal Navy**/air dimension vs land reality.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** Diplomatic **line in sand**; failed **deterrence**.

**Who benefited:** **Poland** morally; **Britain** clarifies alliance system.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Appeasers**; **realists** who saw impossibility.

**Why it mattered later:** UK enters war **3 Sept 1939** after **German invasion** **1 Sept**.

**What students often confuse about it:** Thinking Britain could **save Poland militarily in 1939**; confusing with **Franco-Soviet** treaty.

## 29. Twenty-One Demands — 18 January 1915

**Name:** Twenty-One Demands (Japan to China).

**Date:** Presented **18 January 1915**; coerced acceptance in revised form **25 May 1915**.

**Parties involved:** **Japan** and **Yuan Shikai’s** **Republic of China** (under pressure).

**Main terms:** Japan exploited **WWI** (**European** entanglement) and **Allied** indulgence to extract **concessions** in **China**.

**Territorial terms:** Extended **leaseholds**/**rights** in **Fujian** influence, **South Manchuria**/**Inner Mongolia** privileges; **Shandong** German rights transfer to Japan (postwar **Versailles** issue).

**Military terms:** Restrictions on Chinese **coastal** fortifications / police advisers in some versions (textbook emphasis: **control** and **advisers**).

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** **Joint Sino-Japanese** companies; **advisers**; **political** humiliation → fuels **May Fourth Movement 1919**.

**Who benefited:** **Japan**.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Chinese** nationalists; **USA** (notes diplomacy later).

**Why it mattered later:** **Shandong** dispute at **Versailles**; **Chinese nationalism**; **Pacific** tensions into **Washington Conference**.

**What students often confuse about it:** Dating it to **1930s**; mixing with **Washington Nine-Power** principles.

## 30. Washington Conference Treaties — 1921–1922

**Name / Date:** **Washington Naval Conference** outcomes (**12 Nov 1921 – 6 Feb 1922**), major treaties **1922**.

**Parties involved:** **USA, UK, Japan, France, Italy** (Five-Power); **Nine powers** for China issues; dominions present in British delegation.

**Main instruments (matrix-style):**

### 30a. Five-Power Treaty (Naval Limitation)

**Main terms:** Capital ship **ratio** **5:5:3:1.75:1.75** (**USA:UK:Japan:France:Italy**); battleship **tonnage** caps; **moratorium** on new fortifications (**Pacific** bases clauses favour USA/UK vs Japan — textbook map point).

**Military terms:** Naval arms control; defines **capital ship** classes.

**Who benefited:** **USA** (parity with UK); **UK** (avoid race); **Japan** (recognised as third power — but inferior ratio).

**Dissatisfied:** **Japan** (inferiority); **smaller navies** excluded.

**Later:** Collapsed with **1930s** rearmament; contrast **Anglo-German Naval Agreement**.

### 30b. Four-Power Treaty (USA, UK, France, Japan)

**Main terms:** Replaced **Anglo-Japanese Alliance**; consult on **Pacific** crises.

**Military terms:** No automatic war.

**Later:** Weak crisis management; diplomatic shift.

### 30c. Nine-Power Treaty (China)

**Main terms:** Respect **Chinese sovereignty** and **Open Door**; **no predatory** pursuit of special rights (idealised).

**Economic / political:** Anti-**Japanese** expansion rhetoric; **Shandong** returned to China with conditions (**separate** settlement at conference).

**Who benefited:** **China** on paper; **USA** ideologically.

**Dissatisfied:** **Japan** long-term.

**Student confusion:** Thinking naval ratios are in **Nine-Power** treaty; confusing **Washington** with **Geneva** disarmament.

## 31. Monroe Doctrine (1823) / Roosevelt Corollary (1904)

**Name:** **Monroe Doctrine** + **Roosevelt Corollary**.

**Date:** **2 December 1823** (Monroe Message); **1904** State of the Union (**Roosevelt Corollary**).

**Parties involved:** **USA** (unilateral declaration); directed against **European** re-colonisation in **Americas**; later applied vs **Latin American** states themselves.

**Main terms:** **Monroe:** **No new European colonisation** in **Americas**; **non-intervention** of Europe in **New World**; US abstains from **European** wars. **Roosevelt Corollary:** If Latin American nations **misbehave financially**, USA may exercise **“international police power”** — **preemptive intervention** justification.

**Territorial terms:** Not a treaty; shapes **Caribbean** protectorates.

**Military terms:** **Gunboat diplomacy**; **Panama Canal** zone logic (linked era).

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** **Dollar diplomacy**; **customs** control; **debt** enforcement.

**Who benefited:** **USA** hemispheric dominance.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Latin American** nationalists; **European** creditors sometimes welcomed US “order.”

**Why it mattered later:** Explains **Platt**, **Nicaragua**, **Dominican** interventions; background to **Good Neighbor** shift (1930s) in some syllabi.

**Student confusion:** Thinking Monroe authorised **any** US invasion in 1823 wording; mixing **Monroe** with **Wilson** or **Truman** doctrines.

## 32. Platt Amendment — 2 March 1901 (US law) / embedded in Cuban treaty 1903

**Name:** Platt Amendment.

**Date:** **2 March 1901** (US statute); incorporated into **Cuban constitution** **1901–1903**; **1903 US–Cuba treaty**.

**Parties involved:** **USA** imposes on **Cuba** (as condition of ending **military government**).

**Main terms:** Limits **Cuban sovereignty**; legalises **US intervention**.

**Territorial terms:** **Guantánamo Bay** naval base lease.

**Military terms:** US right to **intervene** to preserve **independence** and **order**.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** Restricts **Cuban treaty-making** with third powers; US oversight of **debt**.

**Who benefited:** **USA**.

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Cuban** nationalists.

**Why it mattered later:** Shapes **Cuban–US** relations into **Cold War** (outside 1945, but courses use it as imperialism case study).

**Student confusion:** Thinking **Treaty of Paris 1898** included Platt (it didn’t); confusing **protectorate** with **annexation**.

## 33. Tanggu Truce — 31 May 1933

**Name:** Tanggu Truce.

**Date:** **31 May 1933**.

**Parties involved:** **Republic of China (Nanjing government)** and **Japan** (Kwantung Army / Japanese forces in **Manchuria/Jehol** context).

**Main terms:** Ceasefire after **Japanese** advances; created a **demilitarised buffer** near **Beijing/Tianjin**; de facto acceptance of **Japanese** control in **Manchukuo** sphere.

**Territorial terms:** **Demilitarised zone** south of **Great Wall** in key strips (textbook map: **Hebei** corridor issues); Chinese troops withdraw from certain areas.

**Military terms:** **Ceasefire lines**; restricts Chinese **military presence** near capital region.

**Economic / reparations / political terms:** Humiliation for **Chiang Kai-shek**; fuels **Chinese** public anger; complicates **United Front** politics.

**Who benefited:** **Japan** tactically; **Chiang** briefly (consolidate vs **Communists** — textbook angle).

**Who was dissatisfied:** **Chinese** public; **CCP** propaganda opportunity.

**Why it mattered later:** Stepping stone to full **North China** crisis and **Marco Polo Bridge** escalation path (1937).

**Student confusion:** Mixing with **Twenty-One Demands** or **Washington** treaties; thinking it **recognised Manchukuo** explicitly (wording is de facto vs de jure — check textbook phrasing).
