# Master chronology no confusion

Revision-first chronology from 1870 to 1945. Each line gives the event, why it matters, the exact pair students confuse, and the distinction rule.

## 1870-1919

- **1870-71 | Franco-Prussian War**  
  Why it matters: French defeat and German victory reshape the European balance and create long-term French insecurity.  
  Confuse with: **1871 German unification**  
  Distinction rule: **War first, unified Germany second.**

- **1871 | German unification**  
  Why it matters: A powerful new German state appears in central Europe.  
  Confuse with: **1870-71 Franco-Prussian War**  
  Distinction rule: **Unification is the outcome of the war.**

- **1873-96 | Long Depression**  
  Why it matters: Encourages tariffs, market-seeking, and pro-imperial economic arguments.  
  Confuse with: **1929 Great Depression**  
  Distinction rule: **Late C19 trade pressure, not post-Wall Street global crash.**

- **1882 | British occupation of Egypt**  
  Why it matters: Secures Suez and feeds wider scramble logic in Africa.  
  Confuse with: **1884-85 Berlin Conference**  
  Distinction rule: **Egypt is direct control; Berlin is the rules framework.**

- **1884-85 | Berlin Conference / Treaty of Berlin**  
  Why it matters: Sets effective occupation rules and accelerates the African scramble.  
  Confuse with: **1926 Treaty of Berlin**  
  Distinction rule: **1885 = Africa; 1926 = Germany-USSR.**

- **1894-95 | First Sino-Japanese War**  
  Why it matters: Japan defeats China and establishes itself as a rising Asian power.  
  Confuse with: **1904-05 Russo-Japanese War**  
  Distinction rule: **1894-95 Japan vs China; 1904-05 Japan vs Russia.**

- **1895 | Treaty of Shimonoseki**  
  Why it matters: Formalizes Japanese gains after defeating China.  
  Confuse with: **1895 Triple Intervention**  
  Distinction rule: **Treaty first, rollback pressure second.**

- **1895 | Triple Intervention**  
  Why it matters: Humiliates Japan and feeds later anti-Russian strategy.  
  Confuse with: **Treaty of Shimonoseki**  
  Distinction rule: **Diplomatic humiliation after military victory.**

- **1898 | Fashoda Incident**  
  Why it matters: Colonial rivalry creates tension but still ends in compromise.  
  Confuse with: **Moroccan crises**  
  Distinction rule: **Fashoda = Britain-France in Sudan; Morocco = Germany-France crisis.**

- **1898 | Spanish-American War**  
  Why it matters: Marks the USA as an overseas imperial power.  
  Confuse with: **Treaty of Paris 1898**  
  Distinction rule: **War vs settlement.**

- **1899 | Open Door policy**  
  Why it matters: The USA shapes China diplomacy without taking a classic colony.  
  Confuse with: **Washington settlement 1921-22**  
  Distinction rule: **Open Door = 1899 policy; Washington = later treaty package.**

- **1899-1902 | Second Boer War**  
  Why it matters: British victory is expensive, divisive, and damaging to prestige.  
  Confuse with: **easy colonial victory**  
  Distinction rule: **Guerrilla war and camps = not easy.**

- **1902 | Anglo-Japanese Alliance**  
  Why it matters: Gives Japan diplomatic recognition and links Britain to East Asian security.  
  Confuse with: **1904 Entente Cordiale**  
  Distinction rule: **1902 Britain-Japan; 1904 Britain-France.**

- **1904 | Entente Cordiale**  
  Why it matters: Settles Anglo-French disputes and prepares later anti-German alignment.  
  Confuse with: **full military alliance from 1904**  
  Distinction rule: **Understanding first, alliance logic later.**

- **1904-05 | Russo-Japanese War**  
  Why it matters: Japan defeats a European great power and shocks the old hierarchy.  
  Confuse with: **First Sino-Japanese War**  
  Distinction rule: **Russia, not China.**

- **1905 | Treaty of Portsmouth**  
  Why it matters: Ends the Russo-Japanese War and confirms Japan's status.  
  Confuse with: **1921-22 Washington settlement**  
  Distinction rule: **Portsmouth ends a war; Washington manages naval rivalry.**

- **1905-06 | First Moroccan Crisis / Algeciras**  
  Why it matters: Germany fails to split the Entente.  
  Confuse with: **1911 Agadir crisis**  
  Distinction rule: **Algeciras first, Agadir later.**

- **1911 | Second Moroccan Crisis / Agadir**  
  Why it matters: Deepens suspicion of Germany before 1914.  
  Confuse with: **1905-06 Algeciras**  
  Distinction rule: **1911 = Panther at Agadir.**

- **1911 | Chinese Revolution**  
  Why it matters: Ends Qing authority but not Chinese fragmentation.  
  Confuse with: **1912 Republic solved the state problem**  
  Distinction rule: **Dynasty falls; instability remains.**

- **Jan-Mar 1912 | Republic of China established**  
  Why it matters: A republic is proclaimed, but military politics still dominate.  
  Confuse with: **1919 May Fourth Movement**  
  Distinction rule: **1912 = state form; 1919 = nationalist protest/intellectual shift.**

- **1914 | Sarajevo and outbreak of First World War**  
  Why it matters: Destroys the old European order and sets up the treaty crisis of 1919-20.  
  Confuse with: **Franco-Prussian War**  
  Distinction rule: **1914 = general war; 1870-71 = German unification war.**

- **1914 | Japan enters WWI**  
  Why it matters: Expands Japanese influence in Asia during the war.  
  Confuse with: **1904-05 Russo-Japanese War**  
  Distinction rule: **1914 Japan vs Germany context; 1904-05 Japan vs Russia.**

- **1915 | Twenty-One Demands**  
  Why it matters: Intensifies Chinese hostility to Japan and deepens Japanese leverage.  
  Confuse with: **Washington Conference**  
  Distinction rule: **1915 coercion vs 1921-22 negotiated restraint.**

- **1916 | Death of Yuan Shih-kai**  
  Why it matters: Accelerates the warlord era.  
  Confuse with: **1912 Republic**  
  Distinction rule: **1912 republic begins; 1916 fragmentation deepens.**

- **Jan 1918 | Wilson's Fourteen Points**  
  Why it matters: Sets the benchmark against which Versailles is judged.  
  Confuse with: **Treaty of Versailles**  
  Distinction rule: **Speech first, treaty later.**

- **Nov 1918 | Armistice and German collapse**  
  Why it matters: Ends fighting and opens the peace settlement.  
  Confuse with: **1919 Versailles**  
  Distinction rule: **Armistice stops war; treaty sets terms.**

- **1919 | Paris Peace Conference**  
  Why it matters: Creates the postwar treaty system and League framework.  
  Confuse with: **Treaty of Versailles alone**  
  Distinction rule: **Paris creates several treaties, not just one.**

- **1919 | Rejection of Japan's racial equality proposal**  
  Why it matters: Deepens Japanese resentment at Western double standards.  
  Confuse with: **Shantung issue at Paris**  
  Distinction rule: **Status/equality issue, not only territorial issue.**

- **1919 | May Fourth Movement**  
  Why it matters: Turns Chinese nationalism into a wider ideological and political movement.  
  Confuse with: **1911 Revolution**  
  Distinction rule: **1911 topples Qing; 1919 radicalizes modern nationalism.**

- **1919 | Treaty of Versailles**  
  Why it matters: Creates German grievance, reparations disputes, military limits, and later revisionism.  
  Confuse with: **all postwar treaties together**  
  Distinction rule: **Versailles = Germany only.**

## 1920-1929

- **1920 onward | League of Nations operates**  
  Why it matters: Becomes the main framework for collective security and arbitration in the 1920s.  
  Confuse with: **League as either total success or total failure**  
  Distinction rule: **Small disputes often yes; major-power crises usually no.**

- **1920 | Treaty of Sevres**  
  Why it matters: Harsh Ottoman settlement later overturned by Turkish resistance.  
  Confuse with: **1923 Lausanne**  
  Distinction rule: **Sevres imposed; Lausanne revised.**

- **1921 | Reparations fixed for Germany**  
  Why it matters: Hardens German resentment and sets up Ruhr and hyperinflation.  
  Confuse with: **Dawes / Young**  
  Distinction rule: **1921 fixes total; 1924 and 1929 reschedule/reduce.**

- **1921 | CCP founded**  
  Why it matters: Starts the organized communist challenge in China.  
  Confuse with: **1945 CCP dominance**  
  Distinction rule: **Founding is not victory.**

- **1921-22 | Washington Conference**  
  Why it matters: Creates naval ratios and temporary Pacific stabilization.  
  Confuse with: **1902 Anglo-Japanese Alliance**  
  Distinction rule: **Multilateral treaty package, not bilateral alliance.**

- **1922 | Mussolini becomes PM**  
  Why it matters: Fascism comes to power in Italy before Nazism in Germany.  
  Confuse with: **Hitler 1933**  
  Distinction rule: **Italy first, Germany later.**

- **1922 | Treaty of Rapallo**  
  Why it matters: Germany and USSR cooperate outside Versailles.  
  Confuse with: **Treaty of Berlin 1926**  
  Distinction rule: **Rapallo first; Berlin renews later.**

- **1923 | Ruhr occupation**  
  Why it matters: Shows reparations and security policy crashing into each other.  
  Confuse with: **1936 Rhineland**  
  Distinction rule: **Ruhr = French coercion; Rhineland = Hitler overturns treaty.**

- **1923 | German hyperinflation**  
  Why it matters: Makes coercive reparations policy look self-defeating.  
  Confuse with: **1929 Crash**  
  Distinction rule: **German currency collapse vs global depression.**

- **1923 | Corfu Crisis**  
  Why it matters: Exposes League weakness against a determined major power.  
  Confuse with: **Abyssinia**  
  Distinction rule: **Corfu is 1923 Italy-Greece; Abyssinia is 1935-36 Italy-East Africa.**

- **1923 | Lausanne**  
  Why it matters: Shows one major postwar treaty system can be revised successfully.  
  Confuse with: **Locarno**  
  Distinction rule: **Lausanne = Turkey; Locarno = western Europe.**

- **1923 | First United Front deepens**  
  Why it matters: Makes later KMT-CCP split more consequential.  
  Confuse with: **Second United Front**  
  Distinction rule: **1920s anti-warlord front vs 1936-37 anti-Japan front.**

- **1924 | Japanese Exclusion Act in the USA**  
  Why it matters: Strengthens anti-Western resentment in Japan.  
  Confuse with: **Washington naval limits**  
  Distinction rule: **Immigration insult vs naval limitation.**

- **1924 | Dawes Plan**  
  Why it matters: Stabilizes Germany through reparations rescheduling and US loans.  
  Confuse with: **Young Plan**  
  Distinction rule: **Dawes first, Young later.**

- **1925 | Locarno Treaties**  
  Why it matters: Peak western détente but eastern insecurity remains.  
  Confuse with: **Stresa Front**  
  Distinction rule: **1925 west-frontier settlement vs 1935 anti-German front.**

- **1925 | Sun Yat-sen dies**  
  Why it matters: KMT politics become more militarized and more Chiang-centered.  
  Confuse with: **Chiang already fully dominant**  
  Distinction rule: **Sun's death accelerates, not instantly completes, Chiang's rise.**

- **1926 | Germany joins League and signs Treaty of Berlin**  
  Why it matters: Shows Stresemann balancing respectability with revisionist options.  
  Confuse with: **Rapallo**  
  Distinction rule: **1926 combines Geneva respectability and Moscow option.**

- **1926 | Northern Expedition begins**  
  Why it matters: Drives nominal Chinese reunification under the KMT.  
  Confuse with: **Long March**  
  Distinction rule: **KMT advance vs CCP retreat.**

- **1927 | Shanghai Purge / White Terror**  
  Why it matters: Opens the long KMT-CCP civil conflict.  
  Confuse with: **Nanjing Massacre 1937**  
  Distinction rule: **1927 anti-communist purge vs 1937 Japanese atrocity.**

- **1928 | Nanking government recognized**  
  Why it matters: China looks unified internationally but remains regionally fragmented.  
  Confuse with: **real full unification**  
  Distinction rule: **Nominal unity only.**

- **1928 | Kellogg-Briand Pact**  
  Why it matters: High point of anti-war rhetoric without enforcement.  
  Confuse with: **effective collective security**  
  Distinction rule: **Moral promise, not police power.**

- **1929 | Young Plan**  
  Why it matters: Last big reparations adjustment before the Depression destroys the system.  
  Confuse with: **Dawes Plan**  
  Distinction rule: **Young reduces further after Dawes.**

- **Oct 1929 | Wall Street Crash**  
  Why it matters: Shatters the financial basis of interwar stabilization.  
  Confuse with: **1923 hyperinflation**  
  Distinction rule: **US/global crash vs German currency collapse.**

## 1930-1945

- **1930 | Central Plains War**  
  Why it matters: Shows Nationalist unity still depends on force.  
  Confuse with: **Northern Expedition**  
  Distinction rule: **Later internal warlord challenge, not initial reunification drive.**

- **1931 | Manchurian Crisis / Mukden Incident**  
  Why it matters: First decisive League failure of the 1930s.  
  Confuse with: **1937 full-scale Sino-Japanese War**  
  Distinction rule: **1931 Manchuria first; 1937 wider China war later.**

- **May 1932 | May 15 Incident**  
  Why it matters: A decisive step in the collapse of Japanese parliamentary democracy.  
  Confuse with: **1936 military-dominated Japan as if it appeared suddenly**  
  Distinction rule: **1932 weakens party rule before later expansion.**

- **1933 | Hitler becomes chancellor**  
  Why it matters: German revisionism fuses with dictatorship and radical expansionism.  
  Confuse with: **Mussolini 1922**  
  Distinction rule: **Italy fascism first; Nazi Germany later and more destructive.**

- **1933 | Germany leaves League and Disarmament Conference**  
  Why it matters: A key state walks out of the interwar security structure.  
  Confuse with: **US non-membership from start**  
  Distinction rule: **USA never joined; Germany joined then left.**

- **1933 | Tanggu Truce**  
  Why it matters: Confirms Japanese gains and deepens criticism of Chiang's sequencing.  
  Confuse with: **League solution to Manchuria**  
  Distinction rule: **Bilateral compromise, not collective-security victory.**

- **1934-35 | Long March**  
  Why it matters: CCP survives and Mao rises.  
  Confuse with: **Northern Expedition**  
  Distinction rule: **Retreat/survival vs advance/reunification.**

- **1935 | Saar plebiscite and open rearmament**  
  Why it matters: Makes German treaty revision visible and concrete.  
  Confuse with: **Rhineland 1936**  
  Distinction rule: **Saar returns and army expands before Rhineland gamble.**

- **Apr 1935 | Stresa Front**  
  Why it matters: Short-lived anti-German front that soon weakens.  
  Confuse with: **Locarno**  
  Distinction rule: **Stresa = 1935 anti-German; Locarno = 1925 settlement.**

- **Jun 1935 | Anglo-German Naval Agreement**  
  Why it matters: Weakens Stresa and alarms France.  
  Confuse with: **strengthened anti-German unity**  
  Distinction rule: **Bilateral deal, multilateral damage.**

- **1935-36 | Abyssinian Crisis**  
  Why it matters: Publicly destroys faith in sanctions and collective security.  
  Confuse with: **Hitler's expansion**  
  Distinction rule: **Italy in East Africa, not Germany in Europe.**

- **Mar 1936 | Rhineland remilitarized**  
  Why it matters: Hitler gains a major strategic and psychological victory at low risk.  
  Confuse with: **Anschluss**  
  Distinction rule: **Treaty zone first, Austria later.**

- **Dec 1936 | Xi'an Incident and Second United Front agreement**  
  Why it matters: Shifts Chinese politics toward anti-Japanese cooperation.  
  Confuse with: **1937 Marco Polo Bridge / full war**  
  Distinction rule: **Xi'an before total war.**

- **1936-39 | Spanish Civil War**  
  Why it matters: Exposes the emptiness of non-intervention and previews wider conflict.  
  Confuse with: **Abyssinia**  
  Distinction rule: **Spain = civil war; Abyssinia = colonial invasion.**

- **1936-37 | Anti-Comintern alignment expands**  
  Why it matters: Connects European and Asian revisionist diplomacy.  
  Confuse with: **Nazi-Soviet Pact**  
  Distinction rule: **Anti-Soviet alignment before 1939 Soviet deal.**

- **Jul 1937 | Full-scale Sino-Japanese War begins**  
  Why it matters: East Asia becomes a major war theater.  
  Confuse with: **1931 Manchuria**  
  Distinction rule: **1931 northeast seizure; 1937 full China war.**

- **Dec 1937 | Nanjing Massacre**  
  Why it matters: Symbolizes the brutality of Japanese expansion and hardens Chinese resistance.  
  Confuse with: **1927 Shanghai Purge**  
  Distinction rule: **Japanese atrocity vs KMT anti-communist purge.**

- **Mar 1938 | Anschluss**  
  Why it matters: Germany absorbs Austria and worsens Czechoslovakia's position.  
  Confuse with: **Munich**  
  Distinction rule: **Austria first, Sudetenland second.**

- **Sep 1938 | Munich Agreement**  
  Why it matters: Peak appeasement; Czech strategic security collapses.  
  Confuse with: **March 1939 Prague occupation**  
  Distinction rule: **Munich transfers Sudetenland; Prague destroys the rest.**

- **Mar 1939 | Occupation of the rest of Czechoslovakia**  
  Why it matters: Exposes Hitler as more than a limited ethnic revisionist.  
  Confuse with: **Munich**  
  Distinction rule: **Prague proves wider aims.**

- **Aug 1939 | Nazi-Soviet Pact**  
  Why it matters: Isolates Poland and removes Germany's immediate two-front-war problem.  
  Confuse with: **ideological friendship**  
  Distinction rule: **Strategy, not ideological harmony.**

- **Sep 1939 | Invasion of Poland and outbreak of European war**  
  Why it matters: Ends appeasement and starts WWII in Europe.  
  Confuse with: **Pearl Harbor as the start of all war**  
  Distinction rule: **Europe 1939; USA enters 1941.**

- **Jul 1941 | US oil embargo on Japan**  
  Why it matters: Sharpens Japan's resource crisis and war calculation.  
  Confuse with: **Pearl Harbor**  
  Distinction rule: **Pressure first, attack later.**

- **7 Dec 1941 | Pearl Harbor**  
  Why it matters: USA enters war and globalizes the conflict fully.  
  Confuse with: **total destruction of US Pacific power**  
  Distinction rule: **Surprise blow, not total knockout.**

- **1945 | Japanese defeat and withdrawal from China**  
  Why it matters: Ends Japanese empire in China but leaves Chinese civil conflict unresolved.  
  Confuse with: **CCP final victory in 1945**  
  Distinction rule: **Foreign war ends; civil struggle continues.**
