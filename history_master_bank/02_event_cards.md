# 02 Event Cards

Source basis: uploaded markdown extraction of Cambridge International AS Level History: International History, 1870-1945. Page references are only included where the extraction made them reasonably clear. Any OCR damage, internal inconsistency, or weakly supported detail is flagged rather than normalised.


Each card separates background from significance. Analytical lines are phrased for revision use, not essay padding.

## British occupation of Egypt (1882)
- **Date:** 1882
- **Background / causes:** British concern over the Suez Canal route and instability in Egypt.
- **What happened:** Britain took control of Egyptian administration.
- **Immediate consequences:** Secured the imperial route to India.
- **Wider significance:** The book treats this as a major stimulant to the wider scramble for Africa.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Imperialism was strategic as well as economic; key routes mattered.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Britain, Egypt, scramble for Africa, Berlin Conference.

## Berlin Conference and Treaty of Berlin (1884-85)
- **Date:** 15 Nov 1884 - 26 Feb 1885
- **Background / causes:** Rapid imperial competition in Africa risked conflict among European powers.
- **What happened:** Powers agreed rules for occupation, notification, free navigation, and negotiation of disputes.
- **Immediate consequences:** Gave the scramble a legal-diplomatic framework.
- **Wider significance:** It reduced immediate inter-European friction but accelerated African partition and ignored African consent.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Imperial rivalry could be managed temporarily; diplomacy sometimes enabled expansion rather than restraining it.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Germany, Britain, France, Belgium, Portugal, Treaty of Berlin, scramble for Africa.

## First Sino-Japanese War (1894-95)
- **Date:** 1894-95
- **Background / causes:** Rivalry over Korea and Japanese modernisation versus Chinese weakness.
- **What happened:** Japan defeated China.
- **Immediate consequences:** Treaty of Shimonoseki imposed Japanese gains.
- **Wider significance:** Confirmed Japan's emergence as a regional great power and exposed Qing weakness.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Modernisation changed power faster than old imperial prestige; East Asia was now central to international politics.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Japan, China, Treaty of Shimonoseki, Triple Intervention, Russo-Japanese War.

## Triple Intervention (1895)
- **Date:** 1895
- **Background / causes:** Russia wanted Port Arthur; France and Germany joined pressure on Japan after Shimonoseki.
- **What happened:** Japan was forced to give up part of its gains in return for compensation.
- **Immediate consequences:** Japanese humiliation; Russian advantage in Manchuria.
- **Wider significance:** A major cause of later Japanese hostility toward Russia and distrust of the European powers.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Diplomatic humiliation could matter as much as battlefield defeat.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Japan, Russia, France, Germany, Russo-Japanese War.

## Fashoda Incident (1898)
- **Date:** 1898
- **Background / causes:** British and French imperial plans collided in Sudan.
- **What happened:** Rival expeditions faced each other, but neither side chose war.
- **Immediate consequences:** Diplomatic settlement.
- **Wider significance:** Showed that imperial rivalry increased tension but did not automatically produce general war.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** International relations before 1914 were unstable but still negotiable.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Britain, France, Entente Cordiale.

## Spanish-American War (1898)
- **Date:** 1898
- **Background / causes:** US concern over Cuba, navalism, the Maine explosion, and expansionist politics.
- **What happened:** The USA defeated Spain quickly on land and sea.
- **Immediate consequences:** Treaty of Paris transferred major possessions to the USA.
- **Wider significance:** Marked the USA's emergence as an imperial power with global reach.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** The USA was no longer simply isolationist in practice.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** USA, Spain, Treaty of Paris, Platt Amendment, Panama.

## Second Boer War (1899-1902)
- **Date:** 1899-1902
- **Background / causes:** Gold, Uitlander grievances, British ambitions, Boer resistance, and the legacy of the Jameson Raid.
- **What happened:** Britain fought a long war against the Boer republics, including a guerrilla phase and concentration camps.
- **Immediate consequences:** British victory under the Treaty of Vereeniging.
- **Wider significance:** Costly victory damaged Britain's image and highlighted military weakness.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Empire could impose major diplomatic and military costs on the imperial power itself.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Britain, Boers, Kruger, Treaty of Vereeniging, end of splendid isolation.

## Anglo-Japanese Alliance (1902)
- **Date:** 1902
- **Background / causes:** British concern about Russia in East Asia; Japanese search for recognition and security.
- **What happened:** Britain and Japan formed a limited alliance.
- **Immediate consequences:** Japan gained diplomatic status; Britain gained a partner in East Asia.
- **Wider significance:** Connected East Asian and European diplomacy more closely.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Japan's rise was recognised by the existing powers.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Britain, Japan, Russo-Japanese War, First World War in Asia.

## Russo-Japanese War (1904-05)
- **Date:** 1904-05
- **Background / causes:** Rivalry over Korea and Manchuria; Japanese anger after the Triple Intervention.
- **What happened:** Japan attacked Port Arthur and defeated Russia on land and at sea.
- **Immediate consequences:** Treaty of Portsmouth ended the war.
- **Wider significance:** The victory shattered assumptions of automatic European superiority.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Industrial modernisation, not civilisation rhetoric, determined power.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Japan, Russia, USA, Treaty of Portsmouth.

## First Moroccan Crisis / Algeciras (1905-06)
- **Date:** 1905-06
- **Background / causes:** Germany challenged French influence in Morocco and hoped to split Britain from France.
- **What happened:** The crisis moved from the Kaiser's Tangier intervention to the Algeciras conference.
- **Immediate consequences:** Germany failed to isolate France.
- **Wider significance:** Strengthened the Entente and deepened German resentment.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Diplomatic crises hardened alignments before 1914.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Germany, France, Britain, Entente Cordiale.

## Agadir / Second Moroccan Crisis (1911)
- **Date:** 1911
- **Background / causes:** Renewed German challenge to French expansion in Morocco.
- **What happened:** Germany sent the Panther to Agadir.
- **Immediate consequences:** Germany backed down after confrontation.
- **Wider significance:** Intensified British suspicion of Germany and fed pre-war bloc tension.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Repeated diplomatic tests were making the system more brittle.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Germany, France, Britain, pre-1914 tensions.

## USA enters the First World War (1917)
- **Date:** April 1917
- **Background / causes:** Unrestricted submarine warfare, economic ties to the Allies, and the Zimmermann Telegram.
- **What happened:** The USA declared war on Germany.
- **Immediate consequences:** Allied resources and manpower were greatly reinforced.
- **Wider significance:** Confirmed the USA as a decisive world power and shaped the peace settlement.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Economic strength could become political and military power.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Wilson, Germany, Fourteen Points, Paris Peace Conference.

## Paris Peace Conference (1919)
- **Date:** 1919
- **Background / causes:** Allied victory, imperial collapse, and pressure to create a new international order.
- **What happened:** The victors negotiated five major treaties, led mainly by Wilson, Clemenceau, and Lloyd George.
- **Immediate consequences:** New borders, mandates, reparations, and the League Covenant.
- **Wider significance:** The settlement structured the 1920s but left major grievances and unresolved tensions.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Peace-making after total war was a compromise, not a neat application of principle.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Versailles, League, successor states, Turkey, reparations.

## Ruhr occupation and hyperinflation (1923)
- **Date:** 1923
- **Background / causes:** German reparations default and French security fears.
- **What happened:** France and Belgium occupied the Ruhr; German passive resistance helped drive hyperinflation.
- **Immediate consequences:** Economic collapse in Germany and wider diplomatic crisis.
- **Wider significance:** Forced a reworking of reparations and showed the limits of coercion.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Economics and diplomacy were inseparable in the interwar order.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** France, Belgium, Germany, Dawes Plan.

## Corfu Crisis (1923)
- **Date:** 1923
- **Background / causes:** Murder of Italian officials on a boundary mission gave Mussolini a pretext to use force.
- **What happened:** Italy bombarded and occupied Corfu, then resisted League authority.
- **Immediate consequences:** Greece compensated Italy.
- **Wider significance:** Revealed that the League struggled against a major power willing to ignore it.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** League procedure meant little without the will of stronger states.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Italy, Greece, League, Mussolini.

## Treaty of Lausanne (1923)
- **Date:** 24 July 1923
- **Background / causes:** Turkish nationalist resistance overturned the practical basis of Sevres.
- **What happened:** A revised treaty recognised the Republic of Turkey.
- **Immediate consequences:** Turkish sovereignty accepted; Sevres superseded.
- **Wider significance:** Demonstrated that the post-war settlements could be revised successfully.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** The Paris system was less fixed than it appeared.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Turkey, Mustafa Kemal, Allies, Sevres.

## Dawes Plan (1924)
- **Date:** 1924
- **Background / causes:** The Ruhr crisis made the old reparations framework unsustainable.
- **What happened:** Reparations were rescheduled and US loans stabilised Germany.
- **Immediate consequences:** Ruhr evacuation and temporary easing of tensions.
- **Wider significance:** Mid-1920s stability depended heavily on US finance.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Interwar recovery was real but fragile because it rested on credit and political trust.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** USA, Germany, France, Young Plan.

## Locarno Treaties (1925)
- **Date:** 1925
- **Background / causes:** Desire to normalise Franco-German relations after the Ruhr and to stabilise Europe.
- **What happened:** Germany accepted western frontiers and entered a new cooperative framework.
- **Immediate consequences:** Stresemann and Briand gained diplomatic prestige.
- **Wider significance:** Peak of interwar reconciliation in the west, but not the east.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** The 1920s achieved real diplomatic improvement without solving all structural problems.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Stresemann, Briand, Germany, France, League.

## Northern Expedition (1926-28)
- **Date:** 1926-28
- **Background / causes:** KMT-CCP United Front, Soviet aid, and the desire to defeat warlords.
- **What happened:** Chiang led a military advance that captured major cities and expanded Nationalist control.
- **Immediate consequences:** Nanking government established; nominal reunification.
- **Wider significance:** China appeared more unified, but military fragmentation and party conflict remained.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** State-building in China was partial and coercive.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Chiang Kai-shek, KMT, CCP, Shanghai Purge, Nanking Decade.

## Shanghai Purge / White Terror (1927)
- **Date:** 1927
- **Background / causes:** Chiang's fear of communist influence and labour militancy.
- **What happened:** The KMT violently attacked communists and workers in Shanghai and elsewhere.
- **Immediate consequences:** First United Front collapsed; CCP forced into survival mode.
- **Wider significance:** Set the pattern of Nationalist-Communist hostility that shaped later Chinese politics.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Anti-communism was central to Chiang's priorities, even before full war with Japan.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Chiang, CCP, Zhou Enlai, Long March.

## Manchurian Crisis (1931-33)
- **Date:** 1931-33
- **Background / causes:** Japanese army ambition, economic pressure, strategic interests, and weak civilian control.
- **What happened:** Japan seized Manchuria and created Manchukuo after the Mukden Incident.
- **Immediate consequences:** League investigation; Japan rejected the outcome and left the League.
- **Wider significance:** The first decisive collapse of collective security in the 1930s.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Aggression became more likely once the League showed it could not enforce its principles.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Japan, China, League, Lytton Report, Tanggu Truce.

## Long March (1934-35)
- **Date:** 1934-35
- **Background / causes:** KMT encirclement campaigns threatened to destroy the CCP base in Jiangxi.
- **What happened:** The CCP retreated over great distance with huge losses; Mao emerged stronger.
- **Immediate consequences:** CCP survived in Yan'an.
- **Wider significance:** Militarily a retreat, politically a foundational myth and leadership shift.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Survival and narrative control can matter more than immediate victory.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Mao, Chiang, CCP, Yan'an, CCP support growth.

## Abyssinian Crisis (1935-36)
- **Date:** 1935-36
- **Background / causes:** Mussolini wanted prestige, expansion, and a success comparable to Hitler's diplomatic gains.
- **What happened:** Italy invaded Abyssinia; the League imposed limited sanctions; the Hoare-Laval scheme leaked.
- **Immediate consequences:** Italy conquered Abyssinia; sanctions failed; League prestige collapsed.
- **Wider significance:** Often treated as the point at which collective security died as a serious deterrent.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Great-power self-interest overrode the Covenant.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Italy, Britain, France, League, Rome-Berlin Axis.

## Rhineland remilitarisation (1936)
- **Date:** March 1936
- **Background / causes:** Hitler wanted to overturn Versailles and Locarno while exploiting Franco-British caution.
- **What happened:** German troops entered the demilitarised Rhineland.
- **Immediate consequences:** No armed response; Hitler gained prestige and security.
- **Wider significance:** One of the most important low-risk gambles of the decade.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Deterrence failed because the defending powers would not act at the decisive moment.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Germany, France, Britain, Locarno, appeasement.

## Xi'an Incident (1936)
- **Date:** December 1936
- **Background / causes:** Frustration with Chiang's priority of fighting communists rather than Japan.
- **What happened:** Chiang was detained by his own side and pressured into cooperation against Japan.
- **Immediate consequences:** Second United Front formed.
- **Wider significance:** Reordered Chinese priorities on the eve of wider war with Japan.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Chinese politics in the 1930s cannot be reduced to a simple KMT-versus-Japan story.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Chiang, Zhang Xueliang, CCP, Second United Front.

## Spanish Civil War (1936-39)
- **Date:** 1936-39
- **Background / causes:** Deep political polarisation, army revolt, and the crisis of the Spanish Republic.
- **What happened:** Civil war drew in Germany, Italy, the USSR, and international volunteers while Britain and France followed non-intervention.
- **Immediate consequences:** Franco won in 1939.
- **Wider significance:** It exposed ideological division and the limits of the democracies' response to fascist intervention.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Non-intervention often worked in favour of the side already receiving effective outside aid.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Franco, Germany, Italy, USSR, Britain, France.

## Full-scale Sino-Japanese War (1937)
- **Date:** 1937 onward
- **Background / causes:** Earlier Japanese expansion, Chinese weakness, and unresolved conflict after Manchuria.
- **What happened:** War expanded across China; major cities fell; atrocities followed.
- **Immediate consequences:** KMT retreated inland; prolonged war began.
- **Wider significance:** East Asia became a central theatre of the wider international crisis.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Japanese aggression was no longer limited or reversible by diplomacy.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Japan, KMT, CCP, Nanjing Massacre, Pearl Harbor.

## Anschluss (1938)
- **Date:** March 1938
- **Background / causes:** Hitler's long-term aim of union with Austria and the weakening of opposition after Abyssinia and Rhineland.
- **What happened:** Germany absorbed Austria.
- **Immediate consequences:** Germany gained resources, manpower, and a stronger strategic position.
- **Wider significance:** Made Czechoslovakia more vulnerable and showed appeasement had not moderated Hitler.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Earlier diplomatic concessions encouraged more ambitious revisionism.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Germany, Austria, Mussolini, Munich.

## Munich Agreement (1938)
- **Date:** 29 September 1938
- **Background / causes:** Sudeten crisis, fear of war, and British-French commitment to appeasement.
- **What happened:** Britain and France accepted German acquisition of the Sudetenland without Czech participation.
- **Immediate consequences:** Czechoslovakia lost key defences and territory.
- **Wider significance:** Postponed war briefly but weakened the anti-German position and discredited guarantees.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Appeasement solved the immediate crisis but worsened the strategic balance.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Chamberlain, Hitler, France, Italy, Czechoslovakia, March 1939 occupation.

## Occupation of Czechoslovakia (March 1939)
- **Date:** March 1939
- **Background / causes:** Hitler used Slovak instability and pressure on Prague after Munich.
- **What happened:** Germany occupied the rest of the Czech lands.
- **Immediate consequences:** The pretence of limited ethnic revisionism collapsed.
- **Wider significance:** A decisive shift in British perception; appeasement lost credibility.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Hitler's aims were wider than revising Versailles for Germans alone.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Germany, Czechoslovakia, Poland guarantee.

## Nazi-Soviet Pact (1939)
- **Date:** 24 August 1939
- **Background / causes:** Failed Anglo-Franco-Soviet talks and Hitler's need to avoid a two-front war.
- **What happened:** Germany and the USSR agreed non-aggression and secret spheres of influence.
- **Immediate consequences:** Poland was diplomatically isolated.
- **Wider significance:** Cleared the immediate path to war and shocked Europe ideologically.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Strategic interest outweighed ideological hostility when the stakes were high enough.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Hitler, Stalin, invasion of Poland.

## Invasion of Poland (1939)
- **Date:** September 1939
- **Background / causes:** Hitler's demands over Danzig and the corridor; pact with the USSR; failed deterrence.
- **What happened:** Germany invaded Poland; Britain and France declared war; the USSR later invaded from the east.
- **Immediate consequences:** European war began.
- **Wider significance:** Final proof that the interwar diplomatic order had collapsed.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** The road to war was cumulative - not the result of one single mistake.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** Britain, France, Germany, Poland, USSR, Nazi-Soviet Pact.

## Pearl Harbor (1941)
- **Date:** 7 December 1941
- **Background / causes:** Japanese expansion, US embargo pressure, and Japanese belief in a pre-emptive strike.
- **What happened:** Japan attacked the US Pacific fleet at Pearl Harbor.
- **Immediate consequences:** The USA entered the war; Germany declared war on the USA.
- **Wider significance:** Turned the regional Asian conflict into a fully global war struggle.
- **Which arguments this event helps prove:** Miscalculation, resource pressure, and strategic overreach drove Japan into a war it could not sustain.
- **Related actors / treaties / later developments:** USA, Japan, oil embargo, Pacific war.
