# 05 — Cause-Consequence Chains

## Format
`cause -> policy/event -> immediate effect -> political consequence -> longer-term significance`

---

## Chain 1: Industrialisation to WWI

```
Industrialisation (post-1850s)
  -> overproduction of manufactured goods
    -> Long Depression (1873-96): falling prices, shrinking profits
      -> desperate need for new markets + raw materials
        -> New Imperialism: European powers seize overseas territory
          -> Scramble for Africa (1880s-1900s): competitive land-grabs
            -> colonial rivalries sharpen (Fashoda 1898, Morocco 1905/1911)
              -> alliance systems harden: Triple Alliance vs Triple Entente
                -> arms race + mutual suspicion
                  -> WWI (1914): alliances drag all powers into Balkan crisis
```

**Key link**: Economic competition drove imperial expansion; imperial expansion drove diplomatic hostility; diplomatic hostility created the alliance blocs that turned a regional war into a world war.

---

## Chain 2: Franco-Prussian War to WWII

```
Franco-Prussian War (1870-71)
  -> French humiliation: loss of Alsace-Lorraine + 5 billion franc indemnity
    -> French desire for revanche (revenge) becomes permanent political force
      -> France demands harsh terms at Versailles (1919): Clemenceau insists on
         maximum punishment
        -> War Guilt Clause (231) + £6.6B reparations + territorial losses
          -> German resentment: "stab in the back" myth; Weimar labelled
             "November criminals"
            -> Hitler exploits grievance: Versailles destruction = core promise
              -> Rearmament -> Rhineland -> Anschluss -> Munich -> war (1939)
                -> WWII: French desire for revenge in 1871 creates German desire
                   for revenge in 1919, which produces WWII
```

**Key link**: The cycle of humiliation—France humiliated in 1871, Germany humiliated in 1919—shows how punitive peace settlements generate the next war rather than preventing it.

## Chain 3: Meiji Restoration to Pearl Harbor

```
Meiji Restoration (1868)
  -> rapid modernisation: Western technology + conscript army + industrial base
    -> military build-up: modern navy + army modelled on Prussia
      -> victory over China (First Sino-Japanese War, 1894-95): gains Taiwan,
         influence over Korea
        -> Triple Intervention (1895): Russia/France/Germany force Japan to
           return Liaodong Peninsula
          -> national humiliation -> massive naval expansion programme
            -> victory over Russia (1904-05): destroys Baltic Fleet at Tsushima
              -> Treaty of Portsmouth (1905): Japan = recognised world power
                -> WWI exploitation: seizes German Pacific colonies + issues
                   Twenty-One Demands to China (1915)
                  -> Washington Conference (1922): Japan forced to accept inferior
                     naval ratio (5:5:3) -> resentment of Western double standards
                    -> Great Depression hits export-dependent economy
                      -> Manchuria invasion (1931): Kwantung Army acts alone
                        -> League condemnation -> Japan leaves League (1933)
                          -> military dictatorship: PM assassinated (1932);
                             army dominates cabinet
                            -> full invasion of China (1937) -> Anti-Comintern
                               Pact -> Tripartite Pact
                              -> US oil embargo (1941) -> Pearl Harbor (Dec 1941)
```

**Key link**: Japan's trajectory from modernisation to militarism was driven by a recurring pattern: achieving parity with Western powers, being denied equal treatment, and responding with aggressive nationalism.

## Chain 4: Treaty of Versailles to WWII

```
Treaty of Versailles (June 1919)
  -> War Guilt Clause (Art. 231) + reparations set at £6.6 billion (1921)
    -> German economic crisis: government prints money to pay
      -> Hyperinflation (1923): mark collapses (4.2 trillion marks = $1)
        -> Dawes Plan (1924): restructures annual payments; US loans flow in
          -> "Golden Age" of Weimar (1924-29): apparent stability, but dependent
             on American capital
            -> Wall Street Crash (October 1929): US loans recalled
              -> German economy collapses: 6 million unemployed by 1932
                -> Political extremism: Nazi vote rises from 2.6% (1928) to
                   37.3% (July 1932)
                  -> Hitler appointed Chancellor (January 30, 1933)
                    -> Systematic destruction of Versailles: rearmament (1935),
                       Rhineland (1936), Anschluss (1938), Sudetenland (1938)
                      -> Invasion of Poland (September 1, 1939) -> WWII
```

**Key link**: Versailles created resentment; Dawes masked it with borrowed prosperity; the Crash exposed the fragility; Hitler exploited the result. The chain from treaty to war ran through economics at every stage.

## Chain 5: US Isolationism to League Failure

```
US isolationism (deep tradition: Washington's Farewell Address, Monroe Doctrine)
  -> Senate rejects Treaty of Versailles (November 1919; March 1920):
     Lodge reservations + irreconcilable opposition
    -> USA never joins League of Nations
      -> League lacks world's largest economy + most powerful navy
        -> League credibility fatally undermined from day one
          -> France feels abandoned: Anglo-American security guarantee collapses
            -> France pursues hard-line security policy: insists on strict
               reparations enforcement
              -> Ruhr occupation (January 1923): French/Belgian troops seize
                 industrial heartland
                -> Anglo-French relations deteriorate: Britain condemns occupation
                  -> International system fractured: no unified response to
                     revisionism possible
                    -> League unable to enforce decisions in 1930s because it
                       never had the collective power to do so
```

**Key link**: US absence was the League's original sin. Without America, France could not feel secure; insecure France destabilised Europe; a fractured Europe made collective security impossible.

## Chain 6: Wall Street Crash to WWII

```
Wall Street Crash (October 24-29, 1929)
  -> Great Depression: global trade falls 65%; industrial production collapses
    -> Mass unemployment: USA 13M; Germany 6M; Britain 3M
      -> Political extremism flourishes worldwide
        -> Germany: Nazi vote surges; Hitler appointed Chancellor (Jan 1933)
        -> Japan: Depression destroys silk/export economy; army offers
           expansion as solution
          -> Aggressive expansion begins:
             Japan invades Manchuria (1931); Hitler rearms (1935)
            -> League fails over Manchuria (1931-33): Lytton Report ignored;
               Japan leaves League
              -> League fails over Abyssinia (1935-36): sanctions exclude oil;
                 Hoare-Laval Pact exposed
                -> Collective security discredited
                  -> Appeasement becomes default Western policy
                    -> Hitler takes Rhineland (1936) -> Austria (1938) ->
                       Sudetenland (1938) -> Czechoslovakia (1939)
                      -> WWII (September 1939)
```

**Key link**: The Crash did not just cause economic misery—it destroyed the entire international framework (Dawes/Young Plans, Locarno spirit, League authority) that had maintained fragile peace since 1924.

## Chain 7: Bolshevik Revolution to WWII

```
Bolshevik Revolution (October 1917)
  -> Russia exits WWI (Brest-Litovsk, March 1918); publishes secret treaties
    -> Western fear of communism intensifies; Allied intervention (1918-20)
      -> Russia excluded from Paris Peace Conference (1919)
        -> Soviet isolation -> Treaty of Rapallo with Germany (1922):
           mutual recognition + secret military cooperation
          -> Western distrust of USSR deepens through 1920s-30s
            -> Britain/France refuse genuine alliance with Stalin (1939):
               Anglo-Franco-Soviet talks stall over Polish refusal to allow
               Soviet troops passage
              -> Stalin pivots: Nazi-Soviet Pact (August 23, 1939)
                -> Hitler's eastern flank secured; Poland surrounded
                  -> Invasion of Poland (September 1, 1939) -> WWII
```

**Key link**: Western fear of communism consistently overrode strategic logic. Excluding Russia from peacemaking, tolerating Hitler as anti-communist bulwark, and failing to ally with Stalin all contributed to the conditions for WWII.

## Chain 8: Appeasement Logic to WWII

```
WWI trauma (750,000 British dead; 1.3M French dead)
  -> overwhelming anti-war public sentiment (Peace Ballot 1935: 11M votes
     for collective security; Oxford Union "King and Country" debate 1933)
  + military unpreparedness (Ten Year Rule; defence cuts through 1920s-30s)
  + Depression economics (cannot afford rearmament)
  + fear of communism (Hitler seen as anti-communist buffer)
  + genuine belief Versailles was unjust (Keynes: "Carthaginian peace")
    -> appeasement policy: concessions to satisfy "legitimate" grievances
      -> Rhineland remilitarised (March 1936): no response
        -> Anschluss with Austria (March 1938): no response
          -> Munich Conference (September 1938): Sudetenland ceded to Germany
            -> Hitler emboldened: each concession proves democracies will not fight
              -> Czechoslovakia seized (March 1939): not a German area—appeasement
                 logic collapses
                -> British guarantee to Poland (March 31, 1939)
                  -> Nazi-Soviet Pact (August 23, 1939): removes last obstacle
                    -> Invasion of Poland (September 1, 1939) -> WWII
```

**Key link**: Appeasement was not irrational—it reflected real constraints. But it rested on a fatal assumption: that Hitler had limited, negotiable aims. Once Prague proved otherwise, the policy reversed—but too late.

## Chain 9: China — Qing Collapse to Japanese Invasion

```
Qing Dynasty weakness (internal decay + Opium Wars + unequal treaties)
  -> foreign exploitation: treaty ports, extraterritoriality, spheres of influence
    -> Republic of China proclaimed (January 1912): Sun Yat-sen president briefly
      -> Yuan Shikai seizes power; attempts to become emperor (1915); dies (1916)
        -> Warlord era (1916-28): China fragmented; central authority collapses
          -> First United Front (1923): KMT + CCP cooperate under Soviet advice
            -> Northern Expedition (1926-28): Chiang Kai-shek leads KMT army north
              -> Shanghai Massacre (April 1927): Chiang purges CCP; thousands killed
                -> Chinese Civil War begins: KMT vs CCP
                  -> Encirclement Campaigns (1930-34): KMT surrounds CCP base areas
                    -> Long March (1934-35): CCP retreats 6,000 miles; ~90% casualties;
                       Mao becomes undisputed leader
                      -> Xi'an Incident (December 1936): Zhang Xueliang kidnaps Chiang;
                         forces KMT-CCP alliance against Japan
                        -> Second United Front (1936-45): nominal KMT-CCP cooperation
                          -> Japan's full invasion of China (July 1937): Marco Polo Bridge
                            -> KMT failures: corruption, conscription brutality, inflation
                              -> CCP gains rural support: land reform, guerrilla warfare,
                                 patriotic resistance image
```

**Key link**: China's trajectory was shaped by the interaction between internal weakness and external aggression. The CCP survived not through ideology but through the KMT's failures and Japan's invasion, which gave Mao time and legitimacy.

## Chain 10: Japan's Democracy Failure to Pearl Harbor

```
Post-WWI economic boom ends (1920s recession; 1923 earthquake devastation)
  -> Washington Conference (1922): Japan forced to accept 5:5:3 naval ratio
    -> public resentment: Japan treated as inferior despite WWI contribution
      -> Great Depression (1929-): devastating for export-dependent economy;
         silk prices collapse; rural poverty
        -> mass unemployment + social unrest + political corruption exposed
          -> Manchuria invasion (September 1931): Kwantung Army acts without
             government authorisation
            -> government unable to control military; PM Inukai assassinated
               (May 1932) for criticising army
              -> military dominates cabinet: effective dictatorship by mid-1930s
                -> full invasion of China (1937)
                  -> Anti-Comintern Pact (1936) -> Tripartite Pact (1940)
                    -> US imposes oil embargo + asset freeze (July 1941)
                      -> Japan calculates: fight now or economic strangulation
                        -> Pearl Harbor (December 7, 1941)
```

**Key link**: Japanese democracy did not fall to a single coup but eroded through economic failure, military insubordination, political assassination, and public disillusionment with Western-style liberalism. The military offered expansion as the answer to Depression—and the civilian government could not resist.
