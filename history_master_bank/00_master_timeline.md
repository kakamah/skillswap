# 00 Master Timeline

Source basis: uploaded markdown extraction of Cambridge International AS Level History: International History, 1870-1945. Page references are only included where the extraction made them reasonably clear. Any OCR damage, internal inconsistency, or weakly supported detail is flagged rather than normalised.


Strict chronological sequence across the whole book. The focus is on major turning points, settlements, wars, diplomatic crises, and power shifts.

| Date / year | Event name | 1-2 sentence description | Key actors | Immediate consequence | Why it matters | Linked topic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1870-71 | Franco-Prussian War | French defeat and German victory transformed the European balance of power. France lost prestige; Germany emerged unified and stronger. | France, Prussia / Germany, Bismarck | German unification; French resentment | Created the post-1871 context for rivalry, nationalism, and later French security fears | Empire / European balance |
| 1871 | German unification | A new industrial and military power appeared in central Europe. Bismarck prioritised security in Europe over colonial adventure. | Germany, Bismarck | Stronger German state | Changed the power structure of Europe and shaped later alliance politics | Empire / European balance |
| 1873-96 | Long Depression | Prolonged economic difficulty encouraged tariff protection, export drives, and arguments for colonial markets and raw materials. | European industrial states | Harder trading conditions | Helped make empire look economically useful to many policymakers | Imperialism |
| 1882 | British occupation of Egypt | Britain took control of Egyptian administration to secure the Suez Canal route. | Britain, Egypt | British strategic control of Egypt | Often treated in the book as a key trigger for the wider African scramble | Imperialism / IR |
| 1884-85 | Berlin Conference / Treaty of Berlin | European powers agreed rules for African partition: notification, effective occupation, negotiated disputes, and free navigation in parts of central Africa. | Britain, France, Germany, Belgium, Portugal, other powers | Partition rules formalised | Reduced the risk of immediate European war in Africa but accelerated the land grab itself | Imperialism / Africa |
| 1894-95 | First Sino-Japanese War | Japan defeated China and exposed Qing weakness. | Japan, China | Chinese defeat; treaty imposed | Marked Japan's rise and deepened the struggle over Korea and Manchuria | Japan / China |
| 1895 | Treaty of Shimonoseki | China accepted Japanese gains, including recognition of Korean independence and the cession of territories. | Japan, China | Japanese diplomatic victory | Confirmed Japan as a serious regional power | Japan / China / Treaties |
| 1895 | Triple Intervention | Russia, France, and Germany forced Japan to scale back gains from Shimonoseki. | Russia, France, Germany, Japan | Japanese humiliation | Fuelled long-term Japanese resentment and helped prepare the road to war with Russia | Japan / IR |
| 1898 | Fashoda Incident | British and French imperial expansion collided in Sudan, but both sides backed away from war. | Britain, France | Diplomatic compromise | Showed that colonial rivalry could sharpen tensions yet still be contained | Imperialism / IR |
| 1898 | Spanish-American War | The USA defeated Spain and acquired overseas possessions. | USA, Spain | Treaty of Paris settlement | Marked the USA's emergence as an imperial and naval power | USA emergence |
| 1899 | Open Door policy | The USA called for equal commercial access in China rather than exclusive colonial control. | USA, China, European powers, Japan | Formalised a US position on China | Showed that the USA was now shaping global diplomacy, not just continental affairs | USA / China |
| 1899-1902 | Second Boer War | Britain fought the Boer republics in a long, costly conflict involving guerrilla war and concentration camps. | Britain, Boers | British victory; reputational damage | Exposed British military and diplomatic weaknesses and contributed to the end of "splendid isolation" | Imperialism / IR |
| 1902 | Anglo-Japanese Alliance | Britain and Japan reached an agreement against threats in East Asia. | Britain, Japan | Japan gained recognition; Britain gained regional support | Elevated Japan diplomatically and linked European and Asian power politics | Japan / Treaties |
| 1904 | Entente Cordiale | Britain and France settled imperial disputes and improved relations. | Britain, France | Reduced Anglo-French colonial friction | Helped realign Europe against a more assertive Germany | IR / Morocco |
| 1904-05 | Russo-Japanese War | Japan launched war against Russia and won major land and naval victories. | Japan, Russia | Russian defeat; peace settlement | First major defeat of a European great power by an Asian state in the modern era | Japan emergence |
| 1905 | Treaty of Portsmouth | Roosevelt mediated peace between Russia and Japan. | Japan, Russia, USA | War ended; Japanese gains recognised | Confirmed Japan's new status and showed growing US diplomatic influence | Japan / USA / Treaties |
| 1905-06 | First Moroccan Crisis / Algeciras | Germany challenged French influence in Morocco but failed to break the Entente. | Germany, France, Britain | Diplomatic setback for Germany | Strengthened Anglo-French cooperation and intensified bloc politics | IR / 1900s tensions |
| 1911 | Agadir / Second Moroccan Crisis | Germany sent the Panther to Agadir to challenge France again. | Germany, France, Britain | German climbdown | Further hardened suspicion of Germany before 1914 | IR / 1900s tensions |
| 1911 | Chinese Revolution | Qing rule collapsed through revolution, opening the way to the end of the dynasty and the creation of a republic. | Sun Yat-sen, Yuan Shih-kai, revolutionaries | Monarchy fatally weakened | Opened the era of weak republican rule, rivalry, and warlordism | China |
| Jan-Mar 1912 | Republic of China established | Sun formally announced the republic in January 1912; after Pu Yi's abdication, Yuan became president in March. | Sun Yat-sen, Yuan Shih-kai | Formal republican state created under fragile leadership | The fall of empire did not solve China's political fragmentation | China |
| 1914 | Assassination at Sarajevo and outbreak of First World War | The July Crisis turned a Balkan assassination into a European and then global war. | Austria-Hungary, Serbia, Germany, Russia, France, Britain | General war in Europe | War destroyed the old order and set up the peace settlement crisis of 1919-20 | War origins |
| 1914 | Japan enters the First World War | Japan joined the war against Germany and seized German possessions in East Asia. | Japan, Germany, Britain | Japanese wartime gains | Expanded Japanese influence in China and the Pacific | Japan / War |
| 1915 | Twenty-One Demands | Japan pressured China for extensive privileges and influence. | Japan, China, Yuan Shih-kai | Chinese humiliation | Deepened Chinese nationalism and suspicion of Japan | China / Japan |
| 1916 | Death of Yuan Shih-kai; warlord era intensifies | Yuan's collapse removed the strongest central figure and accelerated fragmentation. | Yuan Shih-kai, warlords | Competing regional militarists dominated | The weakness of central authority became a core problem in Chinese politics | China |
| Jan 1918 | Wilson's Fourteen Points | Wilson set out principles including open diplomacy, self-determination, and a League of Nations. | USA, Wilson | New peace programme announced | Became a benchmark against which the 1919 settlement was judged | Peace settlement |
| Nov 1918 | German collapse and armistice | Revolution and military defeat forced Germany to seek peace. | Germany, Allies | War ended | Opened the way for the Paris Peace Conference | Peace settlement |
| 1919 | Paris Peace Conference opens | The victorious powers designed the post-war settlement. | Wilson, Clemenceau, Lloyd George, Orlando | Draft treaties prepared | Created the legal and political framework of the 1920s - and many later grievances | Peace settlement |
| 1919 | Rejection of Japan's racial equality proposal at Paris | Japan sought recognition of racial equality in the League Covenant, but Wilson insisted such a change needed unanimity and the proposal failed. | Japan, Wilson, Britain, USA, Paris delegates | Japanese resentment deepened | Helped turn status grievance into a lasting issue in Japan's relations with western powers | Japan / Paris settlement |
| 1919 | May Fourth Movement | Chinese protest against the Shantung settlement broadened into a wider nationalist and intellectual movement. | Chinese students, workers, Beijing government | Demonstrations and strikes | A major ideological turning point in modern China | China |
| 1919 | Treaty of Versailles | Germany lost territory, accepted military limits, and faced reparations under the new settlement. | Germany, Allies | Treaty imposed on Germany | Central to revisionism, Weimar resentment, and later Nazi propaganda | Peace settlement / 1920s |
| 1920 onward | League of Nations begins operating | The League started functioning as the new machinery of collective security and arbitration in the early 1920s. | League members | Geneva institutions established | The decade's diplomacy cannot be understood without its formal role and its practical weaknesses | League / 1920s |
| 1920 | Treaty of Sevres | A harsh settlement was imposed on the Ottoman Empire. | Allies, Ottoman Empire | Turkish resistance intensified | Became the clearest case of a post-war treaty later overturned | Peace settlement / Turkey |
| 1921 | Reparations fixed for Germany | The Reparations Commission fixed a very high total for Germany. | Germany, France, Britain, Reparations Commission | German resentment deepened | Helped produce the Ruhr crisis, hyperinflation, and the search for a new settlement | 1920s tensions |
| 1921 | CCP founded | The Chinese Communist Party emerged as a small but ideologically important force. | CCP founders, Comintern | Organised communist movement created | Opened the KMT-CCP struggle that shaped China to 1945 | China |
| 1921-22 | Washington Conference | Naval and Pacific agreements attempted to calm tensions in East Asia. | USA, Britain, Japan, France, Italy, China | Naval limits and Pacific arrangements agreed | Showed mid-1920s diplomacy could ease tensions, but without strong enforcement | 1920s IR / Japan |
| 1922 | Mussolini becomes prime minister | Fascism took power in Italy. | Mussolini, Italy | Authoritarian rule strengthened | Extremism was no longer a marginal force in Europe | 1920s / 1930s extremism |
| 1922 | Treaty of Rapallo | Germany and Soviet Russia rebuilt relations outside the Paris system. | Germany, USSR | Diplomatic and economic cooperation | Signalled that excluded states would seek alternatives to the Versailles order | 1920s tensions |
| 1923 | Ruhr occupation | France and Belgium occupied the Ruhr when Germany defaulted on reparations deliveries. | France, Belgium, Germany | Passive resistance and crisis | The clearest proof that reparations, security, and economic collapse were tightly linked | 1920s tensions |
| 1923 | German hyperinflation peaks | The mark collapsed amid passive resistance and state financing. | Germany | Economic and social chaos | Destroyed confidence in the settlement and made reparations reform unavoidable | 1920s tensions |
| 1923 | Corfu Crisis | Mussolini used force against Greece and challenged League authority. | Italy, Greece, League | Greece compensated Italy; League sidelined | Exposed the League's weakness against a determined major power | League / 1920s |
| 1923 | Treaty of Lausanne | A revised settlement recognised the Republic of Turkey. | Turkey, Allies | Sevres effectively replaced | Proved that the 1919-20 settlements were not all permanent | Peace settlement / Turkey |
| 1923 | Northern Expedition preparations deepen with Soviet aid | The KMT and CCP worked inside the First United Front. | Sun Yat-sen, KMT, CCP, USSR | Revolutionary coalition strengthened | Set up the military drive against warlords and the later split | China |
| 1924 | Japanese Exclusion Act in the USA | The USA effectively ended further Japanese immigration, deepening Japanese anger at western double standards. | USA, Japan | Mass protests in Japan | Strengthened anti-western opinion and weakened support for cooperative diplomacy | Japan / 1920s diplomacy |
| 1924 | Dawes Plan | Reparations were rescheduled, the Ruhr was evacuated, and US money flowed into Germany. | USA, Germany, France, Britain | Immediate easing of the reparations crisis | Mid-1920s recovery rested heavily on US finance | 1920s rapprochement |
| 1925 | Locarno Treaties | Germany, France, and Belgium accepted western frontiers under a broader diplomatic package. | Stresemann, Briand, Britain, Italy, Germany, France, Belgium | Tensions eased in western Europe | High point of reconciliation, but the east remained less secure | 1920s rapprochement |
| 1925 | Sun Yat-sen dies | Leadership passed from Sun's era to a sharper KMT power struggle. | Sun Yat-sen, KMT | Chiang rose further | Made the future of the revolution more authoritarian and militarised | China |
| 1926 | Germany joins League; Treaty of Berlin | Germany entered the League but also renewed ties with the USSR. | Germany, League, USSR | Germany partly rehabilitated | Stresemann balanced revisionism with diplomatic respectability | 1920s rapprochement |
| 1926 | Northern Expedition begins | Chiang led the KMT advance against warlords. | Chiang Kai-shek, KMT, CCP, warlords | Major military gains in south and central China | Began the process of nominal reunification | China |
| 1927 | Shanghai Purge / White Terror | Chiang violently broke with the communists and crushed labour radicalism in Shanghai and beyond. | Chiang Kai-shek, KMT, CCP, Green Gang | First United Front shattered | Opened the long KMT-CCP civil conflict | China |
| 1928 | KMT captures key northern cities | The Nationalists achieved formal reunification under the Nanking government. | Chiang Kai-shek, KMT | Nanking regime recognised | China looked more unified, but regional and military fragmentation remained | China |
| 1928 | Kellogg-Briand Pact signed | States renounced war in principle as an instrument of policy. | USA, France, many powers | Broad diplomatic endorsement | Showed idealism at its height, but without enforcement power | 1920s rapprochement |
| 1929 | Young Plan | Reparations were reduced further and some external controls were removed. | Germany, USA, France, Britain | More moderate reparations framework | Acknowledged earlier reparations levels were unrealistic | 1920s rapprochement |
| Oct 1929 | Wall Street Crash | The US economic crash spread internationally and broke the financial foundations of 1920s stability. | USA, global economy | Credit contraction and world depression | The Depression radicalised politics and weakened collective security | 1930s crisis |
| 1930 | Central Plains War | Chiang defeated major military rivals inside China. | Chiang Kai-shek, rival warlords | Heavy KMT victory but high cost | Showed that Nationalist unity still depended on force, not settled legitimacy | China |
| 1931 | Manchurian Crisis / Mukden Incident | Japanese forces seized Manchuria after a staged railway incident. | Japan, China, League | Manchukuo created | A decisive test the League failed, encouraging future aggression | League failure / Japan |
| May 1932 | May 15 Incident | Prime Minister Inukai Tsuyoshi was assassinated by young naval officers after criticising the Manchurian action. | Inukai Tsuyoshi, Japanese naval officers, Japanese military | Party government lost further authority | Marked a decisive step in the collapse of Japanese parliamentary democracy | Japan / military rule |
| 1933 | Hitler becomes chancellor | Nazi rule began in Germany after the Depression-driven collapse of Weimar politics. | Hitler, Nazi Party, Germany | One-party dictatorship developed | Turned German revisionism into a far more dangerous project | 1930s extremism |
| 1933 | Germany leaves Disarmament Conference and League | Hitler rejected the existing security framework. | Germany, League | Germany freed itself from diplomatic restraint | Collective security lost one of the key states it needed to contain | League failure |
| 1933 | Tanggu Truce | The Nationalists accepted a settlement that left Japan dominant in Manchuria and stronger in north China. | China, Japan | Japan's position consolidated | Deepened criticism of Chiang's anti-Japanese caution | China / Japan |
| 1934-35 | Long March | The CCP retreated from Jiangxi and survived through a punishing strategic withdrawal. | CCP, Mao, Chiang Kai-shek | CCP survived in Yan'an | Crucial for Mao's rise and the myth-making of communist survival | China |
| 1935 | Saar plebiscite and German rearmament | Germany regained the Saar and openly expanded its armed forces. | Germany, Hitler | Versailles military restrictions eroded | Showed revisionism was becoming action, not just rhetoric | 1930s extremism |
| Apr 1935 | Stresa Front | Britain, France, and Italy declared opposition to treaty revision by force. | Britain, France, Italy | Temporary anti-German front | The front was short-lived and failed to stop Hitler | 1930s diplomacy |
| Jun 1935 | Anglo-German Naval Agreement | Britain accepted a bilateral naval arrangement with Germany. | Britain, Germany | Stresa weakened | A key example of appeasement and disunity among the powers opposing Hitler | Appeasement |
| 1935-36 | Abyssinian Crisis | Italy invaded Abyssinia and the League imposed limited sanctions. | Italy, Abyssinia, League, Britain, France | Sanctions failed; Italy conquered Abyssinia | Destroyed confidence in collective security and pushed Italy closer to Germany | League failure / Appeasement |
| Mar 1936 | Rhineland remilitarised | German troops re-entered the demilitarised Rhineland in violation of the treaties. | Germany, France, Britain | No military response from France or Britain | A major strategic and psychological turning point in Hitler's favour | Appeasement / War road |
| Dec 1936 | Xi'an Incident and Second United Front agreement | Chiang was detained in the Xi'an Incident; by 24 December he agreed to halt campaigns against the CCP and cooperate against Japan. | Chiang Kai-shek, Zhang Xueliang, CCP | Second United Front agreed | Shifted the Chinese conflict toward resistance to Japan | China |
| 1936-39 | Spanish Civil War | Spain became a brutal ideological and military conflict with foreign intervention. | Franco, Republicans, Germany, Italy, USSR, Britain, France | Nationalist victory | Exposed the weakness of non-intervention and previewed wider European war | 1930s extremism |
| 1936-37 | Anti-Comintern alignment expands | Germany, Japan, and later Italy moved closer in anti-Soviet ideological and strategic cooperation. | Germany, Japan, Italy | Axis ties strengthened | Helped reshape the wider diplomatic map of the late 1930s | 1930s extremism |
| Jul 1937 | Full-scale Sino-Japanese War begins | Fighting widened from earlier Japanese expansion into a prolonged war across China. | Japan, China, KMT, CCP | Major Chinese cities fell; war widened | Made East Asia a central front in the wider crisis of the 1930s | China / Japan |
| Dec 1937 | Nanjing Massacre | Japanese forces committed mass killing and terror after taking Nanjing. | Japan, Chinese civilians | International outrage; deep Chinese trauma | Hardened Chinese resistance and exposed the character of Japanese expansion | China / Japan |
| Mar 1938 | Anschluss | Germany absorbed Austria. | Germany, Austria | Austria incorporated into the Reich | Expanded German power and encircled Czechoslovakia more tightly | Appeasement / War road |
| Sep 1938 | Munich Agreement | Britain and France accepted the transfer of the Sudetenland to Germany without Czech participation. | Germany, Britain, France, Italy, Czechoslovakia | Sudetenland ceded | Peak appeasement: it postponed war briefly but weakened the anti-German front further | Appeasement |
| Mar 1939 | German occupation of the rest of Czechoslovakia | Hitler broke the pretence that he only wanted national self-determination for Germans. | Germany, Czechoslovakia | Czech state destroyed | Convinced many in Britain that appeasement had failed | War road |
| Aug 1939 | Nazi-Soviet Pact | Germany and the USSR agreed non-aggression and secretly divided spheres of influence in eastern Europe. | Germany, USSR, Hitler, Stalin | Germany avoided a two-front war at the start | Cleared the immediate path to the invasion of Poland | War road |
| Sep 1939 | Invasion of Poland and outbreak of European war | Germany attacked Poland; Britain and France declared war. | Germany, Poland, Britain, France, USSR | Second World War began in Europe | Ended appeasement and confirmed the collapse of the interwar order | War road |
| Jul 1941 | US oil embargo on Japan | The USA ended oil exports to Japan, sharply worsening Japan's strategic dilemma over resources and expansion. | USA, Japan | Pressure on Japanese leaders intensified | Helped push the crisis toward war in the Pacific | Japan / World war |
| 7 Dec 1941 | Pearl Harbor | Japan launched a surprise attack on the US Pacific Fleet base at Pearl Harbor. | Japan, USA, Tojo, Roosevelt | USA entered the war; Germany declared war on the USA | Turned the Asian and European crises into a fully global war | Japan / World war |
| 1945 | Japanese defeat and withdrawal from China | Japan's war ended with military collapse and surrender. | Japan, China, USA, Allies | Japanese occupation ended | Closed one phase of the East Asian crisis but left China facing renewed civil war | China / Japan |
