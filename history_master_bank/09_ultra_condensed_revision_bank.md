## Key treaties / agreements
- **Berlin (1885):** Rules for partition, not restraint of empire.
- **Shimonoseki (1895):** Confirms Japanese victory over China.
- **Portsmouth (1905):** Ends Russo-Japanese War; boosts Japan.
- **Treaty of Paris (1898):** USA gains overseas possessions.
- **Platt Amendment / 1903 treaty:** Cuba controlled indirectly by USA.
- **Anglo-Japanese Alliance (1902):** Britain backs Japan's status.
- **Entente Cordiale (1904):** Anglo-French colonial settlement.
- **Versailles (1919):** German losses, limits, reparations framework.
- **Saint-Germain / Neuilly / Trianon / Sevres:** Other defeated powers also resent settlement.
- **Lausanne (1923):** Turkey overturns Sevres.
- **Washington settlement (1921-22):** Naval ratios and Pacific compromise.
- **Rapallo (1922):** Germany and USSR rebuild ties.
- **Dawes Plan (1924):** Reparations rescheduled.
- **Locarno (1925):** Western borders stabilised more than eastern ones.
- **Treaty of Berlin (1926):** Germany keeps USSR option open.
- **Kellogg-Briand (1928):** War renounced in principle only.
- **Young Plan (1929):** Reparations reduced again.
- **Stresa (1935):** Anti-German front that quickly weakened.
- **Anglo-German Naval Agreement (1935):** Britain accepts German naval growth.
- **Munich (1938):** Sudetenland transferred to Germany.
- **Nazi-Soviet Pact (1939):** Non-aggression + secret spheres.

## Key actors
- **Britain:** Empire defender; later appeaser under pressure.
- **France:** Security seeker against Germany.
- **Germany:** From Bismarckian caution to Wilhelmine challenge to Nazi aggression.
- **USA:** Selective global power; crucial even when "isolationist."
- **Japan:** From pro-western cooperation to military-dominated expansionism under status grievance and resource pressure.
- **USSR:** Outsider power balancing survival, ideology, and strategy.
- **League of Nations:** Useful in small disputes, weak in major crises.
- **Wilson:** Peace by principle and League design.
- **Clemenceau:** Security and severity toward Germany.
- **Lloyd George:** Balance punishment with recovery.
- **Stresemann:** Peaceful revisionism.
- **Hitler:** Treaty-breaker and expansionist war-maker.
- **Mussolini:** Revisionist fascist opportunist.
- **Sun Yat-sen:** Foundational Chinese nationalist revolutionary.
- **Yuan Shih-kai:** Failed republican strongman.
- **Chiang Kai-shek:** Nationalist unifier, anti-communist, incomplete state-builder.
- **Mao Zedong / CCP:** Surviving revolutionary force that grew during war.

## One-line significance set
- Imperialism mattered because it linked economics, status, and strategy.
- Colonial crises mattered because they hardened alignments before 1914.
- Japan and the USA mattered because global power was no longer purely European.
- Versailles mattered because it created order and grievance at the same time.
- The League mattered because it could settle smaller disputes but failed when major powers resisted.
- The mid-1920s mattered because Dawes and Locarno created apparent stability without solving reparations or eastern insecurity.
- The Depression mattered because it weakened defenders of peace and strengthened its challengers.
- Manchuria mattered because aggression succeeded.
- Abyssinia mattered because sanctions failed publicly.
- Rhineland mattered because non-response emboldened Hitler.
- Munich mattered because it traded time for a worse position.
- China's importance lies in the overlap of nationalism, civil conflict, and foreign aggression.
- Pearl Harbor mattered because it fused separate regional crises into one global war.
