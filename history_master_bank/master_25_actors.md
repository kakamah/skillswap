# Master 25 Actors

Revision-first actor core. Each entry gives aims, key actions, why the actor matters, and exact confusion pairs.

## 1. Britain
- **Aims:** Protect empire, sea routes, and the balance of power; avoid another major European war after 1918 if possible.
- **Key actions:** Egypt 1882; Boer War; Entente Cordiale; Anglo-Japanese Alliance; Versailles role; Dawes/Locarno diplomacy; appeasement; Polish guarantee.
- **Why it matters:** Britain links late-imperial overstretch to the cautious management and then collapse of the interwar order.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Splendid isolation vs continued global commitments; appeasement as calculation vs appeasement as simple cowardice.

## 2. France
- **Aims:** Contain Germany, secure borders, preserve post-1919 gains, recover prestige after 1870.
- **Key actions:** Imperial expansion; Fashoda compromise; hard line at Versailles; Ruhr occupation; eastern alliance search; appeasement participation.
- **Why it matters:** French policy explains both the severity and fragility of the interwar settlement.
- **Confusion pair(s):** French severity at Paris vs later French weakness; Ruhr coercion vs Dawes compromise.

## 3. Bismarck
- **Aims:** Preserve German security after unification; avoid encirclement; manage French hostility.
- **Key actions:** Prioritised European diplomacy over aggressive colonial confrontation.
- **Why it matters:** Bismarck provides the contrast that makes Wilhelmine Germany look more destabilising.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Bismarckian caution vs Wilhelmine Weltpolitik.

## 4. Wilhelmine Germany
- **Aims:** Prestige, world role, naval power, colonial influence, equal status with older empires.
- **Key actions:** Naval expansion; Moroccan crises; assertive diplomacy; anti-British signalling.
- **Why it matters:** Shows how status-seeking intensified pre-1914 distrust even when immediate war was not chosen.
- **Confusion pair(s):** First Moroccan Crisis / Algeciras vs Second Moroccan Crisis / Agadir.

## 5. Stresemann / Weimar Germany
- **Aims:** Revise Versailles peacefully, recover prosperity, restore German prestige.
- **Key actions:** Dawes cooperation; Locarno; League entry; Treaty of Berlin with USSR; Young Plan diplomacy.
- **Why it matters:** Shows that German revisionism did not have to take Nazi form.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Peaceful revisionism vs Nazi revisionism; Rapallo 1922 vs Berlin 1926.

## 6. Hitler / Nazi Germany
- **Aims:** Destroy Versailles, rearm, unite Germans, expand east, dominate Europe.
- **Key actions:** Left League; rearmed; Saar, Rhineland, Anschluss, Sudetenland, Prague, Poland; Nazi-Soviet Pact.
- **Why it matters:** Central actor in the collapse of the interwar order.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Rhineland 1936 vs Anschluss 1938 vs Munich 1938 vs Prague 1939.

## 7. United States
- **Aims:** Protect trade and influence, avoid binding commitments when domestic politics resisted, then shape world order when necessary.
- **Key actions:** Spanish-American War; Open Door; WWI intervention; Fourteen Points; Washington Conference; Dawes/Young support; sanctions on Japan; war after Pearl Harbor.
- **Why it matters:** The USA repeatedly shaped events even when it rejected League membership.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Isolationism vs irrelevance; Washington diplomacy vs League diplomacy.

## 8. Japan
- **Aims:** Security, status, resources, regional dominance in East Asia.
- **Key actions:** Meiji modernisation; war wins over China and Russia; Twenty-One Demands; racial equality clause push at Paris; Washington limits; May 15; Manchuria; China war; Pearl Harbor.
- **Why it matters:** Japan is the key non-European case of rapid modernisation becoming militarised imperial expansion.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Shimonoseki vs Triple Intervention; Paris racial equality issue vs Shantung issue; Manchuria 1931 vs full war 1937.

## 9. Russia / USSR
- **Aims:** Under tsars, expand influence; under the USSR, survive isolation and secure better strategic terms.
- **Key actions:** Triple Intervention; Russo-Japanese War; Brest-Litovsk; Rapallo; Treaty of Berlin; League entry in 1934; Spanish involvement; anti-Hitler diplomacy; Nazi-Soviet Pact.
- **Why it matters:** Soviet policy shows ideology and power politics colliding without cancelling each other.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Rapallo vs Berlin 1926; anti-fascist diplomacy vs Nazi-Soviet Pact.

## 10. Mussolini / Italy
- **Aims:** Prestige, expansion, anti-communist strength, recognition as a great power.
- **Key actions:** Corfu; Fascist dictatorship; Abyssinia; Stresa participation then collapse into Axis alignment; Munich.
- **Why it matters:** Italy helps explain how collective security broke down from within Europe.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Mussolini early anti-German stance vs later Axis partner; Abyssinia as Italian, not German.

## 11. League of Nations
- **Aims:** Prevent war, arbitrate disputes, coordinate collective security, encourage cooperation.
- **Key actions:** Settled some smaller disputes; humanitarian work; investigated Manchuria and Abyssinia.
- **Why it matters:** The League is the clearest institutional test of whether interwar peace could be defended.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Minor-dispute success vs major-power failure; total success vs total failure.

## 12. Wilson
- **Aims:** Fair peace, self-determination, open diplomacy, League of Nations.
- **Key actions:** Fourteen Points; Paris leadership; League advocacy; insisted the racial equality clause needed unanimity.
- **Why it matters:** Wilsonian language became the standard against which the settlement was judged.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Fourteen Points vs Versailles; ideals vs actual compromise.

## 13. Clemenceau
- **Aims:** Security against Germany, revenge for 1870 and wartime devastation.
- **Key actions:** Hard line at Paris; support for strong reparations and security clauses.
- **Why it matters:** Essential for understanding why Versailles looked punitive to Germany.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Clemenceau severity vs Lloyd George balancing.

## 14. Lloyd George
- **Aims:** Punish Germany but preserve European recovery and British trade.
- **Key actions:** Mediating role at Paris; later support for stabilisation and compromise.
- **Why it matters:** Embodies the compromise nature of the peace settlement.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Lloyd George vs Clemenceau; public anti-German rhetoric vs economic realism.

## 15. Sun Yat-sen
- **Aims:** National unity, modern republican China, anti-warlord reconstruction, Three Principles.
- **Key actions:** Led revolutionary movement; built southern government; entered First United Front; backed Whampoa project.
- **Why it matters:** Sun provided the nationalist ideological framework later claimed by rival Chinese movements.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Sun ideological leadership vs Chiang military-political dominance.

## 16. Yuan Shih-kai
- **Aims:** Personal power and central authority.
- **Key actions:** Became president; dissolved parliament; crushed opposition; tried to become emperor.
- **Why it matters:** His failure helps explain why the republic quickly fragmented into warlord politics.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Republic founded 1912 vs stable republic achieved; Yuan vs Chiang as strongman rulers.

## 17. Chiang Kai-shek / KMT
- **Aims:** National unity under KMT leadership, anti-communism, controlled state-building, resistance to Japan on his own timetable.
- **Key actions:** Northern Expedition; Shanghai Purge; Nanking government; extermination campaigns; Xi'an crisis; full war against Japan.
- **Why it matters:** Chiang explains why China was partially unified yet still politically weak.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Northern Expedition nominal unity vs total centralisation; anti-CCP priority vs anti-Japan priority.

## 18. Mao Zedong / CCP
- **Aims:** Communist revolution, survival after 1927, rural mobilisation, eventual dominance of China.
- **Key actions:** Jiangxi base; Long March; Yan'an consolidation; wartime expansion and propaganda.
- **Why it matters:** CCP growth explains why war against Japan also transformed China's internal balance of power.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Long March as military victory vs organisational survival; CCP support from anti-Japanese war vs from wider rural politics.

## 19. Zhang Xueliang
- **Aims:** Stronger resistance to Japan and a united anti-Japanese line.
- **Key actions:** Helped trigger the Xi'an Incident.
- **Why it matters:** Shows major changes in Chinese strategy did not come only from Chiang or Mao.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Zhang Xueliang vs Zhang Zuolin / OCR-damaged Xuolin forms.

## 20. Saionji Kinmochi
- **Aims:** Defend Japan's great-power status at Paris.
- **Key actions:** Led Japan's delegation at the Paris Peace Conference.
- **Why it matters:** His role highlights that Japan was formally treated as a great power yet still denied full status equality.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Japan's seat at Paris vs equality within the western-dominated order.

## 21. Kijuro Shidehara
- **Aims:** Maintain Japanese security and economic expansion through cooperation rather than confrontation.
- **Key actions:** Represented the civilian, pro-cooperation interpretation behind accepting Washington limits.
- **Why it matters:** Shidehara embodies the diplomatic alternative to Japanese military expansionism.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Washington compromise by choice vs compromise by necessity.

## 22. Inukai Tsuyoshi
- **Aims:** Preserve civilian cabinet authority in Japan.
- **Key actions:** Criticised the Manchurian action and was assassinated in the May 15 Incident.
- **Why it matters:** His death marks the collapse of party government as a serious restraint on the military.
- **Confusion pair(s):** May 15 Incident vs later wartime military cabinets.

## 23. Hirohito
- **Aims:** Preserve imperial prestige and authority while ruling through a militarising state.
- **Key actions:** Did not force withdrawal from Manchuria; remained emperor through expansion and war.
- **Why it matters:** He mattered because imperial legitimacy remained central even when the military drove policy.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Hirohito as all-powerful dictator vs Hirohito as powerless figurehead.

## 24. Tojo
- **Aims:** Pursue military-led expansion and secure Japan's strategic position by force.
- **Key actions:** Led Japan during the final pre-Pearl Harbor escalation and wartime expansion.
- **Why it matters:** Tojo personifies the completed transition from party politics to military rule in Japan.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Tojo wartime leadership vs earlier civilian cabinets and the Inukai era.

## 25. Mustafa Kemal
- **Aims:** Overturn Sèvres, expel foreign forces, and establish a sovereign Turkish republic.
- **Key actions:** Led Turkish nationalist resistance, achieved Lausanne, and became first president of Turkey.
- **Why it matters:** Kemal proves not all Paris-era settlements were permanent.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Sèvres 1920 vs Lausanne 1923.
