# Master 200 Facts

Elite factual revision core. One line per fact: fact -> why it matters -> exact confusion pair(s).

## I. Empire and the emergence of world powers, 1870-1919

1. **Franco-Prussian War (1870-71):** French defeat made German unification possible. **Why it matters:** it changed the European balance and left France seeking security. **Confuse with:** German unification (outcome) vs the war itself (cause).
2. **German unification (1871):** a new industrial-military great power appeared in central Europe. **Why it matters:** later alliance politics and French fear make little sense without it. **Confuse with:** Anschluss (1938) vs unification (1871).
3. **Bismarck after 1871:** he prioritised European security over aggressive colonial confrontation. **Why it matters:** it contrasts sharply with later Wilhelmine world policy. **Confuse with:** Bismarckian caution vs Wilhelm II's Weltpolitik.
4. **Long Depression (1873-96):** economic strain encouraged tariff protection and arguments for colonies as markets and raw-material sources. **Why it matters:** it helped make empire look economically useful. **Confuse with:** Long Depression vs Great Depression (1929 onward).
5. **New Imperialism after 1870:** empire accelerated in Africa and Asia. **Why it matters:** it turned extra-European competition into a major part of international politics. **Confuse with:** old mercantilist empire vs late-19th-century imperialism.
6. **Imperial motives were mixed:** economics, strategy, nationalism, prestige, racism, and missionary language all mattered. **Why it matters:** it stops simplistic "empire was only greed" answers. **Confuse with:** single-cause imperialism vs mixed-motive imperialism.
7. **Suez Canal mattered strategically:** route security mattered as much as trade. **Why it matters:** it explains British intervention in Egypt. **Confuse with:** Egypt as economic prize only vs Egypt as route-security issue.
8. **British occupation of Egypt (1882):** Britain took control to secure the Suez route. **Why it matters:** the book treats it as a key stimulant to the wider scramble for Africa. **Confuse with:** Egypt 1882 vs Berlin Conference 1884-85.
9. **Berlin Conference / Treaty of Berlin (1884-85):** powers agreed notification and effective occupation rules for African claims. **Why it matters:** it gave the scramble a framework rather than stopping it. **Confuse with:** Berlin 1885 (Africa) vs Treaty of Berlin 1926 (Germany-USSR).
10. **Effective occupation principle:** powers had to show real control on the ground. **Why it matters:** it accelerated territorial grabbing. **Confuse with:** nominal claim vs effective occupation.
11. **Berlin did not protect Africans:** Africans were excluded from the decisions shaping partition. **Why it matters:** it shows European diplomacy solved European rivalry, not African consent. **Confuse with:** "Berlin ended the scramble" vs "Berlin structured and sped it."
12. **Leopold II's Congo rule:** the Congo Free State was his personal domain before Belgian state takeover. **Why it matters:** it reveals how brutal exploitation could be even without formal national annexation. **Confuse with:** Leopold's Congo vs ordinary Belgian colonial rule after 1908.
13. **Africa changed fast:** about 10% was under European control around 1870; over 90% by 1900 in the book's account. **Why it matters:** it captures the speed of the scramble. **Confuse with:** gradual empire growth vs rapid late-19th-century partition.
14. **Colonial borders were often arbitrary:** frontiers reflected European bargaining, not local realities. **Why it matters:** the book links this to long-term instability. **Confuse with:** imperial administration vs post-colonial state viability.
15. **France sought prestige after 1870:** empire helped answer defeat by Germany. **Why it matters:** imperial expansion was tied to European status politics. **Confuse with:** French imperialism as purely economic vs prestige-driven too.
16. **German colonies were not always profitable:** prestige often outweighed clear economic return. **Why it matters:** it supports the argument that status mattered as much as profit. **Confuse with:** colonial empire always economically rational vs often politically symbolic.
17. **Technology enabled conquest:** steamships, rifles, machine guns, and quinine made inland expansion easier. **Why it matters:** imperialism depended on capability, not just desire. **Confuse with:** ideology alone vs technology plus ideology.
18. **Civilising rhetoric masked self-interest:** moral language often justified strategic or economic expansion. **Why it matters:** it separates justification from motive. **Confuse with:** humanitarian claim vs imperial interest.
19. **Fashoda Incident (1898):** Britain and France clashed in Sudan but backed away from war. **Why it matters:** colonial rivalry could intensify tension without causing general war. **Confuse with:** Fashoda vs Entente Cordiale.
20. **Jameson Raid (1895):** the failed raid deepened mistrust in southern Africa. **Why it matters:** it helped poison relations before the Boer War. **Confuse with:** Jameson Raid vs Second Boer War.
21. **Second Boer War (1899-1902):** Britain fought a long, costly war with guerrilla fighting and camps. **Why it matters:** it damaged British prestige and exposed weakness. **Confuse with:** Boer War as easy British victory vs costly imperial war.
22. **Treaty of Vereeniging (1902):** it ended the Boer War under British supremacy. **Why it matters:** it closed the war militarily but not the reputational damage. **Confuse with:** Vereeniging vs Treaty of Paris (1898).
23. **Boer War weakened British isolation:** Britain looked exposed and diplomatically vulnerable. **Why it matters:** it helps explain later ententes and alliances. **Confuse with:** "splendid isolation" as permanent vs already eroding.
24. **US frontier closure mattered politically:** it helped push elite opinion toward overseas expansion. **Why it matters:** it links domestic development to foreign policy. **Confuse with:** continental expansion vs overseas imperialism.
25. **Mahan's sea-power argument:** naval power was presented as essential to world status. **Why it matters:** it fed US expansionist and strategic thinking. **Confuse with:** Monroe Doctrine vs navalist global strategy.
26. **Spanish-American War (1898):** the USA defeated Spain quickly. **Why it matters:** it marks the decisive outward turn of US power. **Confuse with:** Spanish-American War vs Treaty of Paris 1898.
27. **Treaty of Paris (1898):** Spain ceded major possessions to the USA. **Why it matters:** it gave the USA overseas empire in formal treaty form. **Confuse with:** Treaty of Paris 1898 vs Paris Peace Conference 1919.
28. **Platt Amendment / 1903 treaty:** Cuba was nominally independent but under heavy US control. **Why it matters:** US imperialism often meant control without outright annexation. **Confuse with:** nominal independence vs real sovereignty.
29. **Panama Canal completed (1914):** US-backed canal strategy transformed Atlantic-Pacific movement. **Why it matters:** it gave the USA a strategic maritime shortcut. **Confuse with:** Panama Canal vs Open Door policy.
30. **Open Door policy (1899):** the USA demanded equal trade access in China. **Why it matters:** it showed the USA shaping Asian diplomacy, not just American affairs. **Confuse with:** Open Door vs sphere-of-influence partition.
31. **First Sino-Japanese War (1894-95):** Japan defeated China decisively. **Why it matters:** it exposed Qing weakness and announced Japan's rise. **Confuse with:** First Sino-Japanese War vs Russo-Japanese War.
32. **Treaty of Shimonoseki (1895):** China accepted defeat and Japanese gains. **Why it matters:** it formally confirmed Japanese regional power. **Confuse with:** Shimonoseki vs Triple Intervention.
33. **Triple Intervention (1895):** Russia, France, and Germany forced Japan to retreat from some gains. **Why it matters:** humiliation fed later Japanese hostility, especially toward Russia. **Confuse with:** Triple Intervention vs Russo-Japanese War.
34. **Anglo-Japanese Alliance (1902):** Britain recognised Japan as a serious partner in East Asia. **Why it matters:** it linked European and Asian power politics. **Confuse with:** Anglo-Japanese Alliance vs Entente Cordiale.
35. **Russo-Japanese War (1904-05):** Japan defeated Russia on land and at sea. **Why it matters:** it shattered assumptions of automatic European superiority. **Confuse with:** Russo-Japanese War vs First Sino-Japanese War.
36. **Battle of Tsushima (1905):** Japan destroyed the Russian fleet decisively. **Why it matters:** it made the scale of Russian defeat unmistakable. **Confuse with:** Tsushima vs Port Arthur.
37. **Treaty of Portsmouth (1905):** Roosevelt mediated the peace ending the Russo-Japanese War. **Why it matters:** it confirmed Japan's status and US diplomatic weight. **Confuse with:** Portsmouth vs Washington Conference.
38. **Entente Cordiale (1904):** Britain and France settled colonial disputes. **Why it matters:** it reduced friction between old imperial rivals and tightened anti-German alignment. **Confuse with:** Entente Cordiale vs full wartime alliance.
39. **First Moroccan Crisis (1905-06):** Germany challenged French influence but failed to split Britain and France. **Why it matters:** it strengthened the Entente. **Confuse with:** First Moroccan Crisis / Algeciras vs Agadir.
40. **Algeciras Conference (1906):** Germany emerged diplomatically isolated over Morocco. **Why it matters:** it showed that diplomatic tests could harden blocs. **Confuse with:** Algeciras 1906 vs Agadir 1911.
41. **Second Moroccan Crisis / Agadir (1911):** Germany sent the Panther to pressure France again. **Why it matters:** it intensified British suspicion before 1914. **Confuse with:** Agadir vs Algeciras.
42. **German naval challenge to Britain:** naval competition fed British alarm before 1914. **Why it matters:** prestige and security became linked in Anglo-German tension. **Confuse with:** naval race vs Moroccan crises.
43. **Japan entered WWI (1914):** it seized German possessions in East Asia. **Why it matters:** war increased Japanese influence in the region. **Confuse with:** Japan in WWI vs Japan in 1930s China war.
44. **Twenty-One Demands (1915):** Japan pressured China for extensive privileges. **Why it matters:** it deepened Chinese nationalism and anti-Japanese feeling. **Confuse with:** Twenty-One Demands vs Washington settlement.
45. **Unrestricted submarine warfare mattered:** it helped push the USA toward war. **Why it matters:** military methods turned US neutrality into intervention. **Confuse with:** submarine warfare vs Zimmermann Telegram.
46. **Zimmermann Telegram (1917):** German diplomacy against the USA hardened US opinion. **Why it matters:** it became a direct political reason for US entry. **Confuse with:** Zimmermann Telegram vs Fourteen Points.
47. **USA entered WWI (April 1917):** the USA joined the Allies against Germany. **Why it matters:** US power shaped both victory and peace-making. **Confuse with:** US neutrality before 1917 vs war after April 1917.
48. **Wilson's Fourteen Points (Jan 1918):** they set out open diplomacy, self-determination, and League ideas. **Why it matters:** they became the benchmark used to criticise the 1919 settlement. **Confuse with:** Fourteen Points vs Treaty of Versailles.
49. **Japan wanted status as well as empire:** by 1919 it sought recognition as a true great power, not just territorial gains. **Why it matters:** this helps explain later resentment toward the West. **Confuse with:** Japanese territorial grievance vs status grievance.
50. **Imperial rivalry before 1914 was dangerous but often contained:** many colonial clashes ended in bargaining rather than war. **Why it matters:** it explains why 1914 cannot be reduced to colonial crises alone. **Confuse with:** Fashoda/Agadir as automatic causes of general war.

## II. The Paris settlement, the League, and international relations in the 1920s

51. **Paris Peace Conference (1919):** the victors designed the post-war order. **Why it matters:** the whole interwar system rested on these decisions. **Confuse with:** Paris Peace Conference vs Treaty of Versailles alone.
52. **The defeated were excluded from negotiation:** Germany had no real role in shaping Versailles. **Why it matters:** it helps explain the "dictated peace" grievance. **Confuse with:** peace conference negotiation vs treaty imposition.
53. **Versailles signed on 28 June 1919:** Germany accepted the treaty under pressure. **Why it matters:** the date anchors the formal beginning of the German settlement. **Confuse with:** Armistice 1918 vs Versailles 1919.
54. **Article 231 mattered because it underpinned reparations:** its legal role mattered as much as its symbolic humiliation. **Why it matters:** it clarifies why Germans focused on it. **Confuse with:** Article 231 vs "the only cause of Nazism."
55. **Alsace-Lorraine returned to France:** Versailles reversed a major outcome of 1871. **Why it matters:** it answered a core French grievance. **Confuse with:** Alsace-Lorraine vs Saar.
56. **The Polish Corridor gave Poland sea access:** it separated Germany from East Prussia. **Why it matters:** it became a lasting German grievance. **Confuse with:** Polish Corridor vs Danzig question.
57. **The Saar went under League control for 15 years:** France could use its coal during that period. **Why it matters:** it combined economic punishment with a later plebiscite. **Confuse with:** Saar 1919 arrangement vs Saar plebiscite 1935.
58. **The Rhineland was demilitarised:** Germany could not station troops there. **Why it matters:** it was a key French security safeguard. **Confuse with:** demilitarised Rhineland 1919 vs remilitarised Rhineland 1936.
59. **Germany's army was capped at 100,000:** key weapons such as tanks, aircraft, and submarines were banned. **Why it matters:** later rearmament becomes easier to measure against these limits. **Confuse with:** army limit vs total disarmament.
60. **Anschluss was forbidden by Versailles:** Germany and Austria could not unite. **Why it matters:** it made the 1938 Anschluss a direct treaty reversal. **Confuse with:** Anschluss ban 1919 vs Anschluss event 1938.
61. **German colonies became League mandates:** empire survived in new legal form. **Why it matters:** it shows that anti-German settlement did not mean anti-imperial settlement. **Confuse with:** mandates vs independence.
62. **Treaty of Saint-Germain (1919):** Austria lost major territories and became a much smaller state. **Why it matters:** Austria's weakness helps explain later vulnerability. **Confuse with:** Saint-Germain vs Trianon.
63. **Treaty of Neuilly (1919):** Bulgaria lost territory, accepted army limits, and reparations. **Why it matters:** dissatisfaction with Paris was wider than Germany alone. **Confuse with:** Neuilly vs Trianon vs Saint-Germain.
64. **Treaty of Trianon (1920):** Hungary lost large territories and many ethnic Hungarians ended up outside Hungary. **Why it matters:** it fed revisionism in central Europe. **Confuse with:** Trianon vs Saint-Germain.
65. **Treaty of Sevres (1920):** the Ottoman Empire was reduced drastically. **Why it matters:** it became the clearest example of a settlement later overturned. **Confuse with:** Sevres vs Lausanne.
66. **Treaty of Lausanne (1923):** it replaced Sevres and recognised the Republic of Turkey. **Why it matters:** it proves post-war treaties were not all permanent. **Confuse with:** Lausanne vs Locarno.
67. **Successor states created minority problems:** self-determination did not produce clean borders. **Why it matters:** it built instability into central and eastern Europe. **Confuse with:** self-determination in theory vs mixed populations in practice.
68. **The USA never joined the League:** Senate rejection weakened Wilson's system. **Why it matters:** it reduced both League power and French confidence. **Confuse with:** US non-membership vs US irrelevance.
69. **The League worked best in small disputes:** success depended on consent. **Why it matters:** it explains why it could succeed in the 1920s yet fail later. **Confuse with:** total League success vs total League failure.
70. **The League had no standing army:** it depended on members to act. **Why it matters:** this is the core structural weakness in every major crisis. **Confuse with:** League principle vs League enforcement.
71. **Vilna exposed League limits:** Poland defied League wishes and major powers would not enforce a reversal. **Why it matters:** it showed early that enforcement failed when interests hardened. **Confuse with:** minor League success cases like Aaland.
72. **Aaland and Upper Silesia showed the League could work:** some disputes were resolved peacefully. **Why it matters:** a balanced answer must include genuine successes. **Confuse with:** League success in minor disputes vs failure against major powers.
73. **Reparations were fixed at a very high total in 1921:** Germany faced heavy obligations. **Why it matters:** this helps explain the Ruhr and later rescheduling. **Confuse with:** reparations fixed 1921 vs Dawes 1924 vs Young 1929.
74. **War debts and reparations formed a financial chain:** Allied debt to the USA increased pressure on Germany to pay. **Why it matters:** economics and diplomacy cannot be separated in the 1920s. **Confuse with:** reparations as only a Franco-German issue vs wider debt system.
75. **Ruhr occupation (1923):** France and Belgium occupied Germany's industrial region after default. **Why it matters:** coercion worsened the very instability it was meant to solve. **Confuse with:** Ruhr occupation vs Rhineland remilitarisation.
76. **Hyperinflation peaked in 1923:** German currency collapsed amid passive resistance and state financing. **Why it matters:** it destroyed confidence and made reparations reform unavoidable. **Confuse with:** hyperinflation 1923 vs global depression 1929.
77. **Corfu Crisis (1923):** Mussolini used force and the League failed to impose principle on Italy. **Why it matters:** it exposed weakness against a determined power even in the 1920s. **Confuse with:** Corfu vs Abyssinia.
78. **Washington Conference (1921-22):** it tried to stabilise naval rivalry and the Pacific. **Why it matters:** it was the high point of interwar Pacific diplomacy. **Confuse with:** Washington Conference vs Anglo-Japanese Alliance.
79. **Five-Power naval ratio was 5:5:3:** USA and Britain remained ahead of Japan in battleships. **Why it matters:** Japanese resentment over second-rank treatment mattered later. **Confuse with:** naval ratio issue vs racial equality issue.
80. **The Four-Power framework replaced the Anglo-Japanese Alliance:** old bilateral alignment was removed. **Why it matters:** East Asian diplomacy shifted from alliance to multilateral management. **Confuse with:** Anglo-Japanese Alliance 1902 vs Washington framework 1921-22.
81. **The Nine-Power Treaty upheld the Open Door in China:** powers promised respect for Chinese integrity and equal access. **Why it matters:** it shows the contrast between principle and later Japanese expansion. **Confuse with:** Open Door 1899 vs Nine-Power reaffirmation 1921-22.
82. **Treaty of Rapallo (1922):** Germany and the USSR rebuilt ties outside the Paris framework. **Why it matters:** excluded states could cooperate without League approval. **Confuse with:** Rapallo 1922 vs Treaty of Berlin 1926.
83. **Dawes Plan (1924):** reparations were rescheduled and US loans flowed into Germany. **Why it matters:** mid-1920s recovery rested on American finance. **Confuse with:** Dawes vs Young.
84. **Dawes also brought Ruhr evacuation:** confrontation gave way to managed compromise. **Why it matters:** it marks the shift from crisis to temporary stabilisation. **Confuse with:** Ruhr crisis 1923 vs Dawes recovery 1924.
85. **Locarno (1925):** Germany accepted western borders in a new settlement framework. **Why it matters:** it became the symbol of western détente. **Confuse with:** Locarno vs Stresa.
86. **Locarno protected the west more than the east:** eastern borders remained less secure. **Why it matters:** that limitation is central to judging the 1920s. **Confuse with:** Locarno as solving all frontier problems.
87. **Germany entered the League in 1926:** Stresemann gained diplomatic rehabilitation. **Why it matters:** Germany became partly reintegrated into the system it wanted revised. **Confuse with:** Germany in League 1926 vs USSR in League 1934.
88. **Treaty of Berlin (1926):** Germany renewed Soviet ties even while joining the League system. **Why it matters:** Stresemann mixed cooperation with revisionist flexibility. **Confuse with:** Berlin 1926 vs Berlin 1885.
89. **Kellogg-Briand Pact (1928):** states renounced war in principle. **Why it matters:** it was the peak of interwar legal idealism without real sanctions. **Confuse with:** renouncing war vs preventing war.
90. **Young Plan (1929):** reparations were reduced again and spread over longer periods. **Why it matters:** it admitted earlier totals were unrealistic. **Confuse with:** Young reduction vs cancellation.
91. **Stresemann was a peaceful revisionist:** he wanted change, but by diplomacy. **Why it matters:** it shows revisionism did not have to mean Hitler. **Confuse with:** Stresemann's revisionism vs Hitler's revisionism.
92. **Briand embodied Franco-German détente:** cooperation briefly looked possible in the west. **Why it matters:** it helps explain the phrase "spirit of Locarno." **Confuse with:** Briand's diplomacy vs Clemenceau's settlement severity.
93. **Mustafa Kemal overturned Sevres by force and diplomacy:** Turkish resistance changed the settlement. **Why it matters:** Paris did not settle everything permanently. **Confuse with:** Sevres imposed 1920 vs Lausanne revised 1923.
94. **Mussolini used Corfu to test League weakness:** force worked better than procedure. **Why it matters:** later aggressors learned from such failures. **Confuse with:** Corfu 1923 vs Abyssinia 1935-36.
95. **Mid-1920s recovery depended on US money:** it was never structurally secure. **Why it matters:** the Crash later destroys the financial base of peace. **Confuse with:** apparent stability vs durable stability.
96. **US isolationism was political, not total withdrawal:** Washington, Dawes, and Young prove continued involvement. **Why it matters:** this is a frequent exam trap. **Confuse with:** US non-membership in League vs US irrelevance.
97. **The Paris settlement created both order and grievance:** it solved war termination better than long-term consent. **Why it matters:** this is the central analytical judgement on 1919-20. **Confuse with:** settlement as total failure vs total success.
98. **League agencies and technical work existed beyond crises:** the institution was not only about war prevention. **Why it matters:** it stops over-simplified "League did nothing" claims. **Confuse with:** League diplomacy vs League humanitarian/technical work.
99. **Minority problems cut across the successor states:** language-based self-determination ignored many ethnic and religious complexities. **Why it matters:** minority tensions weakened the new states. **Confuse with:** self-determination in theory vs practical border justice.
100. **The 1920s looked more stable than they were:** Locarno, Dawes, and Kellogg-Briand masked fragility. **Why it matters:** the 1930s collapse grew from unresolved weaknesses. **Confuse with:** temporary recovery vs permanent peace.

## III. The League, extremism, and the road to war in the 1930s

101. **Wall Street Crash (Oct 1929):** US financial collapse spread across the world. **Why it matters:** it broke the credit base of interwar stability. **Confuse with:** Wall Street Crash vs German hyperinflation.
102. **The Depression radicalised politics:** unemployment and insecurity strengthened extremists. **Why it matters:** it helps explain Hitler, Japanese militarism, and wider instability. **Confuse with:** economic crisis as background only vs political turning point.
103. **Japan's democracy weakened in the 1920s-30s:** civilian politics lost authority before full military dictatorship. **Why it matters:** Japan's aggression needs domestic explanation, not just foreign-policy explanation. **Confuse with:** Japan as unified actor vs civil-military struggle.
104. **Rejection of Japan's racial equality proposal (1919) mattered long after 1919:** it fed status resentment toward the West. **Why it matters:** it helps explain why cooperative diplomacy later lost support in Japan. **Confuse with:** status grievance vs purely territorial grievance.
105. **The Shantung reversal angered Japan too:** many Japanese saw it as another Western insult. **Why it matters:** Paris resentment was not about one issue only. **Confuse with:** Shantung grievance vs racial equality grievance.
106. **Japanese Exclusion Act (1924):** US immigration exclusion deepened anti-Western feeling in Japan. **Why it matters:** it linked race, status, and foreign policy resentment. **Confuse with:** immigration exclusion vs naval limitation.
107. **Washington naval limits were unpopular with Japanese nationalists:** cooperation looked like second-rank treatment. **Why it matters:** it weakened support for pro-Western diplomacy. **Confuse with:** Washington as total Japanese success vs total Japanese humiliation.
108. **Party corruption and zaibatsu influence hurt Japanese democracy:** politicians looked self-serving and weak. **Why it matters:** it made military alternatives look more attractive. **Confuse with:** democratic failure as only foreign-policy issue vs domestic political crisis too.
109. **Secret groups like Sakurakai wanted military dictatorship:** ultra-nationalist activism targeted party politics. **Why it matters:** political violence helped destroy civilian rule. **Confuse with:** secret societies vs formal army command.
110. **May 15 Incident (1932):** Prime Minister Inukai was assassinated by young naval officers. **Why it matters:** party government lost further authority. **Confuse with:** May 15 Incident vs Manchurian Crisis.
111. **Army and navy ministers had to be serving officers:** military institutions had leverage over governments. **Why it matters:** civilian control in Japan was structurally weak. **Confuse with:** parliamentary democracy vs full civilian supremacy.
112. **Manchurian Crisis / Mukden Incident (1931):** Japan seized Manchuria after a staged railway explosion. **Why it matters:** it was the first decisive League failure of the 1930s. **Confuse with:** Manchuria 1931 vs full Sino-Japanese War 1937.
113. **Manchukuo was a puppet state:** Japan dressed conquest as state creation. **Why it matters:** aggression was hidden behind legal forms. **Confuse with:** Manchukuo vs real Chinese sovereignty.
114. **The League failed to reverse Manchuria:** procedure did not recover the territory. **Why it matters:** aggressors learned that force could succeed. **Confuse with:** investigation vs enforcement.
115. **Japan left the League in 1933:** it rejected criticism over Manchuria. **Why it matters:** a major power openly abandoned collective-security discipline. **Confuse with:** League vote timing vs formal Japanese break.
116. **Hitler became Chancellor in 1933:** Nazi dictatorship grew out of the Depression crisis. **Why it matters:** German revisionism turned far more dangerous. **Confuse with:** Hitler's appointment vs full dictatorship.
117. **Germany left the Disarmament Conference and League in 1933:** Hitler rejected the interwar framework. **Why it matters:** containment became much harder. **Confuse with:** German withdrawal 1933 vs war 1939.
118. **German rearmament became open by 1935:** Versailles restrictions were being dismantled in practice. **Why it matters:** treaty revision moved from rhetoric to action. **Confuse with:** rearmament vs Saar return vs Rhineland.
119. **Saar plebiscite (1935):** the Saar returned to Germany. **Why it matters:** Hitler gained prestige through a legal-national success. **Confuse with:** Saar 1935 vs Rhineland 1936.
120. **Stresa Front (1935):** Britain, France, and Italy declared opposition to forced treaty revision. **Why it matters:** it was the last serious anti-Hitler front before collapse. **Confuse with:** Stresa vs Locarno.
121. **Anglo-German Naval Agreement (1935):** Britain accepted German naval growth by bilateral deal. **Why it matters:** it weakened Stresa and alarmed France. **Confuse with:** AGNA vs Stresa Front.
122. **Abyssinia was invaded by Italy in 1935:** Mussolini, not Hitler, was the aggressor. **Why it matters:** this is a major exam confusion the bank repeatedly flags. **Confuse with:** Abyssinia vs Hitler's central-European expansion.
123. **Sanctions failed in Abyssinia:** they were too limited to stop conquest. **Why it matters:** collective security lost practical credibility. **Confuse with:** sanctions imposed vs sanctions effective.
124. **Hoare-Laval damaged trust in League principle:** compromise plans made sanctions politics look dishonest. **Why it matters:** public faith in collective security was badly weakened. **Confuse with:** official League sanctions vs unofficial great-power bargaining.
125. **Abyssinia pushed Italy toward Germany:** Mussolini drifted away from Britain and France. **Why it matters:** League failure reshaped alignments, not just prestige. **Confuse with:** Stresa anti-German Italy vs later Axis Italy.
126. **Rhineland remilitarised (March 1936):** Germany broke a core security clause without military punishment. **Why it matters:** Hitler learned the West would not act decisively over treaty enforcement. **Confuse with:** Rhineland 1936 vs Anschluss 1938.
127. **Spanish Civil War began in July 1936:** Spain became a major ideological battlefield. **Why it matters:** it previewed wider European conflict. **Confuse with:** Spain 1936 vs Poland 1939.
128. **Germany and Italy helped Franco:** non-intervention worked unevenly in practice. **Why it matters:** fascist powers gained confidence and experience. **Confuse with:** non-intervention in theory vs one-sided intervention in practice.
129. **The USSR aided the Spanish Republic:** the war became an ideological proxy conflict. **Why it matters:** Spain linked domestic and international extremism. **Confuse with:** Soviet aid in Spain vs Nazi-Soviet Pact later.
130. **Anti-Comintern Pact (1936):** Germany and Japan aligned against communism; Italy joined later. **Why it matters:** it linked Asian and European anti-Soviet politics. **Confuse with:** Anti-Comintern Pact vs Pact of Steel vs Nazi-Soviet Pact.
131. **The Rome-Berlin Axis followed the Abyssinia crisis:** Italy and Germany moved closer after League failure. **Why it matters:** diplomatic isolation changed Mussolini's choices. **Confuse with:** Stresa Front vs Rome-Berlin Axis.
132. **Full Sino-Japanese War (1937):** conflict spread from earlier Japanese expansion into all-out war across China. **Why it matters:** East Asia became a central front of the 1930s crisis. **Confuse with:** Manchuria 1931 vs full war 1937.
133. **Nanjing Massacre (Dec 1937):** Japanese forces committed mass killing after taking the city. **Why it matters:** it symbolised the brutality of Japanese expansion. **Confuse with:** Nanjing 1937 vs Shanghai Purge 1927.
134. **Anschluss (March 1938):** Germany absorbed Austria. **Why it matters:** it expanded German resources and tightened pressure on Czechoslovakia. **Confuse with:** Anschluss vs Munich.
135. **Sudeten crisis (1938):** Hitler used the German minority issue against Czechoslovakia. **Why it matters:** it set up Munich and exposed the stakes of appeasement. **Confuse with:** Sudetenland issue vs full destruction of Czechoslovakia in 1939.
136. **Munich Agreement (Sept 1938):** Britain and France accepted German acquisition of the Sudetenland. **Why it matters:** it was the peak of appeasement. **Confuse with:** Munich vs March 1939 occupation of Prague.
137. **Czechoslovakia was excluded at Munich:** the settlement was made over Czech objections. **Why it matters:** it shows appeasement sacrificed an ally's security. **Confuse with:** collective negotiation vs imposed concession.
138. **Occupation of the rest of Czechoslovakia (March 1939):** Hitler destroyed the idea that he only wanted Germans united. **Why it matters:** it changed British views of Hitler's aims. **Confuse with:** Munich's ethnic justification vs Prague's wider conquest.
139. **Britain guaranteed Poland in 1939:** deterrence replaced concession in principle. **Why it matters:** British policy shifted after Prague. **Confuse with:** Munich guarantee vs Poland guarantee.
140. **Anglo-Franco-Soviet talks failed in 1939:** no effective anti-Hitler grand alliance was created. **Why it matters:** this helped open the way to the Nazi-Soviet Pact. **Confuse with:** failed anti-Hitler diplomacy vs successful Soviet-German diplomacy.
141. **Nazi-Soviet Pact (Aug 1939):** Germany and the USSR agreed non-aggression. **Why it matters:** Hitler avoided immediate two-front war. **Confuse with:** Nazi-Soviet Pact vs Anti-Comintern Pact.
142. **The pact had secret spheres of influence:** eastern Europe was privately divided. **Why it matters:** Poland and the Baltics were exposed. **Confuse with:** public non-aggression vs secret partition logic.
143. **Germany invaded Poland in Sept 1939:** war began in Europe. **Why it matters:** appeasement ended in general war, not durable peace. **Confuse with:** Poland invasion vs Nazi-Soviet Pact.
144. **Britain and France declared war on 3 Sept 1939:** the European conflict became general. **Why it matters:** this is the formal end-point of the interwar order. **Confuse with:** German invasion date vs British/French declaration date.
145. **Appeasement was driven by fear, weakness, and strategy:** not simply by cowardice. **Why it matters:** it is essential analytical precision. **Confuse with:** appeasement vs pacifism.
146. **Manchuria was the first decisive League failure:** not an Asian sideshow. **Why it matters:** it set the precedent that aggression could succeed. **Confuse with:** Manchuria vs Abyssinia as the first major 1930s failure.
147. **Abyssinia destroyed confidence in sanctions:** Britain and France were directly compromised. **Why it matters:** the second failure was even more damaging than the first. **Confuse with:** League criticism vs League credibility collapse.
148. **Rhineland mattered more strategically than its territorial size suggests:** Germany changed the balance at low risk. **Why it matters:** later Anschluss and Munich became easier. **Confuse with:** Rhineland's geography vs its strategic effect.
