# 04 Actor Profiles

Source basis: uploaded markdown extraction of Cambridge International AS Level History: International History, 1870-1945. Page references are only included where the extraction made them reasonably clear. Any OCR damage, internal inconsistency, or weakly supported detail is flagged rather than normalised.


## Britain
- **Aims:** Protect empire, sea routes, and balance of power; avoid another general European war after 1918.
- **Fears:** Threats to India and Suez, German naval challenge, continental war, economic weakness, air attack in the 1930s.
- **Constraints:** Global commitments, cost, public anti-war feeling, military overstretch.
- **Actions:** Egypt 1882; Boer War; Entente Cordiale; Anglo-Japanese Alliance; role in Versailles; Dawes / Locarno diplomacy; appeasement; guarantee to Poland.
- **Key events linked:** Egypt, Boer War, Morocco, Versailles, Locarno, Abyssinia, Rhineland, Munich, Poland.
- **How position changed over time:** From imperial confidence to exposed overextension after Boer War; then a cautious manager of interwar order; then reluctant resister by 1939.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** British policy links the empire story before 1914 to the appeasement story before 1939; its choices repeatedly shaped whether crises were resisted, negotiated, or postponed.

## France
- **Aims:** Recover prestige after 1870, contain Germany, secure borders, preserve post-1919 gains.
- **Fears:** Renewed German attack, diplomatic isolation, weak allies.
- **Constraints:** Demographic weakness relative to Germany, financial strain, reliance on British or wider support.
- **Actions:** Imperial expansion, Fashoda compromise, strong line at Versailles, Ruhr occupation, alliance search in eastern Europe, involvement in appeasement.
- **Key events linked:** Franco-Prussian War legacy, Fashoda, Versailles, Ruhr, Locarno, Rhineland, Munich.
- **How position changed over time:** From revenge-focused power after 1871 to insecure defender of Versailles and then weakened partner in appeasement.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** French policy is central to understanding both the harshness and the fragility of the interwar order.

## Bismarckian Germany
- **Aims:** Preserve German security in Europe after unification.
- **Fears:** Encirclement, French revenge.
- **Constraints:** Need to avoid overextension after 1871.
- **Actions:** Prioritised European diplomacy over early colonial ambition.
- **Key events linked:** German unification, early empire debate.
- **How position changed over time:** Bismarckian caution gave way to Wilhelm II's more assertive Weltpolitik.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** The contrast between Bismarck and later German policy helps explain changing tensions before 1914.

## Wilhelmine Germany
- **Aims:** Prestige, world role, naval status, colonial influence.
- **Fears:** Being denied equal global status; diplomatic isolation.
- **Constraints:** Britain and France grew closer; colonial ambitions often outpaced resources.
- **Actions:** Naval expansion, Moroccan crises, support signals to anti-British forces, more assertive diplomacy.
- **Key events linked:** Kruger telegram, Morocco, naval race.
- **How position changed over time:** From newly unified power to increasingly distrusted challenger before 1914.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** Shows how status-seeking diplomacy can intensify tension even without immediate war aims in every crisis.

## Weimar Germany / Stresemann
- **Aims:** Revise Versailles peacefully where possible; recover prestige; restore prosperity.
- **Fears:** Domestic extremism, reparations pressure, diplomatic isolation.
- **Constraints:** Military limits, economic weakness, treaty obligations.
- **Actions:** Compliance mixed with revisionism; Locarno; League entry; Treaty of Berlin with USSR.
- **Key events linked:** Ruhr aftermath, Dawes, Locarno, League entry, Young Plan.
- **How position changed over time:** From coerced defeated state to semi-rehabilitated power by the later 1920s.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** Stresemann shows that revisionism did not have to take Nazi form.

## Nazi Germany / Hitler
- **Aims:** Destroy Versailles, rearm, unite Germans, expand eastward, dominate Europe.
- **Fears:** Encirclement, delay, internal weakness before rearmament matured.
- **Constraints:** Early military weakness; need to judge British and French reactions correctly.
- **Actions:** Left League; rearmed; Saar, conscription, Rhineland, Anschluss, Sudetenland, Prague, Poland; pact with USSR.
- **Key events linked:** 1933 takeover, Rhineland, Munich, March 1939, Poland.
- **How position changed over time:** From opportunistic treaty-breaker to open war-maker by 1939.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** Central agent in the collapse of the interwar system, but the book also stresses the enabling failures of others.

## USA
- **Aims:** Protect trade, expand influence, avoid entanglement when possible, then shape peace after 1917.
- **Fears:** European intervention in the Americas, threats to trade routes, later Japanese expansion and world instability.
- **Constraints:** Domestic isolationism, Senate resistance, distance from Europe and Asia.
- **Actions:** Spanish-American War, Open Door, Panama, WWI intervention, Fourteen Points, Washington Conference, Dawes / Young support, sanctions on Japan, Pearl Harbor response.
- **Key events linked:** 1898 war, Fourteen Points, Senate rejection, Washington, Dawes, Pearl Harbor.
- **How position changed over time:** From continental power to imperial power to indispensable but selective global actor.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** The USA repeatedly shaped the system even when it refused formal permanent commitments.

## Japan
- **Aims:** Avoid subordination, gain security and status, secure resources, dominate East Asia.
- **Fears:** Foreign encirclement, resource vulnerability, exclusion by western powers.
- **Constraints:** Resource dependence, tensions between civilian and military authority, strategic exposure, party instability, and the emperor's ambiguous constitutional position.
- **Actions:** Meiji modernisation; victories over China and Russia; alliance with Britain; Twenty-One Demands; pursuit of status at Paris in 1919; acceptance of Washington naval limits; drift away from party politics through ultra-nationalism, secret societies, and the May 15 Incident; Manchuria; China war; Pearl Harbor.
- **Key events linked:** Shimonoseki, Triple Intervention, Portsmouth, Twenty-One Demands, racial equality clause dispute, Washington Conference, Japanese Exclusion Act, May 15 Incident, Mukden, 1937 war, Pearl Harbor.
- **How position changed over time:** From rising model moderniser to aggressive military dictatorship.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** Japan is the clearest non-European case of rapid modernisation translating into imperial power, but the chapter also shows how status resentment, civilian weakness, and military autonomy turned that power into dictatorship and expansion.

## Russia / USSR
- **Aims:** Under the tsars, expand influence; under the USSR, survive isolation and shape security on better terms.
- **Fears:** Encirclement, capitalist hostility, German power, Japanese threat.
- **Constraints:** 1905 defeat, revolution, civil war, non-membership / partial exclusion from parts of the interwar system.
- **Actions:** Triple Intervention role, Russo-Japanese War, Brest-Litovsk exit, Rapallo, Treaty of Berlin, League entry in 1934, Spain involvement, attempted anti-Hitler diplomacy, Nazi-Soviet Pact.
- **Key events linked:** 1905 defeat, Brest-Litovsk, Rapallo, Franco-Soviet diplomacy, Nazi-Soviet Pact.
- **How position changed over time:** From imperial rival in East Asia to revolutionary outsider to pragmatic power-balancer.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** USSR policy exposes how ideology and strategy often collided but did not erase power politics.

## Italy / Mussolini
- **Aims:** Prestige, expansion, recognition as a great power, anti-communist strength.
- **Fears:** Socialism, weakness, diplomatic marginality.
- **Constraints:** Economic weakness, military limits, dependence on diplomatic openings.
- **Actions:** Corfu, Fascist dictatorship, Abyssinia, Stresa participation then collapse into Axis alignment, role at Munich.
- **Key events linked:** Corfu, Abyssinia, Rome-Berlin Axis, Pact of Steel.
- **How position changed over time:** From dissatisfied victor to revisionist aggressor and German partner.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** Italy helps explain how the League was undermined from within Europe before war with Germany began.

## League of Nations
- **Aims:** Prevent war, arbitrate disputes, encourage collective security, improve international cooperation.
- **Fears:** Lack of member support, refusal of powers to act, absence of key states.
- **Constraints:** No standing army, unanimity procedures, dependence on Britain and France, US non-membership.
- **Actions:** Minor dispute settlements, social and humanitarian agency work, investigations in Manchuria and Abyssinia.
- **Key events linked:** Vilna, Aaland, Upper Silesia, Corfu, Manchuria, Abyssinia.
- **How position changed over time:** Limited but real success in the 1920s; strategic collapse in the 1930s.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** It is the institutional core of the interwar peace experiment and the clearest measure of its failure.

## Wilson
- **Aims:** Fair peace, self-determination, open diplomacy, League of Nations.
- **Fears:** Harsh peace breeding future conflict.
- **Constraints:** Allied security demands; US Senate hostility; limited leverage once domestic support weakened.
- **Actions:** Fourteen Points; leading role in Paris; advocacy of the League.
- **Key events linked:** 1917 war entry, Fourteen Points, Paris Peace Conference.
- **How position changed over time:** From highly influential wartime leader to politically weakened figure unable to secure US entry into his own system.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** Wilsonian ideals became the standard by which many judged the peace - even where the settlement diverged sharply.

## Clemenceau
- **Aims:** Security against Germany, revenge for 1870 and 1914-18 devastation.
- **Fears:** Another German recovery and invasion.
- **Constraints:** Needed British and US support for maximum guarantees.
- **Actions:** Hard line at Paris; support for strong reparations and security clauses.
- **Key events linked:** Versailles, reparations debates, French security policy.
- **How position changed over time:** Strongest at Paris; French insecurity remained even after the treaty.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** Essential to understanding why the settlement looked punitive to many Germans.

## Lloyd George
- **Aims:** Punish Germany but preserve European recovery and British trade interests.
- **Fears:** Total German collapse, French overreach, domestic pressure to "make Germany pay".
- **Constraints:** Public opinion, coalition politics, wartime costs.
- **Actions:** Mediating role at Paris; later support for compromise and stabilisation.
- **Key events linked:** Paris Peace Conference, interwar settlement debates.
- **How position changed over time:** Balanced between French severity and Wilsonian leniency.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** Embodies the compromise nature of the peace settlement.

## Sun Yat-sen
- **Aims:** National unity, modern republican China, Three Principles, anti-warlord reconstruction.
- **Fears:** Foreign domination, military fragmentation, weak republican institutions.
- **Constraints:** Limited military power, dependence on regional allies and Soviet help.
- **Actions:** Led revolutionary movement, set up southern government, entered First United Front.
- **Key events linked:** 1911 Revolution, republican founding, United Front, Whampoa.
- **How position changed over time:** Symbolic founder more than secure ruler; influence outlasted his practical power.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** Sun matters because the KMT, the republic, and later rival Chinese movements all claimed his nationalist legacy even though he never secured stable national power.

## Yuan Shih-kai
- **Aims:** Personal control and central authority.
- **Fears:** Revolutionary collapse, provincial disobedience.
- **Constraints:** Weak legitimacy, dependence on military strength, foreign pressure.
- **Actions:** Became president, crushed opponents, dissolved parliament, tried to become emperor.
- **Key events linked:** 1912 republic, 1913-16 dictatorship, rise of warlordism.
- **How position changed over time:** From apparent stabiliser to failed would-be autocrat.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** His failure helps explain why the republic quickly fragmented.

## Chiang Kai-shek / KMT
- **Aims:** National unity under KMT leadership, anti-communism, state-building, eventual resistance to Japan on his own terms.
- **Fears:** Communist revolution, warlord challenge, premature war with Japan.
- **Constraints:** Weak finances, factionalism, incomplete territorial control, Japanese pressure.
- **Actions:** Northern Expedition, Shanghai Purge, Nanking government, extermination campaigns, New Life Movement, limited resistance then full war.
- **Key events linked:** 1926-28 Expedition, 1927 White Terror, Long March, Xi'an, 1937 war.
- **How position changed over time:** Rose from military leader to dominant Nationalist figure but never fully solved legitimacy or control problems.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** Central to why China was partially unified yet still politically fragile.

## CCP / Mao Zedong
- **Aims:** Communist revolution, survival after 1927, expansion of rural support, eventual dominance of China.
- **Fears:** Destruction by the KMT, factional weakness, isolation.
- **Constraints:** Small size at first, military inferiority, dependence on remote bases.
- **Actions:** Rural organising, Jiangxi base, Long March, Yan'an consolidation, wartime expansion.
- **Key events linked:** CCP founding, White Terror, Long March, Xi'an, anti-Japanese war.
- **How position changed over time:** From marginal urban revolutionary party to resilient rural-national force by 1945.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** The CCP's growth explains why war against Japan also reshaped China's internal balance of power.

## Zhang Xueliang
- **Aims:** Resist Japan more seriously and force a united anti-Japanese line.
- **Fears:** Continued Chinese weakness under Chiang's anti-CCP priority.
- **Constraints:** Limited room against Chiang and Japanese pressure.
- **Actions:** Helped trigger the Xi'an Incident.
- **Key events linked:** Xi'an Incident, anti-Japanese united front.
- **How position changed over time:** Moved from regional military actor to decisive but personally costly interventionist in national politics.
- **Why this actor matters historically:** Demonstrates that shifts in Chinese strategy did not come only from Chiang, Mao, or Japan.
