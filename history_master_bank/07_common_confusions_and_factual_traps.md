# 07 Common Confusions and Factual Traps

Source basis: uploaded markdown extraction of Cambridge International AS Level History: International History, 1870-1945. Page references are only included where the extraction made them reasonably clear. Any OCR damage, internal inconsistency, or weakly supported detail is flagged rather than normalised.


| Wrong version | Correct version | Memory fix / distinction rule |
|---|---|---|
| Algeciras and Agadir are the same crisis. | Algeciras belongs to the first Moroccan crisis (1905-06); Agadir is the second crisis (1911). | "A before G": Algeciras first, Agadir later. |
| Entente Cordiale was a full military alliance from 1904. | It began as a settlement of disputes and diplomatic understanding, not a simple formal alliance. | Think "cordiale" = better relations first, not full military merger. |
| Empire after 1870 was purely about greed. | The book stresses mixed motives: economics, strategy, nationalism, prestige, racism, missionary language. | Use "many motives, one direction." |
| Fashoda proved colonial rivalry always caused war. | Fashoda ended in compromise. | If Fashoda had led to war, the Entente would have been much harder later. |
| Triple Intervention was part of the war with Russia. | It came after Shimonoseki and helped cause later Japanese anger toward Russia. | Treaty first, humiliation second, revenge later. |
| Treaty of Paris means 1919. | In this course, Treaty of Paris (1898) ends the Spanish-American War; Paris Peace Conference (1919) creates the post-WWI settlement. | Add the year every time you say "Paris." |
| The Boer War was easy for Britain. | It was costly, prolonged, and damaging to British prestige. | If camps and guerrilla war appear, it was not easy. |
| Versailles was the only 1919-20 treaty that mattered. | There were five major treaties and multiple dissatisfied states. | Germany is central, but not alone. |
| Article 231 alone "caused Hitler." | It mattered because it underpinned reparations and humiliation, but later politics also mattered. | Separate grievance from later political exploitation. |
| US isolationism meant the USA stopped mattering after 1919. | The USA still mattered through loans, debts, Washington diplomacy, Dawes, and Young. | Non-membership in the League did not mean non-importance. |
| The League was either a total success or total failure. | It had some real success in minor disputes but failed badly where major powers had vital interests. | Ask: small dispute or great-power test? |
| Rapallo and the 1926 Berlin treaty are the same. | Rapallo is 1922; Treaty of Berlin is 1926. Both involve Germany and the USSR. | Same pair, different year, different context. |
| Kellogg-Briand outlawed war effectively. | It renounced war in principle but had no strong enforcement. | A promise without teeth is still only a promise. |
| Locarno solved all frontier issues. | It stabilised the west more than the east. | West secure-ish, east still vulnerable. |
| Mussolini was always Hitler's partner. | He began as anti-German on Austria but moved toward Hitler after Abyssinia and League failure. | Italy shifts across the decade. |
| Manchuria was just an Asian sideshow. | It was the first decisive League failure of the 1930s. | If collective security failed first there, it was not peripheral. |
| Hitler's first big success was Munich. | Earlier steps mattered: League exit, rearmament, Saar, naval deal, Rhineland, Anschluss. | Build the ladder, do not jump to 1938. |
| Stresa and Locarno are interchangeable. | Locarno = 1925 settlement framework; Stresa = 1935 anti-German front. | L before S; 1925 before 1935. |
| Anglo-German Naval Agreement strengthened anti-German unity. | It weakened Stresa and alarmed France. | Bilateral deal, multilateral damage. |
| Abyssinia was Hitler's invasion. | Abyssinia was invaded by Mussolini's Italy. | Italy -> East Africa; Germany -> central/eastern Europe. |
| Appeasement just means cowardice. | In the book it is tied to fear of war, military weakness, economic strain, and strategic calculation. | Define the policy before judging it. |
| Munich was only about ethnic Germans. | It also destroyed Czech strategic security and exposed Soviet exclusion. | Territory transfer had strategic effects. |
| March 1939 was just more self-determination. | Occupation of the rest of Czechoslovakia showed wider German aims. | No large German minority argument can explain Prague. |
| Nazi-Soviet Pact means ideology stopped mattering. | It means strategy overrode ideology temporarily. | Tactical pact, not ideological fusion. |
| 1912 solved China's state problem. | 1912 ended the dynasty but not fragmentation or military politics. | Republic on paper, weakness in practice. |
| Northern Expedition fully unified China. | It created nominal unity under Nanking, but warlord and regional power remained. | "Nominally unified" is the safer phrase. |
| Chiang mainly focused on Japan before 1937. | He prioritised the CCP for much of the period. | Ask what Xi'an was reacting against. |
| CCP growth to 1945 came only from beating Japan militarily. | The chapter also stresses KMT weakness, rural support, propaganda, and positioning. | Political growth, not just battlefield victory. |
| Xi'an Incident and Marco Polo Bridge are the same. | Xi'an is 1936 internal Chinese crisis; Marco Polo Bridge / full war is 1937. | Xi'an before total war. |
| Pearl Harbor destroyed total US Pacific capacity. | US carriers were absent and key fuel storage survived. | Surprise blow, not total knockout. |
| The source text is always internally perfect. | It contains OCR slips and a few internal inconsistencies that need checking. | Use the uncertainty file when a detail looks wrong. |
