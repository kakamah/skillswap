# 07 — Common Confusions and Factual Traps

## Format
**WRONG** → **CORRECT** → **Memory Fix**

---

### 1. Treaty of Versailles Severity

- **WRONG**: Treaty of Versailles was extremely harsh / a "Carthaginian peace."
- **CORRECT**: It was a compromise. Clemenceau wanted the Rhineland detached permanently and higher reparations. Foch said "this is not peace; it is an armistice for twenty years." Germany remained Europe's potentially strongest economy; its industrial base was intact; population larger than France's. Keynes's famous critique exaggerated harshness.
- **FIX**: *"Versailles was harsh enough to resent but not harsh enough to prevent recovery."*

---

### 2. Dawes Plan vs Young Plan

- **WRONG**: Dawes Plan reduced reparations.
- **CORRECT**: Dawes Plan (1924) restructured annual payments according to Germany's capacity to pay; it did NOT reduce the total. The Young Plan (1929) reduced the total from £6.6 billion to approximately £2 billion and set a final deadline (1988).
- **FIX**: *"Dawes = restructure; Young = reduce."*

---

### 3. Locarno Borders

- **WRONG**: Locarno guaranteed all of Germany's borders.
- **CORRECT**: Locarno (1925) guaranteed only western borders (France and Belgium). Eastern borders with Poland and Czechoslovakia had arbitration treaties only—NOT guarantees. This was a deliberate omission by Stresemann, who intended to revise eastern borders peacefully.
- **FIX**: *"Locarno faced west, not east."*

---

### 4. League of Nations Track Record

- **WRONG**: The League of Nations always failed.
- **CORRECT**: The League had genuine successes in the 1920s: Teschen dispute (1920), Åland Islands (1921), Upper Silesia plebiscite (1921), Greece-Bulgaria crisis (1925), Mosul dispute (1926). It also ran effective humanitarian agencies (refugees, health, labour). It failed when major powers' vital interests were at stake (Manchuria, Abyssinia).
- **FIX**: *"League worked on small fish, not big sharks."*

---

### 5. Treaty of Sèvres vs Lausanne

- **WRONG**: Treaty of Sèvres ended Turkey's involvement / was the final settlement.
- **CORRECT**: Sèvres (1920) was imposed on the Ottoman Sultan but never properly ratified or implemented. Mustafa Kemal's nationalist movement rejected it and fought the Turkish War of Independence (1919-23). Sèvres was replaced entirely by the Treaty of Lausanne (1923), which was far more favourable to Turkey—the only defeated power to successfully renegotiate its peace treaty.
- **FIX**: *"Sèvres = draft; Lausanne = final."*

---

### 6. Appeasement Motivation

- **WRONG**: Appeasement was simply cowardice or naivety.
- **CORRECT**: Appeasement had multiple rational justifications: (1) overwhelming anti-war public opinion (Peace Ballot; "King and Country" debate); (2) genuine military unpreparedness (Ten Year Rule had gutted defence spending); (3) Depression economics made rearmament unaffordable; (4) fear of communism—Hitler seen as buffer against Soviet expansion; (5) widespread belief that Versailles was genuinely unjust and German grievances were legitimate.
- **FIX**: *"Appeasement was logical but wrong."*

---

### 7. Munich Reception

- **WRONG**: Munich Agreement was universally condemned at the time.
- **CORRECT**: Chamberlain was initially celebrated. 369 MPs voted for Munich vs 150 against. Crowds cheered his return. He was nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize. Only a minority (Churchill, Duff Cooper, Eden) opposed it publicly. Condemnation came later—especially after Prague (March 1939).
- **FIX**: *"Munich was popular—at first."*

---

### 8. Sun Yat-sen vs Chiang Kai-shek

- **WRONG**: Sun Yat-sen and Chiang Kai-shek had the same political aims.
- **CORRECT**: Sun's Three Principles of the People included nationalism, democracy, and people's livelihood (land reform/social welfare). Chiang abandoned democracy and social reform; became increasingly authoritarian/militarist; allied with landlords, businessmen, and gangsters (Green Gang); ran a one-party state modelled partly on fascist lines.
- **FIX**: *"Sun dreamed democracy; Chiang delivered dictatorship."*

---

### 9. CCP Popularity

- **WRONG**: The CCP was always popular in China.
- **CORRECT**: CCP had only ~57,000 members in 1927; nearly destroyed in Shanghai Massacre. Growth came from: (1) Mao's land reform in base areas; (2) anti-Japanese resistance giving patriotic legitimacy; (3) KMT failures—corruption, conscription brutality, hyperinflation. The CCP grew because the KMT failed, not because communism was inherently popular with Chinese peasants.
- **FIX**: *"CCP grew because KMT failed, not because communism was popular."*

---

### 10. Manchuria Invasion Authority

- **WRONG**: Japan's invasion of Manchuria (1931) was government policy.
- **CORRECT**: The Kwantung Army staged the Mukden Incident and invaded without cabinet authorisation. The civilian government was presented with a fait accompli. PM Inukai, who criticised the army's actions, was assassinated in May 1932. The army demonstrated it could act independently and punish politicians who opposed it.
- **FIX**: *"The army invaded; the government fell."*

---

### 11. Stresa Front Strength

- **WRONG**: The Stresa Front (1935) was a strong, united alliance against Hitler.
- **CORRECT**: The Stresa Front (Britain, France, Italy) was vague in wording; did not even mention Germany by name in its communiqué; and collapsed within months when Britain signed the Anglo-German Naval Agreement (June 1935) without consulting France or Italy—directly contradicting Stresa's spirit.
- **FIX**: *"Stresa = paper, not steel."*

---

### 12. Triple Alliance vs Triple Entente

- **WRONG**: Triple Alliance and Triple Entente are interchangeable terms / same thing.
- **CORRECT**: Triple Alliance (1882) = Germany + Austria-Hungary + Italy. Triple Entente (1907) = Britain + France + Russia. Italy later switched sides (1915). The Alliance was a formal military pact; the Entente was a looser set of understandings (not a binding alliance until wartime).
- **FIX**: *"Alliance had Germany; Entente had Britain."*

---

### 13. Berlin Conference and Africa

- **WRONG**: The Berlin Conference (1884-85) divided Africa among European powers.
- **CORRECT**: Berlin Conference set rules for future claims (effective occupation, notification) but did NOT draw the map of Africa. The scramble was already underway before the conference and continued after it. The conference regulated the process; it did not initiate or complete it.
- **FIX**: *"Berlin set rules; didn't draw maps."*

---

### 14. US Entry into WWI Trigger

- **WRONG**: The USA entered WWI because of the sinking of the Lusitania.
- **CORRECT**: Lusitania was sunk in May 1915; USA did not enter the war until April 1917—nearly two years later. Wilson ran for re-election in 1916 on "He kept us out of war." The immediate triggers in 1917 were: (1) Germany's resumption of unrestricted submarine warfare (February 1917); (2) Zimmerman Telegram (offering Mexico an alliance against USA, intercepted January 1917). Lusitania created outrage but not war.
- **FIX**: *"Lusitania = anger; Zimmerman = war."*

---

### 15. Pearl Harbor Damage

- **WRONG**: Pearl Harbor destroyed the US Pacific Fleet.
- **CORRECT**: Significant damage but not fatal. All 3 aircraft carriers were at sea and escaped. Of 8 battleships hit, 6 were eventually repaired and returned to service (shallow harbour made salvage possible). Crucially, oil storage facilities and submarine base survived. Japan failed to launch a third strike wave. The attack galvanised American resolve far more than it damaged American capability.
- **FIX**: *"Pearl Harbor hurt but didn't kill."*

---

### 16. Axis/Anti-Comintern/Pact of Steel

- **WRONG**: Rome-Berlin Axis, Anti-Comintern Pact, and Pact of Steel are the same agreement.
- **CORRECT**: Three separate agreements:
  - **Rome-Berlin Axis** (October 1936): Italy-Germany political alignment; Mussolini coined "axis" term.
  - **Anti-Comintern Pact** (November 1936): Germany-Japan agreement against Communist International; Italy joined 1937.
  - **Pact of Steel** (May 1939): Italy-Germany full military alliance with offensive obligations.
- **FIX**: *"Axis = political; Comintern = ideological; Steel = military."*

---

### 17. Long March as Victory

- **WRONG**: The Long March was a CCP victory.
- **CORRECT**: It was a catastrophic military defeat. The CCP began with ~86,000; estimates of survivors range from 4,000 to 8,000 (~90%+ casualties from combat, starvation, desertion, exposure). However, Mao transformed it into a propaganda triumph—a founding myth of heroic endurance that legitimised CCP rule for decades.
- **FIX**: *"Long March = defeat turned legend."*

---

### 18. First vs Second Sino-Japanese War

- **WRONG**: The First Sino-Japanese War and Second Sino-Japanese War had the same cause.
- **CORRECT**:
  - **First Sino-Japanese War (1894-95)**: Fought over control of Korea; Japan won easily; Treaty of Shimonoseki gave Japan Taiwan, Pescadores, Liaodong Peninsula (later returned under Triple Intervention).
  - **Second Sino-Japanese War (1937-45)**: Full-scale invasion of China proper; triggered by Marco Polo Bridge Incident (July 1937); resulted in occupation of eastern China, Nanjing Massacre, and prolonged guerrilla resistance.
- **FIX**: *"First = Korea dispute; Second = all-out war."*

---

### 19. First United Front vs Second United Front

- **WRONG**: The First United Front and Second United Front were the same arrangement.
- **CORRECT**:
  - **First United Front (1923-27)**: KMT-CCP cooperation to unify China; Soviet-advised; ended when Chiang purged CCP in Shanghai Massacre (April 1927).
  - **Second United Front (1936-45)**: KMT-CCP cooperation against Japan; triggered by Xi'an Incident; cooperation was nominal—both sides prepared for post-war civil war.
- **FIX**: *"First Front = unite China; Second Front = fight Japan."*

---

### 20. Hoare-Laval Pact Implementation

- **WRONG**: The Hoare-Laval Pact was implemented.
- **CORRECT**: The secret plan (December 1935) to give Mussolini two-thirds of Abyssinia was leaked to the press before it could be implemented. Public outrage forced both British Foreign Secretary Hoare and French PM Laval to resign. The pact was repudiated by the British government. But the damage was done: it exposed the hypocrisy of League sanctions policy and convinced Mussolini that Britain and France were not serious opponents.
- **FIX**: *"Hoare-Laval was dead on exposure."*
