# Master 25 Treaties and Settlements

Revision-first treaty core. Each entry gives the essential terms, why it matters, and the exact confusion pair students mix up.

## 1. Treaty of Berlin
- **Date:** 1885
- **Parties:** European powers at the Berlin Conference
- **Core terms:** Notification of occupation; effective occupation; negotiated disputes; free navigation in central African river regions.
- **Why it matters:** Structured and accelerated the scramble for Africa.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Treaty of Berlin **1885** vs Treaty of Berlin **1926**

## 2. Treaty of Shimonoseki
- **Date:** 1895
- **Parties:** Japan, China
- **Core terms:** Chinese defeat recognised; Korea's status changed; Japanese territorial gains confirmed.
- **Why it matters:** Marked Japan's arrival as a major East Asian power.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Shimonoseki vs Triple Intervention

## 3. Treaty of Vereeniging
- **Date:** 1902
- **Parties:** Britain, Boer republics
- **Core terms:** Ended the Boer War under British supremacy after prolonged fighting and a guerrilla phase.
- **Why it matters:** Closed the war militarily but left Britain with heavy political and reputational costs.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Vereeniging vs Treaty of Paris 1898

## 4. Treaty of Paris
- **Date:** 1898
- **Parties:** USA, Spain
- **Core terms:** Ended Spanish-American War; Spain ceded major overseas possessions to the USA.
- **Why it matters:** Confirmed the USA as an imperial and naval power.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Treaty of Paris **1898** vs Paris Peace Conference **1919**

## 5. Platt Amendment / Cuban treaty framework
- **Date:** 1901 / 1903
- **Parties:** USA, Cuba
- **Core terms:** USA gained wide influence over Cuban affairs and base rights including Guantanamo.
- **Why it matters:** Shows US control without full annexation.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Cuban nominal independence vs real sovereignty

## 6. Anglo-Japanese Alliance
- **Date:** 1902
- **Parties:** Britain, Japan
- **Core terms:** Mutual support if attacked by multiple powers; recognition of Japanese interest in Korea.
- **Why it matters:** Elevated Japan diplomatically and linked European and Asian power politics.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Anglo-Japanese Alliance vs Entente Cordiale

## 7. Entente Cordiale
- **Date:** 1904
- **Parties:** Britain, France
- **Core terms:** Settlement of colonial disputes and improved diplomatic relations.
- **Why it matters:** Reduced Anglo-French friction and set the background for later anti-German alignment.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Entente Cordiale vs full military alliance

## 8. Treaty of Portsmouth
- **Date:** 1905
- **Parties:** Japan, Russia, mediated by the USA
- **Core terms:** Ended the Russo-Japanese War; recognised Japanese gains and Russian retreat.
- **Why it matters:** Confirmed Japan's status and showed US diplomatic importance.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Portsmouth vs Washington settlement

## 9. Treaty of Versailles
- **Date:** 1919
- **Parties:** Germany, Allied powers
- **Core terms:** Territorial losses, military restrictions, war guilt clause, reparations framework, League Covenant.
- **Why it matters:** Central treaty of the interwar order and German revisionist grievance.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Versailles vs whole Paris peace system

## 10. Treaty of Saint-Germain
- **Date:** 1919
- **Parties:** Austria, Allied powers
- **Core terms:** Austria lost Bohemia and Moravia to Czechoslovakia, territory to Yugoslavia, Romania, Poland, and Italy.
- **Why it matters:** Reduced Austria to a weak state and confirmed Habsburg collapse.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Saint-Germain vs Trianon

## 11. Treaty of Neuilly
- **Date:** 1919
- **Parties:** Bulgaria, Allied powers
- **Core terms:** Bulgaria lost territory to Greece, Yugoslavia, and Romania; army cut; reparations imposed.
- **Why it matters:** Shows post-war dissatisfaction was not limited to Germany.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Neuilly vs Trianon vs Saint-Germain

## 12. Treaty of Trianon
- **Date:** 1920
- **Parties:** Hungary, Allied powers
- **Core terms:** Hungary lost Slovakia and Ruthenia to Czechoslovakia, Transylvania to Romania, and Croatia/Slovenia to Yugoslavia.
- **Why it matters:** Created major Hungarian revisionist bitterness.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Trianon vs Saint-Germain

## 13. Treaty of Sevres
- **Date:** 1920
- **Parties:** Ottoman Empire, Allied powers
- **Core terms:** Severe territorial losses, mandate arrangements, and permanent opening of the Dardanelles.
- **Why it matters:** Provoked Turkish nationalist resistance.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Sevres vs Lausanne

## 14. Treaty of Munich / Munich Agreement
- **Date:** 1938
- **Parties:** Germany, Britain, France, Italy
- **Core terms:** Sudetenland transferred to Germany without Czech participation.
- **Why it matters:** Peak appeasement; Czechoslovakia lost major defences and strategic depth.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Munich 1938 vs occupation of the rest of Czechoslovakia 1939

## 15. Washington settlement
- **Date:** 1921-22
- **Parties:** USA, Britain, Japan, France, Italy, China, others
- **Core terms:** Five-Power naval ratios; Four-Power Pacific agreement; Nine-Power support for China/Open Door.
- **Why it matters:** Temporarily stabilised Pacific tensions.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Washington settlement vs one single Washington treaty

## 16. Treaty of Rapallo
- **Date:** 1922
- **Parties:** Germany, USSR
- **Core terms:** Diplomatic and economic normalisation; renunciation of claims; associated with later secret military cooperation.
- **Why it matters:** Let excluded powers work outside the Versailles system.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Rapallo vs Treaty of Berlin 1926

## 17. Treaty of Lausanne
- **Date:** 1923
- **Parties:** Turkey, Allied powers
- **Core terms:** Recognised the Republic of Turkey and replaced Sevres with a more favourable settlement.
- **Why it matters:** Proved the post-war treaties could be revised.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Lausanne vs Locarno

## 18. Dawes Plan
- **Date:** 1924
- **Parties:** Germany, USA, France, Britain, reparations powers
- **Core terms:** Reparations rescheduled to German capacity; Ruhr evacuation; US loans.
- **Why it matters:** Mid-1920s stability depended on US finance.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Dawes Plan vs Young Plan

## 19. Locarno Treaties
- **Date:** 1925
- **Parties:** Germany, France, Belgium, Britain, Italy
- **Core terms:** Western frontiers guaranteed; arbitration widened; Britain and Italy guaranteed the western settlement.
- **Why it matters:** High point of western détente, but eastern frontiers remained weaker.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Locarno vs Stresa Front

## 20. Treaty of Berlin
- **Date:** 1926
- **Parties:** Germany, USSR
- **Core terms:** Renewed Rapallo-style cooperation and neutrality logic while Germany also operated inside the League.
- **Why it matters:** Shows Stresemann's diplomacy was cooperative and revisionist at the same time.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Treaty of Berlin **1926** vs Treaty of Berlin **1885**

## 21. Kellogg-Briand Pact
- **Date:** 1928 / 1929 in force
- **Parties:** USA, France, many other states
- **Core terms:** War renounced as an instrument of national policy; no real enforcement mechanism.
- **Why it matters:** Symbol of idealist diplomacy without coercive force.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Renouncing war in principle vs preventing war in practice

## 22. Young Plan
- **Date:** 1929
- **Parties:** Germany, USA, France, Britain, reparations powers
- **Core terms:** Lower reparations total and longer payment schedule; some controls eased.
- **Why it matters:** Last major reparations adjustment before the Depression shattered stability.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Young Plan vs Dawes Plan

## 23. Tanggu Truce
- **Date:** 1933
- **Parties:** China, Japan
- **Core terms:** Left Japan dominant in Manchuria and stronger in north China.
- **Why it matters:** Confirmed that Manchuria had become a Japanese strategic gain, not a temporary crisis.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Tanggu Truce vs League response to Manchuria

## 24. Stresa Front
- **Date:** 1935
- **Parties:** Britain, France, Italy
- **Core terms:** Common opposition to treaty revision by force and support for Austrian independence; no strong enforcement.
- **Why it matters:** Its collapse made later German advances easier.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Stresa vs Locarno

## 25. Nazi-Soviet Pact
- **Date:** 1939
- **Parties:** Germany, USSR
- **Core terms:** Public non-aggression plus secret spheres of influence in eastern Europe.
- **Why it matters:** Removed Hitler's immediate two-front problem and isolated Poland.
- **Confusion pair(s):** Nazi-Soviet Pact vs Anti-Comintern Pact
