# Master 50 Events

Revision-first event core. Each event gives date, what happened, why it matters, and the exact event it is commonly confused with.

## 1870-1914

1. **Franco-Prussian War** — **1870-71**  
   - **What:** Prussia defeated France and made German unification possible.  
   - **Why it matters:** Shifted the European balance and left France with a lasting security grievance.  
   - **Confused with:** First World War outbreak (1914).

2. **German unification** — **1871**  
   - **What:** Germany emerged as a new industrial and military great power.  
   - **Why it matters:** Reshaped alliance politics and later rivalry in Europe.  
   - **Confused with:** Anschluss (1938).

3. **British occupation of Egypt** — **1882**  
   - **What:** Britain took control of Egyptian administration to secure the Suez route.  
   - **Why it matters:** Linked strategic route defence directly to imperial expansion.  
   - **Confused with:** Berlin Conference (1884-85).

4. **Berlin Conference / Treaty of Berlin** — **1884-85**  
   - **What:** European powers agreed rules for African partition, including effective occupation.  
   - **Why it matters:** Structured and accelerated the scramble for Africa.  
   - **Confused with:** Treaty of Berlin (1926).

5. **First Sino-Japanese War** — **1894-95**  
   - **What:** Japan defeated China and exposed Qing weakness.  
   - **Why it matters:** Marked Japan's rise as a regional great power.  
   - **Confused with:** Russo-Japanese War (1904-05).

6. **Treaty of Shimonoseki** — **1895**  
   - **What:** China accepted Japanese gains after defeat in war.  
   - **Why it matters:** Confirmed Japan's new regional weight.  
   - **Confused with:** Triple Intervention (1895).

7. **Triple Intervention** — **1895**  
   - **What:** Russia, France, and Germany forced Japan to surrender part of its Shimonoseki gains.  
   - **Why it matters:** Humiliated Japan and fed later anti-Russian strategy.  
   - **Confused with:** Russo-Japanese War (1904-05).

8. **Fashoda Incident** — **1898**  
   - **What:** British and French imperial expansion collided in Sudan, but both sides stepped back.  
   - **Why it matters:** Showed colonial rivalry could be contained by compromise.  
   - **Confused with:** Moroccan crises (1905-06 / 1911).

9. **Spanish-American War** — **1898**  
   - **What:** The USA defeated Spain and took overseas possessions.  
   - **Why it matters:** Marked the USA's emergence as an imperial and naval power.  
   - **Confused with:** Treaty of Paris (1898).

10. **Open Door policy** — **1899**  
    - **What:** The USA pushed equal commercial access in China rather than exclusive partition.  
    - **Why it matters:** Showed the USA was shaping Asian diplomacy.  
    - **Confused with:** Washington Conference (1921-22).

11. **Second Boer War** — **1899-1902**  
    - **What:** Britain fought a long, costly war against the Boer republics.  
    - **Why it matters:** Damaged British prestige and exposed imperial overstretch.  
    - **Confused with:** easy colonial victory.

12. **Anglo-Japanese Alliance** — **1902**  
    - **What:** Britain and Japan formed a security agreement in East Asia.  
    - **Why it matters:** Elevated Japan diplomatically and linked European and Asian strategy.  
    - **Confused with:** Entente Cordiale (1904).

13. **Entente Cordiale** — **1904**  
    - **What:** Britain and France settled colonial disputes and improved relations.  
    - **Why it matters:** Helped realign Europe against a more assertive Germany.  
    - **Confused with:** Triple Entente as if identical from 1904.

14. **Russo-Japanese War** — **1904-05**  
    - **What:** Japan defeated Russia on land and sea.  
    - **Why it matters:** First major modern defeat of a European great power by an Asian state.  
    - **Confused with:** First Sino-Japanese War (1894-95).

15. **Treaty of Portsmouth** — **1905**  
    - **What:** US-mediated peace ended the Russo-Japanese War.  
    - **Why it matters:** Confirmed Japan's status and showed growing US diplomatic influence.  
    - **Confused with:** Washington settlement (1921-22).

16. **First Moroccan Crisis / Algeciras** — **1905-06**  
    - **What:** Germany challenged French influence in Morocco but failed to break the Entente.  
    - **Why it matters:** Strengthened Anglo-French cooperation before 1914.  
    - **Confused with:** Agadir / Second Moroccan Crisis (1911).

17. **Second Moroccan Crisis / Agadir** — **1911**  
    - **What:** Germany sent the Panther to Agadir to pressure France again.  
    - **Why it matters:** Deepened British and French suspicion of Germany.  
    - **Confused with:** Algeciras (1905-06).

18. **Chinese Revolution** — **1911**  
    - **What:** Qing rule collapsed through revolution.  
    - **Why it matters:** Ended the dynasty but not China's instability.  
    - **Confused with:** May Fourth Movement (1919).

19. **Republic of China established** — **Jan-Mar 1912**  
    - **What:** Sun proclaimed the republic; Yuan became president after abdication.  
    - **Why it matters:** Created a republic in form, not a stable unified state in practice.  
    - **Confused with:** end of warlordism.

20. **Sarajevo and outbreak of First World War** — **1914**  
    - **What:** The July Crisis turned a Balkan assassination into general war.  
    - **Why it matters:** Destroyed the old order and set up the peace settlement crisis.  
    - **Confused with:** Franco-Prussian War (1870-71).

## 1915-1929

21. **Twenty-One Demands** — **1915**  
    - **What:** Japan pressured China for extensive concessions.  
    - **Why it matters:** Intensified Chinese nationalism and anti-Japanese resentment.  
    - **Confused with:** Washington Conference (1921-22).

22. **Death of Yuan Shih-kai / intensification of warlord era** — **1916**  
    - **What:** Yuan's collapse removed the strongest central figure in republican China.  
    - **Why it matters:** Deepened fragmentation and military regionalism.  
    - **Confused with:** 1912 Republic.

23. **USA enters First World War** — **1917**  
    - **What:** The USA joined the war against Germany.  
    - **Why it matters:** Shifted resources and made Wilson central to peace-making.  
    - **Confused with:** US isolationism after 1919.

24. **Wilson's Fourteen Points** — **Jan 1918**  
    - **What:** Wilson proposed open diplomacy, self-determination, and a League.  
    - **Why it matters:** Became the benchmark used to judge the later peace settlement.  
    - **Confused with:** Treaty of Versailles (1919).

25. **German collapse and armistice** — **Nov 1918**  
    - **What:** Military defeat and revolution forced Germany to stop fighting.  
    - **Why it matters:** Opened the way for the Paris Peace Conference.  
    - **Confused with:** Treaty signing in 1919.

26. **Paris Peace Conference** — **1919**  
    - **What:** Victorious powers designed the post-war settlement and League system.  
    - **Why it matters:** Created the legal and political structure of the interwar world.  
    - **Confused with:** Berlin Conference (1884-85).

27. **Rejection of Japan's racial equality proposal** — **1919**  
    - **What:** Japan's proposed League Covenant clause on racial equality failed at Paris.  
    - **Why it matters:** Deepened Japanese resentment toward western powers.  
    - **Confused with:** Shantung dispute at Paris.

28. **May Fourth Movement** — **1919**  
    - **What:** Protest against the Shantung settlement widened into a nationalist and intellectual movement.  
    - **Why it matters:** Major turning point in modern Chinese political thought.  
    - **Confused with:** 1911 Revolution.

29. **Treaty of Versailles** — **1919**  
    - **What:** Germany lost territory, accepted military limits, and faced reparations under Article 231.  
    - **Why it matters:** Central source of Weimar grievance and later Nazi propaganda.  
    - **Confused with:** the whole Paris system of five treaties.

30. **League of Nations begins operating** — **from 1920**  
    - **What:** Geneva institutions started functioning as machinery of collective security.  
    - **Why it matters:** Essential framework for interwar diplomacy and its failures.  
    - **Confused with:** League always succeeding or always failing.

31. **Treaty of Sevres** — **1920**  
    - **What:** A harsh settlement was imposed on the Ottoman Empire.  
    - **Why it matters:** Became the clearest example of a treaty later overturned.  
    - **Confused with:** Treaty of Lausanne (1923).

32. **CCP founded** — **1921**  
    - **What:** The Chinese Communist Party emerged as a small but organised movement.  
    - **Why it matters:** Opened the KMT-CCP struggle that shaped China to 1945.  
    - **Confused with:** communist victory in 1949.

33. **Washington Conference / settlement** — **1921-22**  
    - **What:** Naval and Pacific agreements attempted to calm tensions in East Asia.  
    - **Why it matters:** Temporary stabilisation of Pacific relations.  
    - **Confused with:** Anglo-Japanese Alliance (1902).

34. **Treaty of Rapallo** — **1922**  
    - **What:** Germany and Soviet Russia rebuilt relations outside the Paris framework.  
    - **Why it matters:** Showed excluded powers could bypass the Versailles system.  
    - **Confused with:** Treaty of Berlin (1926).

35. **Mussolini becomes prime minister** — **1922**  
    - **What:** Fascism took power in Italy.  
    - **Why it matters:** Extremism moved from fringe to government in a major state.  
    - **Confused with:** Hitler becomes chancellor (1933).

36. **Ruhr occupation** — **1923**  
    - **What:** France and Belgium occupied the Ruhr after German default on reparations deliveries.  
    - **Why it matters:** Showed security policy and economic policy were inseparable.  
    - **Confused with:** Rhineland remilitarisation (1936).

37. **German hyperinflation peaks** — **1923**  
    - **What:** The mark collapsed amid passive resistance and state financing.  
    - **Why it matters:** Made the old reparations framework unsustainable.  
    - **Confused with:** Wall Street Crash / Great Depression (1929).

38. **Corfu Crisis** — **1923**  
    - **What:** Mussolini used force against Greece and defied League authority.  
    - **Why it matters:** Early proof that the League struggled against determined major powers.  
    - **Confused with:** Abyssinian Crisis (1935-36).

39. **Treaty of Lausanne** — **1923**  
    - **What:** A revised settlement recognised the Republic of Turkey.  
    - **Why it matters:** Proved that the post-war settlements were not all permanent.  
    - **Confused with:** Locarno (1925).

40. **Dawes Plan** — **1924**  
    - **What:** Reparations were rescheduled; the Ruhr was evacuated; US money flowed into Germany.  
    - **Why it matters:** Mid-1920s recovery depended on US finance.  
    - **Confused with:** Young Plan (1929).

41. **Japanese Exclusion Act** — **1924**  
    - **What:** The USA effectively ended further Japanese immigration.  
    - **Why it matters:** Strengthened anti-western opinion in Japan.  
    - **Confused with:** Washington naval restrictions.

42. **Locarno Treaties** — **1925**  
    - **What:** Germany accepted western frontiers in a broader diplomatic package.  
    - **Why it matters:** High point of western détente, but eastern insecurity remained.  
    - **Confused with:** Stresa Front (1935).

43. **Northern Expedition begins** — **1926**  
    - **What:** Chiang led a military advance against warlords.  
    - **Why it matters:** Began the process of nominal reunification under Nanking.  
    - **Confused with:** Long March (1934-35).

44. **Shanghai Purge / White Terror** — **1927**  
    - **What:** Chiang violently crushed communists and radical labour in Shanghai and beyond.  
    - **Why it matters:** Opened the long KMT-CCP civil conflict.  
    - **Confused with:** Nanjing Massacre (1937).

45. **Kellogg-Briand Pact** — **1928**  
    - **What:** States renounced war in principle as an instrument of policy.  
    - **Why it matters:** Showed idealism without strong enforcement.  
    - **Confused with:** collective security that could actually stop aggression.

46. **Wall Street Crash** — **Oct 1929**  
    - **What:** The US financial crash spread internationally and broke the foundations of 1920s stability.  
    - **Why it matters:** Radicalised politics and weakened defenders of peace.  
    - **Confused with:** Hyperinflation crisis (1923).

## 1930-1945

47. **Manchurian Crisis / Mukden Incident** — **1931**  
    - **What:** Japan seized Manchuria after a staged railway incident and created Manchukuo.  
    - **Why it matters:** First decisive League failure of the 1930s; aggression succeeded.  
    - **Confused with:** Full Sino-Japanese War (1937).

48. **Rhineland remilitarisation** — **1936**  
    - **What:** German troops re-entered the demilitarised Rhineland without armed opposition.  
    - **Why it matters:** Hitler learned the West would not fight over treaty enforcement.  
    - **Confused with:** Ruhr occupation (1923).

49. **Munich Agreement** — **1938**  
    - **What:** Britain and France accepted the transfer of the Sudetenland to Germany without Czech participation.  
    - **Why it matters:** Peak appeasement: short-term peace at long-term strategic cost.  
    - **Confused with:** Occupation of the rest of Czechoslovakia (March 1939).

50. **Pearl Harbor** — **7 Dec 1941**  
    - **What:** Japan attacked the US Pacific Fleet base in Hawaii.  
    - **Why it matters:** Brought the USA into the war and fused regional conflicts into global war.  
    - **Confused with:** Invasion of Poland (1939) as the start of global war.
